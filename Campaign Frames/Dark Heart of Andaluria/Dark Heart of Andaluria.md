# DARK HEART OF ANDALURIA

*As the nights grow long in a land of rich wine and ancient superstitions, a band of monster hunters battles the creatures that stalk the shadows.*

Designed by Carlos Cisco, Rue Dickey, Rowan Hall, and Rob Hebert

**Complexity Rating: •••**

## THE PITCH

*Read this section to your players to introduce them to the campaign.*

Andaluria is a land of sweeping vineyards where beauty and dread intertwine. By day, the streets and valleys are alive with festivals, music, and the scent of wine—but when the sun sets, monsters come out to feast. For generations, the old superstitions and folk rites kept the beasts at bay, but of late the nights have grown unnaturally long, and the citizens of Andaluria live in fear of increasingly terrifying monsters.

In a Dark Heart of Andaluria campaign, you'll play monster hunters who must investigate horrific crimes, navigate political strife, and slay supernatural creatures to protect the people from eternal darkness.

## OVERVIEW

*If your group decides to play this campaign, give your players the following information before character creation.*

A land of sirocco winds, volcanic soil, and simmering intrigue, **Andaluria** is a region forged by rebellion. One hundred years ago, its people rose up against the tyrannical **Emperor Arcedrio** (he/him) and cast off the yoke of imperial control. Today, Andaluria appears to thrive due to its lush vineyards, its lucrative harbor trade, and the canny administration of its **Council of Five**. Yet beneath the surface, ancient rituals, secret bargains, and monstrous hungers still shape the fate of its people.

Andaluria's landscape, like its history, was sculpted by shadow and flame. **The Serracorte**, a jagged chain of dormant volcanoes, dominates the north—their obsidian slopes, ideal for growing grapes, rise into scrubland and thin pasture where hardy shepherds and wild creatures make their homes. These mountains shelter Andaluria from the harsher climates beyond, giving the region its bright, blazing days and dark, sultry nights. The **Valmorria River** flows from the mountain peaks through fertile valleys tangled with grapevines before emptying into a deep southern bay where the capital city of **Jesaña** commands the coast, and locals celebrate the long, hot summers with music, dancing, and drink.

But the valleys and vineyards of Andaluria also bear more sinister fruit: since time immemorial, bloodthirsty beasts— ghosts, ghouls, and creatures that defy description—have emerged from the shadows to feast on the people's fear. For generations, Andalurians defended themselves from these creatures with the twin weapons of faith and folklore: the sacred rites of the **Church of the Golden Eminence** and the practical magic of the nomadic **Caio** people. These rituals and wards—holy symbols in the shape of the sun, charms of ash and bone, and offerings to unseen spirits—were once all the protection people needed. Of late, however, these rites and relics have lost their bite.

For a century, Andaluria has celebrated its independence with festivals of wine and fire, culminating on the summer solstice. But this year, the nights have grown mysteriously longer— and more dangerous—with each sunset. Now, the moon lingers overhead unnaturally, monsters prowl the fields where revelers once danced, and the wealthy elite hide from the horror in masquerades at the hilltop castillo of the mysterious **Condesa Ysabela Delamoña IV** (she/her). The locals whisper that death has come to Andaluria, and they fear that, at this year's **Midsummer Festival**, the streets of Jesaña will run red with more than wine. What was once joyous is now desperate—celebration turned into apprehension, laughter masking dread, and every shadow hiding fangs.

In this climate of fear, the polemical **Archbishop Veyra** (he/ him) has risen to power by preaching a new church orthodoxy, one uncorrupted by "Caio heresy." As the people's faith in the old ways falters, Veyra's fiery sermons find purchase and threaten to inaugurate a new age of zealotry.

But not everyone has responded to the night's encroachment with trepidation and obedience: many brave Andalurians have taken up the mantle of "monster hunter," defending their villages with blade, flame, and forbidden lore when amulets and incantations aren't enough. Across the countryside, clandestine cells of the **Sombral Spade**, a secret order of monster slayers led by the enigmatic **Julio Cantafierro** (he/ him), have arisen to face down the dark. To the people, these hunters are heroes. To the church, they are heretics. To those in power, they are a dangerous necessity.

You are a band of monster hunters, brought together to battle the night. Can you defeat the darkness, or will you become what you wish to destroy?

## COMMUNITIES

*All communities are available, but some have unique aspects in a Dark Heart of Andaluria campaign. As needed, provide the following information to your players and choose one or more of the questions to ask them during your session zero.*

### Freeborne

The uprising that deposed the emperor was a formative event in the history of Andaluria, and many communities define themselves by their contributions to the rebellion. Members of these communities strongly distrust anything connected to the emperor—and eye most nobles with suspicion.

- Your family lore tells of a relative who acted heroically during the rebellion against the emperor. What did they do that fills you with pride, and how has it shaped your idea of what a "hero" really is?
- You've heard rumors that an influential figure has been operating behind the scenes to reestablish the empire and take the crown for themself. Who are they, and from what institution do they draw their power?
- You have a small, private act of rebellion you regularly perform to remind you of your freedom. What is it, and how long have you been doing it?

### Hearthborne

Away from the petty politics of the capital, many Andalurians lead steady, wholesome lives laboring in the fields or farming the upper reaches of the Serracorte. In light of the recent attacks, many hearthborne communities have become more wary of outsiders than they once were.

- While most of your community's important celebrations are marked by joy and exuberance, one ritual is distinctly somber and reserved. What is it, and why is it so meaningful to you?
- Someone in your community disappeared without a trace. What do people say happened to them?
- You carry with you something that reminds you of home and hearth, bringing comfort and banishing fear in time of need. What is it?

### Highborne

In Andaluria, social classes are starkly divided. The highest social sphere of Jesaña is occupied by the ruling council and their families, plus any nobles within their orbit. There are also influential socialites and political elites from wealthy merchant families who trade an assortment of goods, such as the region's famous wine, to foreign lands for gold, silk, and other luxuries. Outside the city, wealth is primarily measured in land, with the richest families owning vineyards on the most productive tracts in the Serracorte foothills.

- What is your community's most prized possession, and what has been sacrificed to keep it?
- How did your family earn its wealth or reputation? Does it fill you with pride or shame?
- You harbor a dark secret that could ruin your family. What is it, and what could convince you to reveal it to outsiders?

### Loreborne

Andaluria is a land where secrets abound and hidden knowledge is a powerful weapon against monsters both magical and mundane. A loreborne community can be centered around chronicling the region's dark past, cataloging the sins of powerful figures, or wielding esoteric lore against the creatures of the night.

- What rumor have you heard about the resurgence of monsters in Andaluria, and how do you plan to test it?
- You have been tasked with finding hidden information about an important figure. Who is your target, and why would you risk everything to learn this knowledge?
- Your community has a specific code they use to test one another before sharing information. What is it, and why is it used?

### Orderborne

Many orderborne characters are adherents—or even clergy members—of the Church of the Golden Eminence, the most strictly hierarchical institution in Andaluria. The church has waned in popularity in recent decades, but the uptick in monster activity is calling many lost members back into the fold—and Archbishop Veyra's harsh ideology. This has caused a rift between those who value tradition and those who champion reform.

- You practice a daily ritual that outsiders view as vapid or nonsensical but you believe is essential. What is it?
- Your faith was once shaken when you witnessed another member of your community break one of its tenets without consequence. Why do you think they got away with it?
- You care very much about someone who doesn't share your beliefs. Who are they, and what punishment are you terrified will befall them?

### Wanderborne

The Caio are the most prominent wanderborne community in Andaluria. Many caravan throughout the region as the seasons change and their herds cycle between grazing patches. Wanderborne characters can also come from outside Andaluria—many are drawn to the area by the promise of seasonal work in the fields or at the city docks.

- What one item do you never begin a journey without?
- You came to Andaluria from a far-off place. Why did you leave, and what drew you here?
- Which waypoint on your annual circuit do you always wish your people could linger at?

## ANCESTRIES

*All ancestries are available, but some have unique aspects in a Dark Heart of Andaluria campaign. As needed, provide the following information to your players.*

### Aetheris & Infernis

In Andaluria, aetheris and infernis can be born to any family regardless of their parents' ancestry. Many church adherents believe the birth of an aetheris is a divine blessing and often recruit or exalt the families into which they are born. On the other hand, some church zealots view infernis as ill omens or punishments for sin and ostracize their birth families as a result.

### Clanks

The first clanks in Andaluria were humble creations—wood and iron machines built to toil in vineyards, sort grapes, make bottles, and craft wine. Today, the noble houses commission exquisite models crafted from porcelain or brass with gemstone inlays, flaunting their wealth in mechanical form. Whether a vineyard relic or a jeweled servant, a clank in Andaluria carries the signs of their maker's intended purpose.

### Elves

Elves of Andaluria embody the land's flora in their mystic form, recalling gnarled grapevines, hardy shrubs, and pale ash trees that cling to volcanic soil. In them, the land seems to find its voice: resilient, beautiful, and a little uncanny.

## CLASSES

*All classes are available, but some have unique aspects in a Dark Heart of Andaluria campaign. As needed, provide the following information to your players.*

### Bards

Bards are beloved in Andaluria as the keepers of songs, tales, and folk wisdom—the knowledge of which can help people protect themselves from terrors of the dark. Their music fills festivals, their poetry preserves the history of rebellion, and their stories remind the people who they are. In Jesaña, wealthy patrons shower bards with riches to keep the revels—and their reputations—alive. In less-wealthy caravans or villages, bards may be offered food, drink, comfortable lodging, and other necessities or niceties in exchange for their expertise.

- What song, tale, or superstition do you never tire of sharing?
- You know one song or story you never share publicly. What is it, and why do you keep it secret?

### Guardians & Rangers

The first monster hunters in Andaluria were rangers, trained to track and kill the unnatural. Many dedicate themselves to a single type of quarry—vampires, werewolves, or another creature of the night—as one might devote themselves to the hunt of elk or boar. Guardians stand beside them as the hammer to their spear, wielding specialized arms and brutal methods against creatures that don't die easily. Together, they form the backbone of Andaluria's monster-hunting tradition.

- What creature of the night are you sworn to destroy—and why?
- Someone's face, twisted in terror, is seared into your memory. Who is it, and why can't you forget this moment?

### Seraphs

Most Andalurian seraphs channel the radiant essence of the Golden Eminence. If you decide to play a seraph, think about how your character views the institution of the church—or its current leadership.

- Your display of power once converted a group of people to the Church of the Golden Eminence. What did you do, and who did you inspire to believe?
- You harbor a secret doubt about your professed faith. What is it, and what do you believe will happen if it's revealed?

### Warlocks & Witches

Attitudes toward warlocks and witches vary. According to official church canon, pacts and relationships with otherworldly entities are sinful by their very nature. But to many Andalurians, consorting with such beings is a matter of survival. As a result, warlocks and witches who live or work in areas dominated by the church often do so in secret or risk social persecution, while those who live where folk traditions prevail often enjoy increased status and respect.

- Someone you love who believes in the power of the Golden Eminence once turned against you. Who was it, and what happened?
- You use your magic to perform a service for members of your community. What do you do, and who taught you this skill?

## PLAYER PRINCIPLES

*If your group decides to play this campaign, give your players the following information before character creation.*

### Be the Hero

Ultimately, it's up to you to protect the innocent and defeat evil. While there are others who share your mission, they have their own mysteries to solve and monsters to defeat. You can't rely on them to do the work, so get your hands dirty.

### Confront Darkness in Whatever Form It Takes

Evil often arises in the form of vicious, supernatural creatures stalking the shadows, but it also manifests in the hearts of people corrupted by hate, greed, or desire. Hunt down darkness of every kind, mortal and monster alike.

### Embrace the Unknown

Your greatest weapons in this battle will often be ancient legends, strange superstitions, dark omens, and esoteric rituals. Seek out odd truths and dare to walk into places others fear to tread—you never know what you'll find in the dark.

### Be Proactive

Once your party picks up a case, it's up to you to pound the pavement, dig up dirt, and run down leads. If you ever get stuck, think of all the ways you can find information: searching for evidence, interviewing witnesses, gathering rumors, using magic rituals, running experiments, or seeking out experts. Don't wait for the action to come to you; chase it down.

### Put Your Heads Together

A successful hunt requires your party to acquire scattered bits of information and then piece it together to learn what you're up against, where to find it, and how to defeat it. Once you've gathered several clues, regroup and compare notes. Conjecture, guess, and think outside the box. Brainstorm how the clues you've uncovered might fit together, then figure out how to test your theory.

## GM PRINCIPLES

*Keep the following guidance in mind while you GM this campaign.*

### Portray a Vibrant World Twisted by Shadows

Andaluria should feel rich, decadent, and dangerous. Offer players moments of revelry and beauty—festivals, feasts, and wine—then twist them into something dark and unsettling. Show your players the extravagance of the upper class and the heavy toll it takes on those who labor for them.

### Feed the Characters' Fears

Lean into uncertainty. Show the PCs shadows moving just out of sight, give weight to every half-heard superstition, endanger those they love, and remind them that the night in Andaluria is never safe.

### Keep Monsters Mysterious

As the PCs uncover their quarry's hidden patterns and weaknesses, don't let their understanding devolve into a mechanistic model for how all supernatural entities behave. Keep the creatures the party encounters eerie by revealing strange or unexpected powers and vulnerabilities in individuals or groups. Challenge the players' assumptions about why certain rites and weapons work while others don't.

### Show and Tell

Give clues freely. If the players get stuck, show them what they've missed or remind them of what they've forgotten. Point them toward solid lines of investigation that lead to obvious conclusions. Don't try to trick your players or hide the ball.

### Let the Players Be Correct

When the players land on a reasonable solution to a mystery or a convincing method of dispatching the monster, let their solution be the correct one even if it doesn't match what you had planned. It's not reasonable to expect your players to read your mind—remember the core GM principles to "hold on gently" and "be fans of the players." Move things around behind the scenes to support the party's conclusions and plans.

### Tempt the Players with Dark Bargains

Power, forbidden knowledge, and survival often come at a cost. Present alluring "deals with the devil" that force players to grapple with the same choices that corrupted Andaluria long ago.

## DISTINCTIONS

*Use this information to prepare your campaign. You can also share it with your players as needed.*

### The Summer of Shadows

Andaluria's calendar is filled to bursting with feast days and festivals. The biggest and most important of these fiestas is the Midsummer Festival, which celebrates both the summer solstice (the shortest night of the year) and the anniversary of the uprising that won Andaluria its freedom. This year, however, the nights linger unnaturally, each one stretching longer than the last, and people from every echelon of society wonder aloud what this mysterious phenomenon heralds.

#### MONSTERS IN ANDALURIA

Werewolves, demons, and a thousand other creatures of the night have always stalked the shadows of Andaluria. In the past, sacred rituals and folk magic protected people from these mysterious beings, but as the nights have grown longer, new and more powerful monsters have appeared, undeterred by the practices of the past, and each horror must be investigated, understood, and slain through courage and ingenuity.

#### THE SOMBRAL SPADE

In the past, when lord and church refused to defend the villages, the people defended themselves. Today, the Sombral Spade, an illustrious order of monster hunters, has reignited this tradition. Their leader, Julio Cantafierro, is secretly cursed with lycanthropy, an affliction held at bay only by a Caio concoction made with special herbs foraged from the highest reaches of the Serracorte. Now, the Spade operates a clandestine network of cells dedicated to eradicating monsters throughout the region—beholden not to the council or the church, but the people of Andaluria.

#### A DEMONIC LEGACY

The recent eruption of monstrous activity within Andaluria is no accident. Unbeknownst to most locals, the volcanoes of the Serracorte Mountains conceal a portal to the Circles Below. A century ago, Emperor Arcedrio used this "hellgate" to gain otherworldly power. Though the portal was sealed with Arcedrio's death, it remains to this day, waiting to be located and reopened by someone hungry for power and heedless of danger. Enter Archbishop Mateo Veyra, a powerful patriarch of the Church of the Golden Eminence.

Driven by a desire to restore the waning influence of his church and enhance his own status, Veyra consulted the apocryphal texts hidden deep within his basilica's library, stole the fallen emperor's blackened heart from the Condesa's estate, and used it to unleash hell on Andaluria. As Veyra draws dark energy from the Circles Below, the night lingers unnaturally and the fires beneath the soil of the Serracorte flare back to life. Now Veyra preaches penitence and obedience to the church, proving the power of his god by banishing the very creatures he summons.

### The Caio People

Highly influential in political, economic, and social spheres, the Caio people have a storied history in Andaluria. Their culture is steeped in an ever-evolving oral tradition of folktales and fables, passed down through innumerable generations.

Heavily persecuted by the emperor as ready scapegoats in his play for power, the Caio were forced into hiding at the height of his reign. From these isolated communities rose a group of hardened, hopeful adventurers who toppled the tyrant. Those heroes established powerful dynasties in Jesaña and shepherded in a new age of art, music, and culture.

The Caio people use familial terms loosely and fondly, with no special status afforded to blood connection. Many Caio were originally wanderers or outcasts who came to Andaluria in search of community. Anyone with a willingness to learn Caio customs—and share their own—is welcomed into the fold, bringing new traditions and ideas while still honoring the beliefs and practices of their newly adopted home.

#### FORTUNES & FORTUNE TELLING

Throughout Andaluria, Caio fortune tellers, known as drabardi, are revered for their skill in navigating the threads of fate, and fortune-telling is a celebrated profession in many Caio communities. Different methods—such as cartomancy, tasseography, rune casting, osseomancy, and more—are passed down within families, and people from nearly every caste or community visit drabardi for guidance and insight. Most Andalurians consider ignoring drabardi divinations foolhardy and offer little sympathy when misfortune follows a warning unheeded.

If a PC wishes to have their fortune told by a drabardi, you can use the opportunity to guide their next story arc or future character development. Fortunes should provide cryptic guidance, not prescriptive instructions. You can use the fortune prompts below, or take inspiration from them to create your own:

*A lark sings from the [direction], heralding the coming [noun]. Time is [adjective], the song's end [promising phrase].*

*Great potential rests on [possessive pronoun or proper noun] shoulders, pressing with the weight of [heavy object]. To lift this weight, one must [piece of advice].*

*Vice and virtue are two sides of the same coin. Beware an excess of [virtue], lest it summon its darker half.*

*The stars look upon you with [emotion], awaiting your next move. A faint light points toward [location], beckoning.*

### Faith & Folklore

There are two predominant religious strains in Andaluria: the Caio folk tradition and the Church of the Golden Eminence. The Caio people embrace a syncretized collection of fables and folk rituals that elevate practicality above orthodoxy. These stories, superstitions, and sacred rites—which combine protective charms, cryptic folkways, and spirit worship—arise out of popular practice and are sustained through oral tradition. Members of the church, meanwhile, adhere to a set of beliefs ordained by the clergy. Their primary article of faith is that all reality is the sacred emanation of a single god, unnamed but identified as the Divinarch and symbolized by a sun. Today, cultural drift and shifting political realities have eroded the boundaries between these religions, and many Andalurians practice an amalgam of the two.

This cultural cross-pollination is not celebrated by everyone, however. There are those who still curse the church for its role in the emperor's persecution of the Caio people, and church orthodoxy deems Caio rites and rituals blasphemy. As a result, its most hardline adherents often ignore, insult, or even attack folk practitioners in spite of a growing faction of church reformists fighting to purge the institution of its prejudice.

But necessity makes for strange bedfellows, and a smart monster hunter will use whatever works—belief be damned. In Andaluria, folk rites and church rituals both have the power to protect people. As the region's nights lengthen and its monsters grow stronger, many of the usual tools are proving insufficient. Everyday Andalurians are beginning to look beyond their own beliefs for succor—not in the name of spiritual curiosity, but of physical survival.

### A Region Forged by Rebellion

Over a century ago, the tyrannical Emperor Arcedrio tightened his grip over Andaluria by seducing the leadership of the Church of the Golden Eminence into his service with dark power from origins unknown. The church branded the Caio people heretics and turned much of the commonfolk against them. In response to this wave of persecution, five Caio families rose up and led the rebellion that shattered Arcedrio's imperial rule, then took power for themselves and founded the Council of Five, Andaluria's most influential political body.

Though many Caio believe they betrayed their people's ways in exchange for luxury and decadence, the Council of Five became instrumental in transforming Andaluria from a loose affiliation of hamlets and estates into a sprawling economic powerhouse fueled by the production of some of the world's most sought-after wine, and their direct descendants still wield tremendous political influence from their seats of power in Jesaña's magnificently appointed Plaza del Sol.

#### A LAND DIVIDED

Today in Andaluria, nobility and peasantry, councilor and commoner, church and folk traditions all clash in a perpetual cycle. Andaluria's economy primarily relies upon its vineyards. But while the landowners profit, the laborers see the least coin, and the struggle between the rich and the poor shapes who lives, who starves, and who is fed to the shadows.

#### JESAÑA

The crown jewel of Andaluria is Jesaña, a city of twisting streets, bright markets, and sprawling docks. The buzz of its painted warehouses and dockside taverns only enhances the elegance of the terraced plazas where the council holds court, while the harbor bustles with trade from foreign lands. The city is as much a mask as it is a metropolis: beneath its sunlit streets and sparkling fountains lurk hidden crypts, secret cabals, and the sinful stain of an ancient curse.

The capital city is ruled by five hereditary offices that wield immense power. Each of the current councilmembers is a direct descendant or heir of the original five Caio people who overthrew Emperor Arcedrio:

- **Hellenor Galdez** (human, she/her) is the Harbormaster and oversees ships and trade.
- **Marwin Chopex** (emberkin, he/him) is the Quartermaster and handles the storehouses, armories, and wartime supply.
- **Andrea Nocta** (firbolg, she/her) is the Captain of the Nightwatch and deals with the guards, patrols, and spies.
- **Miquela Shaas** (faun, they/them) is the Archivist and manages records, decrees, and secret histories.
- **Exel Legasi** (aetheris, he/him) is the Market Keeper and governs guilds, fairs, and coinage.

Though united in rule, each office protects its own interests first. Their rivalries, compromises, and bargains shape the fate of the entire region.

#### THE CONDESA'S SECRET

In Andaluria, wealth is measured in wine, and there is one person whose riches rival the council's: Condesa Ysabela Delamoña. But the most powerful noble in Andaluria is no mere mortal. Condesa Delamoña is a vampire who has operated for centuries under the guise of an unbroken line of successors, her "daughters" merely masks for her immortal self. Her wealth flows from her fabled vineyards, but her vines drink more than water. Peasants vanish from her lands, taken in payment for her eternal harvest. The council looks the other way because her wines also fill their coffers—as surely as her secrets fill their nightmares.

The Caio uprising might never have succeeded without the Condesa's wealth, which armed the rebels as their resources waned. When victory came, Delamoña claimed the emperor's palace stronghold, the hilltop Castillo del Cardenal, which overlooks the vineyards below and holds the emperor's remains within its dungeon walls. There, the Condesa throws elaborate masquerades and bacchanals for the region's wealthy elite. As the owner of the largest and most productive vineyards that supply the city merchants with the rich, crimson wine they trade away for foreign gold—and as the beloved patron of Andaluria's popular summer festivals—the Condesa wields unmatched influence among the nobles and commonfolk alike. Perhaps this is why the whispered rumors and secret accusations of strange disappearances and blood-fed vines that haunt her name are never aired in public.

## THE INCITING INCIDENT

*You can use the prompt below to start your campaign, or create your own.*

The wine festival in the vineyard village of Santavio has always been a night of drink, music, and revelry. This year, however, the joy turned to horror: When the first casks of Condesa Delamoña's prized red were tapped, an acrid stench rose up, and foul creatures burst from the barrels. Twisted things of vine and claw, dripping with bloodied pulp, descended upon the celebration. Panic surged as the monsters dragged villagers screaming into the vineyards.

The Council of Five has dispatched the Nightwatch, led by Andrea Nocta, to "restore order," but their mission is clear: protect the remaining casks of wine, not the people. Nocta's forces are not equipped with the knowledge or experience to hunt the monsters terrorizing Santavio, and if these creatures return, they could threaten villagers and vintage alike.

As the citizens blockade themselves inside the quaint inns and cottages of Santavio, a story starts to spread: This is the curse of the Condesa filtering down to the people who work her land. They say her grapes are secretly fed with the blood of the innocent, and their tortured souls have transformed the vines into coagulated vessels of retribution. Meanwhile, Archbishop Mateo Veyra claims that Caio heretics are responsible for invoking these evil creatures and beckons the terrorized locals into the nearby church for protection.

The leader of the Sombral Spade, Julio Cantafierro, has requested the party's aid in tracking down the creatures responsible for the attacks tonight, recovering anyone they find alive, and destroying the source of the vineyard's corruption to prevent another tragedy like this from happening again.

What is the party's relationship to Cantafierro and the other members of the Sombral Spade? Is it money, honor, or something else entirely that compels the party to take up the hunt? Will the party prioritize the lives of the villagers taken or the people left behind? Based on the stories from witnesses to the attack, what about the monsters terrifies the party most? What evidence did the creatures leave behind, and how does it put the party on their quarry's trail?

### Transformations

The Dark Heart of Andaluria campaign frame includes special mechanics for introducing the Reanimated, Vampire, and Werewolf transformations. The following sections detail these mechanics, as well as advice for implementing them.

#### Reanimated

When a PC dies, discuss with their player whether they're interested in their character becoming reanimated. If they are, work with them between sessions to discuss what laboratory, workshop, or place of magic their character awakens within during a future session (for example, a necromancer's laboratory hidden beneath the Plaza del Sol). The character gains the Reanimated transformation and their maximum loadout is reduced to one. During a rest, they can use a downtime move to raise their maximum loadout by one until they reach the normal maximum loadout of five.

#### Vampire

When the PCs are facing a prominent vampire adversary, you can add the following feature to their stat block.

***Vampire's Curse - Action:*** Make an attack roll against a target within Melee range. On a success, you can **spend a [[Fear]]** to have this adversary sink their teeth into the target's neck and try to turn them into a vampire. Roll a number of **d8s** equal to this adversary's tier and deal that much physical damage to the target. Then roll a **d6**. If the rolled result matches any of the damage dice results, the target marks all their [[Stress]] and gains the Vampire transformation.

If the player wishes for their character to become a vampire, then the attack roll succeeds automatically. The PC takes the damage, marks all their [[Stress]], and gains the transformation.

#### Werewolf

When the PCs are facing a prominent werewolf adversary, you can add the following feature to their stat block.

***Wolf's Curse - Action:*** Make an attack roll against a target within Melee range. On a success, you can **spend a [[Fear]]** to have this adversary viciously bite the target. Roll a number of **d20s** equal to this adversary's tier and deal that much physical damage to the target. If the target takes Major or greater damage from this attack, activate a long-term Lycanthropy Countdown (6). It ticks down each time the PC takes a rest without using a downtime move to halt their lycanthropy's progression. When it triggers, the PC gains the Werewolf transformation.

If the player wishes for their character to become a werewolf, the attack roll succeeds automatically. The PC takes the damage and activates a long-term Lycanthropy Countdown (6) regardless of how many HP they marked as a result of the attack. When the countdown triggers, the PC gains the transformation.

#### IMPLEMENTING & REMOVING TRANSFORMATIONS

Before implementing transformation mechanics at your table, discuss with your players whether they want to include them. If you remove transformations from the campaign, talk about why their characters are immune to werewolf and vampire bites. Have they acquired rings or tattoos to protect them? Do they prepare tinctures or potions during downtime? Are they descended from familial lines with inherent resistance?

If you're using these mechanics, consider how the PCs might stop or remove the Vampire or Werewolf transformation, should they wish to. There could be a ritual known by a certain faction, a consumable available only in a village that's difficult to reach, a person who's known for aiding those who carry such curses, or anything else the party can seek out.

Prioritize player agency and consent over everything else, and ensure you're aiming to foster trust and collaborative worldbuilding.

### The Hunt

The Hunt is an optional procedure for preparing and running monsters during individual sessions or short arcs. In a Dark Heart of Andaluria campaign, not every major foe needs to be a monster, and this process can be modified for any prominent adversary, whether or not they possess supernatural abilities.

The basic structure of the Hunt comprises five beats: Arrival, Investigation, Escalation, Confrontation, and Epilogue. Not every Hunt needs to follow this structure, but it provides a solid basis for a monster-hunter adventure loop. As always, use what speaks to you and leave behind what doesn't.

- **Arrival:** The PCs are hooked into the adventure with a strange event that implies monster activity.
- **Investigation:** The PCs follow leads and gather clues to discover the truth of the monster.
- **Escalation:** The PCs bring together what they've discovered about the monster and develop a plan of action for finding and defeating it.
- **Confrontation:** The PCs attempt to banish, bind, or destroy the monster using what they've previously learned.
- **Epilogue:** The PCs wrap up loose ends and move on to their next mission.

#### Making a Monster

Monsters are distinguished from other adversaries in a Dark Heart of Andaluria campaign by two things: they're contaminated by the evil energy emanating from the hellgate beneath the Serracorte Mountains, and they're driven to destroy, consume, or corrupt the inhabitants of Andaluria. To create a monster, answer four questions about it:

**What is it?** Typically, the monster is a creature your players might easily recognize from legends and folklore, such as a vampire, werewolf, ghost, or demon. You can also draw on less-common monsters (such as the yeti or chupacabra), use an otherwise normal person corrupted by evil (such as a serial killer or tyrant), or invent something totally new. Give your monster a unique or interesting twist to make it less predictable.

**What does it want?** Decide what the monster wants. Common motivations include wrath, lust, power, sorrow, revenge, greed, hunger, envy, and fear. Here are some examples:

- The ghost of a noble seeks vengeance against the rival who arranged their assassination.
- An exile from an otherworldly court wishes to reestablish their power in Jesaña's noble halls.
- A grieving siren lures locals into the bay, hoping their presence will banish her sorrow.

**Where can you find it?** Decide on the monster's lair or hunting grounds. Try to think of places that can provide interesting set pieces: haunted houses, ancient cemeteries, village festivals, cursed forest clearings, and so forth.

**How can you defeat it?** All monsters have at least one weakness that enables the PCs to defeat them. Weaknesses can be material (such as cold iron or wooden stakes), ritual (such as chanting a sacred verse or placing a holy symbol over a door), circumstantial (such as direct sunlight or the sound of church bells), or some combination of these (such as drawing a circle of salt while reciting an ancient poem). If a monster is killed in a way that doesn't involve its weakness, it revives or escapes, often returning stronger than before. The PCs must wield a monster's weakness against it to slay it permanently.

***Optional:*** **What else should you worry about?** A well-designed monster is more than an individual physical threat to the party—the monster often commands other adversaries, endangers NPCs, and stalks special events or locations. You can use the following suggestions to make a monster more complex:

- Create the monster's underlings, such as weaker monsters or humanoid NPCs aligned with them.
- Invent important bystanders who could be endangered by the monster's actions, such as innocent villagers, other investigators, or city officials.
- Design events or locations that can serve as key set pieces highlighting the monster's strengths, weaknesses, and motivations.

#### Preparing Liminal Clues

Every horror leaves traces. Some are visible. Others wait in the threshold between knowing and unknowing.

A **Liminal Clue** is a piece of evidence or information that exists in potential until it's discovered. When the party searches a forsaken chapel, questions a dying witness, or sifts through the ashes of a beast's lair, the clue takes form—not because it was hidden there or had that form all along, but because the party's investigation calls it into being.

#### HOW LIMINAL CLUES WORK

Before a session begins, answer the questions from the previous "Making a Monster" section:

- What is it?
- What does it want?
- Where can you find it?
- How can you defeat it?

Then devise 4 to 6 pieces of information or evidence that point the players toward answering at least one of the questions about the monster. These are Liminal Clues—facts that float above the fiction like ghosts, waiting to manifest when called into play.

Write the Liminal Clues on notecards and keep them on hand during the session. Whenever the PCs delve into danger or explore the mystery in a way that could reveal the answer to one of the questions, you can refer to your collection of Liminal Clues. If a clue fits the tone and place, it manifests in that moment. Describe how the clue is revealed. Once all clues have emerged, the PCs can confront the horror fully armed with knowledge.

#### LET THEM WANDER

Don't bind clues to any particular place. Instead, let them linger over the scenes where it would make sense for them to appear. A bloodstained sigil might surface in a chapel or a dream, or a survivor's testimony might emerge from a child's drawing or an overheard conversation. Prepare only what information or evidence will manifest, not how or where.

#### HONOR THE NEW REALITY

Once revealed, a Liminal Clue becomes solid. Record it as an undeniable discovery: a fact, a sign, or a symptom of the monster's nature. It's no longer merely potential—it's now part of the world's grim reality.

#### GENERAL OR SPECIFIC?

The Liminal Clues you prepare in advance should be a mix of general information ("the victim was torn apart and partially devoured") and more specific pieces of evidence ("the only member of the family who wasn't attacked wears a silver necklace"). General information can usually arise in a wider variety of locations, but it can be difficult to improvise its form in the moment. Specific pieces of evidence, meanwhile, have slightly more defined forms but can be more difficult to fit into the party's investigation. Having a mix of both distributes the mental load.

#### GO WITH THE FLOW

Learning to use Liminal Clues is one of the trickiest and most rewarding parts of running a Hunt, so give yourself grace as you practice, and prioritize providing PCs with clues sooner rather than later.

The party might at times need to receive information in multiple ways before they'll act on it. If the players miss or ignore a clue you've given, use more than one of the ideas you planned (or another one you create on the fly) until the information has been reinforced enough for them to act on it. Where helpful, you can be candid with your players. Use phrases like "It's obvious that…" or "You can tell that…" to clearly communicate critical information.

#### Structure of a Hunt

The following sections provide guidance on running the session using the monster you've created and Liminal Clues you've prepared.

#### ARRIVAL

This is the scene or sequence that first hooks the PCs into the adventure by establishing a strange event that implies monster activity. There are many ways by which the PCs can be drawn into the mission: They might be present when a monster attacks, they could be called in by an NPC to investigate after the fact, or they might simply stumble upon the aftermath by happenstance. During this section of gameplay, the party acquires the first layer of information about the mystery or mission. The information you deliver to the party during this sequence should provide the players with multiple solid avenues of investigation.

#### INVESTIGATION

The PCs follow leads discovered during the Arrival or in this sequence. Look for opportunities to introduce Liminal Clues. If the party's investigation stalls, remind them of the following possible methods of investigation:

- **Searching for and Analyzing Physical Evidence:** This can include tracks or trails (wet footprints), trace evidence (wolf hair), notable items or props (a silver dagger inscribed with an insignia), or the results of medical examinations (the victims' lungs are filled with water) and scientific experiments (the wolf hair found at the scene turns to dust when touched with silver).
- **Interviewing Eyewitnesses:** An eyewitness might see the monster's activity directly if it occurs in public, or they might notice suspicious events or behavior that they can relate to the PCs after the fact.
- **Seeking Advice from Experts:** Archivists, investigators, and veteran monster hunters can offer guidance on dispatching certain types of monsters or interpreting certain clues.
- **Collecting Gossip and Rumors:** Interviewing NPCs wherever they gather, such as at the local market or tavern, often yields important information.
- **Researching Lore and Local History:** Helpful local history or monster lore can often be found in written records, such as church libraries or public archives.
- **Using Magic:** PCs with the appropriate spells or abilities can perform divination rituals or psychometric readings to gain information.

#### ESCALATION

In this sequence, the PCs bring together what they've discovered about the monster and develop a plan of action for finding and defeating it. Don't expect the players to necessarily reach the exact solution you intended—pivot to accommodate anything reasonable that the players come up with.

***Tip:*** *If the party seems stuck or confused, have the monster or its allies threaten or attack them (or an important NPC) and convey any undiscovered information in an obvious and impossible-to-ignore manner*.

#### CONFRONTATION

In this sequence, the PCs attempt to banish, bind, or destroy the monster using what they've learned. This usually means infiltrating the monster's lair, setting a trap, or surveilling the site of a future attack, and often culminates in a combat— though sometimes the party will come up with a clever plan to avoid violence altogether. In those instances, let the players succeed and reward them for thinking outside the box.

#### EPILOGUE

The party ties up any loose ends. This is a good opportunity to push forward any long-term projects, advance character arcs, pull on additional story threads, and attend to bookkeeping. Optionally, you can set the hook for the next session's Hunt.

#### Example of Play

### Prepping the Session

Shelley (she/her) is planning to run a monster hunt for Quincey (he/him), Mina (she/her), and Lucy (she/her).

#### MONSTER CONCEPT

Shelley starts by making her monster:

- **What is it?** Shelley's been on a classic J-horror kick, so she wants to do a ghost story in the vein of *Dark Water* or *The Ring*. She decides on the ghost of a young woman that sneaks into people's houses at night and sucks the souls out of their bodies.
- **What does it want?** Looking through the list of motivations, Shelley feels like wrath and revenge both make sense for a ghost. She decides that this is the ghost of a young woman named Mary, who went missing one year ago and was never found. Mary's spirit wants revenge on the village for not finding her or bringing her kidnapper to justice.
- **Where can you find it?** Shelley thinks the old well at the center of town would be a suitably spooky place to be a ghost's lair, so she decides her bones are still there at the bottom of the well.
- **How can you defeat it?** Shelley thinks the ghost can be laid to rest by finding its bones and burning them.
- ***Optional:*** **What else should you worry about?** Shelley thinks it could be cool if the ghost can summon or command the souls that she's already absorbed, so she chooses an appropriate stat block to represent them in case she finds a place to confront the PCs with them.

#### ACT STRUCTURE

Shelley looks at the Hunt structure and comes up with brief concepts for what could happen in each act. She isn't trying to plan out a plot; she just wants a general idea of what might happen, knowing that she'll almost certainly have to pivot or improvise in response to the players' decisions.

**Arrival:** The party will be called into a small village on the outskirts of Jesaña to investigate the mysterious death of a sleeping teenage boy. Shelley prepares a few questions she can ask the players at the top of the session to collaboratively build a tighter connection between the party and the mystery:

"One of you knows the mother of the victim. What's your relationship to her? How does she feel about you?"

"You've been to this village before. What business did you previously have here?"

"What rumors have you heard about this part of Andaluria that give you pause?"

**Investigation:** The party will have free rein to talk with the locals and investigate public spaces in the village. There, the party will learn about Mary's disappearance one year ago.

**Escalation:** Mary will strike again the night of the hunters' arrival. Depending on where the PCs are, they might have the chance to intercept the ghost and prevent her from taking another victim. If Shelley needs to threaten the PCs directly, she can have Mary or her spectral minion (the spirit of her first victim) attack the party or a notable NPC.

**Confrontation:** The party can try to lay Mary's spirit to rest. Shelley expects this to be a combat, so she reskins the Minor Chaos Elemental into Mary's ghost. She expects the showdown to happen at the well in the center of town, where Mary's bones are hidden, but it can easily be moved to another location if needed.

**Epilogue:** Shelley expects the PCs will have a roleplay scene or two with the locals. Depending on how well the party was able to defend the village, they might receive a unique item connected to the lore they uncovered or relationships they explored before leaving the village.

#### LIMINAL CLUES

Finally, Shelley needs 4 to 6 Liminal Clues—preferably a mix between general information that can be revealed in numerous places and specific evidence that gives the players a really strong lead (but may have to be fudged during play):

- The victim's spirit was drained while he slept.
- This week is the anniversary of Mary's disappearance.
- The village children don't play near the well anymore because they keep hearing mysterious crying echoing from there.
- A local returning from the fields late at night saw a young woman walking barefoot in the village. She didn't answer when called and disappeared when pursued.
- The ghost avoids bright light and open flame.
- There are wet footprints in the victim's bedroom.

### Playing the Session

As Shelley describes the party arriving at the village, she asks her prepared questions to the players. Together, they establish that Quincey's character, a slyborne infernis ranger, used to work in Jesaña with the victim's mother, and they had a romantic relationship before she got married to someone else and moved to the village to raise a family.

#### ARRIVAL

When the party investigates the victim's bedroom, Mina's character (a highborne human witch) uses her "Commune" feature to contact the boy's spirit. Shelley decides that because the victim's spirit was absorbed, Mina can't establish a connection to it, but her attempt still yields useful information: the room is suffused with the spectral echoes of the ghost's spirit-draining attack. This is close to the first Liminal Clue that Shelley prepared, but it's not an exact match; Shelley rewrites the clue as "The victim's spirit was drained by a ghost" and hands the card to Mina. Lucy's character (a ridgeborne faerie sorcerer) looks around the room for physical evidence, so Shelley describes wet footprints leading from the window to the bed. Importantly, Shelley didn't ask for an action roll from Lucy; just by searching an area where the clue could be found, Lucy caused it to manifest.

#### INVESTIGATION

The party splits up to expand their investigation. Quincey and Lucy look at the "wet footprints" clue and ask whether the village is near a stream or a watermill. Shelley could say, "No, but there's a well," but instead pivots to make the players correct and amends the Liminal Clue about the well to the mill. Shelley asks how they would find out, and the players say they just want to look around. Shelley says, "Yes, there is a dilapidated mill at the edge of the village, which no one's used for years." Quincey and Lucy decide to investigate the mill.

Mina, meanwhile, decides to pursue the ghost lead: "What can I find out about ghosts in this area?" Shelley asks whether Mina has specific questions in mind or just wants general information. Mina says she wants general information on ghosts in Andaluria. Shelley says Mina knows (by reputation) of a retired ghost hunter who lives in a cottage in the woods a couple hours away. Does she want to take the better part of the day to go visit them? Mina says yes, and Shelley cuts back to Quincey and Lucy…

As this sequence proceeds, Shelley invents locations and NPCs as needed, prompting the players for input as she goes. Quincey and Lucy encounter a local group of kids daring one another to steal something from "Mary's mill," and Shelley gives them the Liminal Clue that says, "The village children don't play near the mill anymore because they keep hearing mysterious crying echoing from there." Mina visits with the retired ghost hunter and learns that the only sure way to defeat a ghost for good is to lay their spirit to rest, either by magic ritual or by burning their remains. Shelley makes a note for herself that the Liminal Clue "The ghost avoids bright light and open flame" has now changed into "To defeat a ghost, lay its remains to rest."

#### ESCALATION

Mina rushes back to the village as the sun sets. Quincey and Lucy enter the mill but are expelled by Mary and her spectral minions because they don't have a way to defeat her yet. The PCs reconvene in the tavern at the center of town and share the information they've collected. Shelley asks them to spitball different ways they might lay the ghost's remains to rest based on the information they have. Quincey recalls that the local children called it Mary's Mill—who's Mary? The PCs ask the other patrons at the tavern and learn about Mary Whitton, the woman who went missing one year ago, and that her disappearance was never solved. After some discussion (and light prompting from Shelley), the players come up with their answers to the four key questions:

- **What is it?** The ghost of Mary Whitton, sucking the spirit out of the locals in their sleep.
- **What does it want?** She's angry that the village never solved her disappearance.
- **Where can we find it?** In the abandoned mill.
- **How do we defeat it?** Find her bones inside the mill and bury them.

Note that the answer to the final question is not what Shelley planned, and it's based on a little bit of a guess on the players' part. However, it makes sense that Mary is haunting the mill because her body was hidden there. The players decide to raid the mill and search for Mary's remains so they can lay her to rest.

#### CONFRONTATION

The party heads to the mill that night. Shelley runs the combat mostly as anticipated, but with a complication that Mary can't be defeated until a player finds her bones by searching the mill with appropriate action rolls. When the players discover Mary's bones, Shelley asks them to describe the ritual they use to lay her to rest—but rules that it will succeed automatically because failure would be anticlimactic.

#### EPILOGUE

Shelley decides to run the Epilogue as a montage in which each player gets to describe one way their PC honors Mary's memory:

- Quincey convinces the town to tear down the mill.
- Lucy crafts a simple headstone for Mary and places it in the local graveyard.
- Mina returns to the retired ghost hunter and has tea with them (she liked this NPC and wants them to become a regular fixture throughout the campaign).

Shelley finishes the session with a teaser of the next session's mystery: She describes a local forester running full tilt through the dark forest, blood streaming down his face. He breaks through the treeline and skids to a stop just before narrowly avoiding toppling over a cliff. The full moon glows behind him as his eyes dart back and forth, desperate for an escape route. Then a bone-chilling howl pierces the night sky.

## SESSION ZERO QUESTIONS

*Ask your players any of these questions, or make your own.*

- You once fought a monster together and one of you nearly died. Who saved them, and what scar, physical or otherwise, remains from that night?
- Who in Jesaña (a noble, a guild, or even the council) claims you owe them a debt?
- What local ward, charm, or ritual do you all believe in, even if others dismiss it as peasant nonsense?
- What festival or celebration did you all attend together in the past, and what happened there that still lingers between you?
- Which of the ruling families has taken an interest in your group and why? Do they see you as useful pawns, dangerous meddlers, or future allies?
- How does the church regard your party? Are you wayward souls to bring back into the fold, heretics to be watched, or necessary protectors of the people?
