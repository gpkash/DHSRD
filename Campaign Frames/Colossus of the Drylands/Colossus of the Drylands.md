# COLOSSUS OF THE DRYLANDS

> A prosperous region of gunslingers and outlaws has fallen into terror as colossal creatures awaken from beneath the earth.

## THE PITCH

The **Drylands** had moved on from old stories. Superstitious miners spoke of crystals grown from the life-essence of **Forgotten Gods**, but the real folk had real work to do, and gods were left to campfire tales — until a massive colossus answered the call of something ancient and apocalyptic, rising from the earth and devastating the land. The dusty town of **Wyllin's Gulch** was thrown from its routine of industry and exploitation, forced to raise scattered armies and watch them die to the behemoth's strength. And this is only the first of many titanic beasts. PCs play a heroic posse of mavericks who must save their home from a legion of colossi and the primordial god seeking vengeance.

## OVERVIEW

### The Prophecy of the Drylands God

In the center of the world, a mountain. Deep beneath it, an ancient and terrible **Forgotten God** struggling to break free of his confinement. One day **Kudamat, the First Doom** will escape his earthen tomb with the help of his nine colossi — the **Children of Godfell** — to sunder the world. As the New Gods fall from the **Hallows Above**, Kudamat will slay them with his stone sickle, flooding the **Mortal Realm** with their blood. In that sanguine tide, Kudamat and the new generation of colossi (each made from the god's own being) will destroy mortal creatures and make room for abhorrent creations to repopulate land, sea, and sky. In his triumph, Kudamat will free the trapped gods of his generation and become high lord among all divinities.

That's how the old story went. Most people thought it was just another tall tale of the Drylands.

### The History of the Gods

When the Forgotten Gods built the Mortal Realm in the **Earliest Age**, they made a place of joys and sorrows, freedom and labor — and could not fathom the **New Gods** would one day overthrow them, casting them from the Hallows Above into the Mortal Realm and the **Circles Below**.

Many Forgotten Gods were trapped within the ground. Their deific essence bleeds from their wounds; whether intentional or not, that power seeped into the earth where they're entombed, running like veins of stone up to the surface. The crystal born from these veins is **essentia** — and **all magic in the Mortal Realm comes from its power**. There's a mad scramble to harvest it; in the eyes of those in power, land with a vein of essentia is worth the millions of lives sacrificed in the mines built to dig it up.

### Godfell Mountain

**Godfell Mountain** is one such land. Sitting amid high mesas in arid country peppered by meager lakes and sparse forests, it marks the richest essentia mines on the continent. It isn't literally the center of the world, but it's the hub of a region full of newfound wealth, industry, and an ever-expanding network of dusty towns run by crooked sheriffs.

The veins of essentia running from Godfell come from Kudamat himself. As the makeshift towns follow the veins ever closer to Godfell, they move toward the blighted earth the New Gods left to keep mortals from the prison of the ancient god. The mountain is a desolate and dangerous place where few can survive — but that doesn't stop the powerful mining operations and bold prospectors from trying their luck.

Kudamat was trapped inside Godfell Mountain — or rather, the mountain formed around him at the end of the Earliest Age. To trap the **First Doom**, the usurper gods split part of his being into nine pieces and tasked nine mortals with hiding the **soul shards** so Kudamat could not reach his full power.

But this paramount task should not have gone to mere mortals. The nine riders took the shards as far from the mountain as they could and buried them in the arid earth — but it wasn't far enough. Over the centuries, Kudamat reached out through the dirt and stone, veins of crystalline essentia stretching out like tendrils to find his missing pieces. Over time, the godly strife of the Earliest Age and the resting places of the soul shards drifted out of memory and into legend. Legends most sensible folk ignored.

### The Awakening

When Kudamat found the first piece of his soul, he began channeling its power to restore his godhood, and a single thundering quake tore a fissure through the center street of Wyllin's Gulch. Locals took it for a terrible earthquake; they didn't understand the apocalyptic toll ringing across the sands. Kudamat then used a fragment of his divine might to craft a colossus and droves of accompanying **aberrations** to guard the soul shard while he siphons the rest of his power and seeks the other eight shards. Even with the barest portion restored, Kudamat's reawakening is all but inevitable. He grows stronger by the day, searching for the remaining pieces and building more colossi to guard them. If left unchecked, he'll reclaim the power of all nine and break his earthen shackles.

**Preacher Rayos** (he/him) was the first — or only the loudest — to recognize the rising colossus outside Wyllin's Gulch as the first step in the Prophecy of the Drylands God. He proclaimed Kudamat's story, warning of the increased dangers of the god's charged essentia. He stressed the importance of finding the soul shards and keeping the god from siphoning them. He took his flock from the **Left Luck Outpost** to Wyllin's Gulch — the unofficial capital of the western line — where he continues to preach, growing more popular each day. He already had a following in his community, and now times are apocalyptic enough that people are eager for a spiritual leader.

### Wyllin's Gulch

Wyllin's Gulch worships no gods but the earning and spending of gold. In the early days of the Essentia Rush, roughly 40 years ago, the Gulch was established as the first western outpost in the region around Godfell, just a few miles from a rich vein. As more crystals were unearthed, powerful moguls established processing plants. Belching factories were built up the sides of the **Twin Turtles** — two great mesas bordering the burgeoning town — their massive conveyor lifts hauling tons of essentia to the manufactories that process the crystals into the conductive batteries most folk use to power their homes, weapons, and magical items.

Today, with its natural fortifications and wealthy elite, Wyllin's Gulch is the gatekeeper for anyone approaching Godfell on the western train line. Townspeople either work mining and processing essentia or run money traps for new fools come to try prospecting.

A wide variety of folks live here. The arid plains, breathtaking skies, and possibility of boundless riches attract all types. Living away from the prying eye of larger law enforcement is another perk — there are sheriffs in Wyllin's Gulch, but they're paid to protect the mines and manufacturing rather than uphold any version of "justice." Outlaw gangs steal valuable crystals; others see mining as anathema and destroy the equipment that tears essentia from the ground. Most outlaws find more success at smaller essentia outposts than at this bastion of dusty fortitude.

### Essentia Outposts

Outposts range from a few tents to thriving towns. Some form when a few folks mine the same vein or sift the same river. Some are built by companies that purchased a parcel containing a vein and installed a small worker settlement. Others are larger towns built by people wanting community alongside their work. One thing is always true: where there's an outpost around Godfell, there's a vein of essentia. Now, these outposts face the coming threat of more colossi rising as Kudamat locates his shards and uses the veins to siphon back his power.

## COMMUNITIES

All [[Communities]] are available; many have unique aspects in this campaign.

- **[[Loreborne]]** — Many hold fervent beliefs about the Forgotten Gods, the New Gods, and essentia. Loreborne take a more scholarly, possibly evangelical approach. Some follow the "forgotten ways" and use raw essentia in their spiritual practice; others abstain entirely, believing it's a profanity to the New Gods.
- **[[Orderborne]]** — Some come from the law (sheriffs and deputies, often the most powerful members of their town, usually in mine-mogul pockets). Others are organized outlaw bands — some moral (sabotaging essentia operations), some practical (need crystals to sell or power their own magic). Either way they operate by a code, unique as it may be.
- **[[Highborne]]** — Most come from elsewhere, seeking to enrich themselves further through the essentia industries. Some born into wealth; others fallen business owners forced to the desert for their "next big venture." They often find themselves at odds with the beating sun and clinging dust — but they have employees to do anything that could even vaguely be classified as "work."
- **[[Ridgeborne]] and [[Underborne]]** — Both thrive in this region of mountains, mines, high deserts, and sparse greenery. Ridgeborne might come from small outposts or family encampments atop dangerous crags — some bold enough to live where others won't, on Godfell itself. Underborne might be communities that took to old essentia mines for refuge, building homes in the darkness; or essentia miners forming small underborne communities with fellow professionals who spend most of their lives excavating.
- **[[Slyborne]]** — Plenty of bad actors who want to get rich quick: selling useless parcels, shilling water-and-vinegar "snake oil," outright theft. But some communities are more interested in stopping major operations from stripping essentia. The **Harlan Family** — a small but active posse led by **Trace Harlan** (they/them) — works out of Wyllin's Gulch to stop ambitious tycoons from mining and producing essentia batteries.
- **[[Wanderborne]]** — Just as likely to originate from within the region as arrive from elsewhere. Their wanderings tend to gravitate toward essentia veins. Some communities are as old as Godfell Mountain itself, with stores of essentia they visit across the desert. Others are new groups of unprepared prospectors more likely to die by sun or snake than find any ease.

> [!session-zero]+ Sample background questions
> - *(Loreborne)* Kudamat's impending return changed many beliefs — but not yours. What did you experience that makes your faith unshakeable?
> - *(Orderborne)* You and your posse have killed to get what you need. What rules do you have about who you'll never harm?
> - *(Highborne)* What disaster befell your family's fortune that precipitated your move to the Drylands?
> - *(Ridgeborne/Underborne)* You once found something unexpected near Godfell Mountain. What was it, and who did you tell?
> - *(Slyborne)* What darkness in your past did you attempt to leave behind when coming to the Drylands?
> - *(Wanderborne)* You used to live in an outpost, but something terrible happened that caused you to leave. What happened, and why will you never return?

## ANCESTRIES

All [[Ancestries]] are available. Setting-specific notes:

- **All ancestries** — Many folks profess to feel essentia in the ground, especially while Kudamat is using it to siphon back his power. No one can prove this, but many use the claim to land high-paying mining positions.
- **[[Clank]]** — Powered by essentia. If a player picks clank, they should consider how their character regularly obtains the resource and what happens if they can't get enough.
- **[[Infernís]]** — Should consider how their heritage from the demons of the Circles Below plays into the various faiths of the Drylands.
- **[[Fungril]], [[Galapa]], and [[Ribbet]]** — Natives of the Drylands tend to be well-adapted to the desert. Those who aren't from the deserts commonly live near the few lakes in the region or devise systems to travel with large amounts of water.

## CLASSES

All [[Classes]] are available. Setting-specific notes:

- **[[Druid]]** — More equipped than many to navigate the inhospitable environment. Familiar with desert flora and fauna; spells and abilities should reflect that.
- **[[Ranger]]** — Many are "lone riders" who prefer only the company of their mount and ride from outpost to outpost. Often defenders of the weak who stand against local law enforcement when deputies push people around.
- **[[Seraph]]** — Many have strong faith relationships, worshipping either the Forgotten Gods (such as Kudamat) or the New Gods. This affects their relationship with essentia: the former use the crystals prolifically; the latter may have a fraught relationship with magic. Many become preachers, healers, and prophets.
- **Syndicate [[Rogue]]** — Often operates as deputies, mining-operation overseers, or outlaw posse members. Whatever group, charm and clandestine work are key to their organization's covert goals.

## PLAYER PRINCIPLES

- **Embrace the Drylands.** Consider how your character's place in the Drylands shapes their aesthetics and approach to the world. Find reasons to love the land despite its harshness as you fight to protect it from the monsters trying to tear it asunder.
- **Face the unstoppable.** Your character stands against creatures taller than the largest buildings, their destructive power matched only by the worst natural disasters. Decide what makes your character particularly equipped to destroy colossi — and, more importantly, what's personally at stake should they fail.
- **Navigate your faith.** The Drylands are a land of extremes — extremely hot, harsh, easy to die. Religion follows. Some believe gods never existed and the essentia stories are marketing. Others worship Forgotten Gods, attributing spiritual aspect to their use of essentia. Still others see essentia as profanity and commit crimes to close mines in the name of the New Gods. Consider what your character believed before the first colossus rose — and how it might have shifted.
- **Ride like the wind.** Just about everyone born in the Drylands can ride a mount from the moment they walk; newcomers learn quickly. Unless a feature provides an animal companion or applicable spell, your mount won't change your mechanics or grant combat advantage. It will, however, let you cross between towns and stretches of desert with added ease and speed. Pick a mount from the list below or invent your own:

> [!mount]+ Mount options
> Horse · Buffalo · Deer · Elk · Bear · Mountain Lion · Dire Wolf · Rhinoceros · Giant Goat · Giant Jackalope · Giant Rattlesnake · Giant Lizard · Giant Turtle · Giant Toad · Giant Scorpion · Giant Centipede · Shadow Beast · Robotic Mount

## GM PRINCIPLES

- **Contrast the mundane and the extraordinary.** Before the first colossus, things were ordinary: train rides, prospecting, drinking, eating, dodging posses. Now, with the colossus rampaging, mines flush with explosive power, and creatures forming overnight — the cataclysmic foes become more apocalyptic against the mundane moments only small towns can offer. (And the impending First Doom doesn't slow debt collectors or essentia refineries hungering for fuel.)
- **Emphasize scope and scale.** The Drylands are a big place. Massive mesas, sky to the horizon, infinite-feeling nights. Even on a normal day this place makes you feel small. Confrontations with colossi remind: the adventurers are ants to them, scaling their bodies and stinging with tiny weapons. A single footstep could level a town. Let both the colossi and the landscape feel immense.
- **Make colossi an allegory for personal woe.** The colossi are formed from aspects of Kudamat's soul and the environments they inhabit — but they can serve as allegories for the PCs' personal journeys. In session zero, note themes and struggles the players want to highlight in backstory… then make them gigantic. (Example: a corrupt small-town sheriff with too much influence becomes a metaphor mapped onto **Zuudra, the Many Arms of Calamity** — destructive overreach, an inescapable grip on the outpost.)
- **Offer chances for creative solutions to colossal problems.** Some players will be satisfied climbing and stabbing weak points until the colossus dies. Others will plan elaborate traps or attempt to tame one. Lean into player creativity — give them plenty of nails until they decide which hammer to use. Each colossus should have a unique personality, physicality, and weakness that encourages ingenuity.

## DISTINCTIONS

### The Children of Godfell

Each of Kudamat's nine soul shards is protected by a colossus called a **Child of Godfell** — a physical manifestation of the god's will, hellbent on protecting their soul shard as its power siphons back to the mountain. Formed from elements of the soul shard's surroundings, each is unique in personality, form, and capabilities.

You'll need nine colossi to populate the area around Godfell. Use any of the examples below as inspiration, or invent your own.

#### Example Children of Godfell

- **Aandira, the One Who Devours** — humanoid-spider mix; scurries on bent-back limbs with a dozen mouths of broken teeth.
- **Aikar, Shepherd of the Meek** — bipedal; arms affixed with bladed shields. Aikar's howl can stop living creatures in their tracks.
- **Aphedos, the Creeping Darkness** — formed from thousands of independently operating flies. Impossible to climb; must be brought down by alternate means.
- **Balorma, the Blooming Hornwolf** — wolf with elk horns of flourishing plant life. When she steps, she plants roots that suck the life from all they touch.
- **Chakantis, He Who Knows the End** — crystalline beetle whose wingbeats rain deadly shards onto the ground; abdomen vibrates at frequency that shatters even stone.
- **Daktadae, the Cleaver** — massive steel rhinoceros formed from a steam train, a cleaver emerging from his head instead of a horn. Seeks vengeance on those who've harmed him in the past.
- **Eriphisia, the Radiant Light of Eternal Cleansing** — crystalline statue with dozens of eyes that cast blinding light, reflecting the sun upon victims as a ray of white-hot death.
- **Ga'mor, the Shade of Quick Death** — formed from the shadows of mountains, buildings, and people. When this amalgam steps into new darkness, it can adjust its shape to match the bounds of the shadow.
- **Heladra, Sower of Sorrows** — cloaked figure who weeps waterfalls from unseen eyes, flooding the land wherever she travels.
- **Ikeri, Injuries Untold** — apelike creature with the face of a bird; made of stone, carries an enormous mesa upon their back.
- **Impahk, They Who Know No Rest** — massive desert cyclone that never stops moving and consumes all they touch.
- **Kelir, the Virulent Hate and Her Hundred, Hundred Children** — crablike, mountainous beast whose children scurry around her, bringing food as tribute.
- **Mirmett, the Lover of the Hollow Hearts** — giant humanoid cage that places enemies inside themself to be killed or eaten by the creatures within.
- **Moraidus, the Keeper of Graves** — centipede formed from grave dirt and tombstones; arms made of skeletons that hang from their composite form, scrabbling at anything that comes near.
- **Nocte, the Invisible Tide** — imperceptible to the eye; swims through the ground like a fish through water and attacks from below.
- **Oosha, Eerrae, and Yyisu, Three Who Parse Infinity** — three humanoid figures that fight separately or come together as one terrifying form. Cut victims into pieces with rusted blades.
- **Poy, Sky Skimmer of the Dust Sea** — massive shoebill stork formed from the dust of the Drylands; traps enemies in their cavernous beak.
- **Quelle, the Innocent Pain** — feline creature covered in thousands of burrowing tendrils; fascinated by living beings, tears enemies apart to look inside.
- **Syan'til, the Serpent of the Last Light** — serpent of lightning who races down from the sky to strike with blazing speed.
- **Takkan, the Bone Bearer** — built from hundreds of bones from deep within the earth; ever-shifting, ever-grinding form.
- **Usandi, the Bear of Five Faces** — sees in all directions; ring of snapping teeth around the neck, sharpened spine bones jutting from the back, thrashing claws on the limbs.
- **Vian and Naiv, the Makers of Undoing** — vespertilian beasts that corrode what they touch until it falls to dust. Can only be killed by simultaneously striking weak points on both creatures.
- **Xehamras, the Last Breath** — suffocates victims and consumes their dying breath to increase his power, all while barking raspy laughter from his coyote head.
- **Zuudra, the Many Arms of Calamity** — cephalopod with eight writhing limbs that rapidly shift between hardened stone spikes and rivers of flowing magma.

#### Designing your own colossus

- What do they look like? Similar to an animal, a player ancestry, or built from pieces of the physical landscape? An amalgamation of many things?
- What is their personality? Quick to dispatch enemies, or do they toy with victims? Loyal to Kudamat, or do they enjoy learning about mortals?
- How do they move? Lumber across the landscape, fly on feathered wings, disappear and reappear elsewhere? Different forms based on terrain or situation?
- What abilities do they have? Many arms attacking many enemies, an army of shadow beasts, ripping memories from minds? Consider how they attack at full strength and when gravely injured.
- What is their weakness? Vulnerable to fire or another element? Do they undergo a transformation that leaves them open? A pair strong together, weak when separated?
- How does the colossus relate to themes the players want to explore? A colossus that animates the dead if characters have experienced grief. One that grows plants wherever they step if the campaign is about environmental destruction. One that corrodes all they touch if characters begin to doubt if their actions are heroic.

### The Soul Shards

No one knows exactly where each shard is, but regions overrun with aberrations and supercharged essentia indicate one is nearby. When Kudamat locates a shard and begins siphoning, the ground around it quakes with one powerful tremor and the supercharged essentia vein glows brightly, day and night. This helps locals pinpoint location — so to prevent it being found, Kudamat simultaneously creates a sentinel: a colossus that stalks and guards the entire region.

Once a colossus is slain, the soul shard shatters deep beneath the earth, causing a ring of essentia to violently erupt out of the ground in a mile radius around the shard's location. Destroying the shards prevents Kudamat from absorbing further power — the sooner each shard is shattered, the less power Kudamat will have when he emerges from the mountain.

### The Aberrations

From the divine power surging through the veins come new and terrible beasts. **Aberrations** are smaller than the colossi but share the same goal: keep all creatures away from the soul shards. Some even resemble the colossi they serve.

Any existing adversaries can be reflavored as aberrations, but you can build your own. When designing:

- What do they look like? Twisted and terrible beings never before seen in the Mortal Realm. They usually resemble the surrounding landscape, a characteristic they embody, or the colossi they serve.
- Where can they be found? Consider how they hunt in their environment. Sizes range from miniscule to house-sized, each operating differently.
- What powers do they have? Resemble a nearby colossus? Or something else entirely? Some fight in packs, some have one powerful ability, some wear the party down with sheer numbers.

### Essentia

All magic in the Drylands is powered by essentia. Without crystals and the batteries made from them, magic users can't access this power. Across the realm, since the dawn of mortalkind, people have mined the earth and plumbed the seas for these crystals. Only in the recent past have people learned that, while raw essentia provides enormous power, it's more reliable (and more efficient with less-potent stones) to manufacture **essentia cells** — making magic more accessible and the Drylands increasingly wealthy.

Ask players about their character's relationship with essentia:

- Are they willing to harness the life-essence of ancient gods, or do they consider it profanity?
- Do they install raw or polished essentia crystals in armor or weapons, or use modern essentia cells?
- How do they acquire new essentia when crystals or cells wear out — buy in shops, steal, mine as they travel?

### Drylands Outposts

Since the Essentia Rush began ~40 years ago, settlements like Wyllin's Gulch formed around Godfell. They're always called outposts because everyone knows that once the local veins are cleaned out, the town packs up overnight.

#### Example outposts

- **The Cinderline** — built on unique black sand around a river that washed bits of essentia downstream. No one's panned successfully in years. Locals fiercely protect their water — the Cinderline is the last fresh-water stop before a long stretch of desert.
- **Havalaugh** — sprawling tents reaching at least a mile, no permanent buildings. Run by **Pickens** (they/them); fairly receptive to outsiders. Premier marketplace on the eastern side; the **High Summer Festival** is famous enough to nearly quintuple the population for a week. No sheriff — but anyone who harms a citizen is never heard from again.
- **The Iron Kettle** — built around a massive **Iron Tusk Corp** mine. Rustic place full of skilled smiths and crafters. The need for steel goods has given the smiths more leverage than Iron Tusk would prefer; a powerful trade union keeps them in check and governs "The Kettle."
- **Pryor** — built into a long-abandoned essentia mine, often unnoticed by passing travelers who don't know where to look. Insular folk who don't welcome guests — but if you have the right contact, you'll learn the best Drylands tricks. Most families stretch back to before the Essentia Rush.
- **Defiance** — famous for its position high atop a mesa. Rumored to have been founded by thieves escaping the law; a considerable cache of artillery aimed at the ground below, and they aren't known to fire warning shots.

## THE INCITING INCIDENT

When the first colossus rose outside Wyllin's Gulch, most people spent considerable time panicking. The sheriff raised a small army; brave miners piled old equipment in its path; factories shuttered to protect their stores of essentia. Every effort was ineffective.

After most of his deputies died in a single crushing blow, **Mayor Logan Hartly** (she/her) put out an ad: *"Help Wanted. Whoever can dispatch the colossus and its pack of aberrations will receive a chest of fully processed essentia."* It's a good deal for anyone who can manage to fight a gigantic force of nature and live.

Meanwhile, **Baron Ryder Destry** (he/him) — his name, not a title — recently learned that Kudamat's siphoning has supercharged the essentia veins connected to the shard, making the stones ten times as valuable. He's offering "an army's supply of powerful essentia" to anyone who'll collaborate with the colossus to stand guard around the soul shard until Kudamat completes his ritual. Destry swears his factory guard can keep Wyllin's Gulch safe and he'll keep mining the powerful essentia day and night.

The party was assembled by the mayor's "Help Wanted" ad — but Destry's offer puts more than a few wrenches in an already near-impossible plan. Other groups are already on their way to the colossus to help guard the shard. Smaller aberrations have begun to rise from the veins. If the party can fight through them, they'll have to topple a colossus — a feat no one in the Drylands has ever accomplished.

Will the party take the mayor's offer or stand alongside the colossus while Destry mines? Can they play both sides? Will Hartly keep her word if they topple it? Can they kill the massive creation before it destroys Wyllin's Gulch or any of the smaller outposts? Once they've destroyed it, are the stories true? Will another rise to fulfill the Prophecy?

### Hunts and Interludes

Play in this frame is broken into two phases.

**Hunt phase** — Once the party finds out a new colossus has risen, they're in the hunt. Riding into the relevant outpost, gathering info, saving innocents from new aberrations, tracking the colossus, and battling it. The threat should always feel present, even from a distance. A hunt might last days to a week, but every moment should count as Kudamat siphons power and the colossus wreaks havoc.

**Interlude phase** — Once the colossus is slain, PCs take time for smaller, more personal tasks throughout the region: ruffians robbing folks outside the local saloon, a derailed essentia-eruption train missing entirely, a farmer's cattle devoured by something strange in the night. An interlude lasts days to weeks or more, depending on player desires.

### Colossal Adversaries

This frame introduces a unique adversary type: **Colossus**. You'll need to build colossi for your party to fight.

#### Colossus stat blocks

Because they're so large, colossi use **multiple adversary stat blocks**, each representing a segment of their form. Example: Ikeri, Injuries Untold could be six stat blocks — two legs, two arms, a torso, a head. These segment stat blocks exist within a **colossus framework**: a stat block holding info and features that apply to the colossus as a whole.

Some segments can be **Broken**. While Broken, the segment can't use actions or reactions until the condition is cleared. When a segment marks its last HP, it's **Destroyed** and can no longer use any of its features. A colossus is defeated when all its segments are Destroyed — but colossi often have an alternative defeat condition. A Destroyed segment isn't gone or detached; it just doesn't work anymore.

#### Example feature library

> [!feature]+ Passives
> - **Fatal:** When this segment is Destroyed, the colossus is defeated.
> - **Chain:** When all segments in this chain are Destroyed, the colossus is defeated. *(Chain (A) / Chain (B) for clarity across multiple chains.)*
> - **Strike (Range):** This segment is immune to damage from attacks not made within the listed range.
> - **Armored:** When this segment marks HP from an attack, it marks 1 fewer HP.
> - **Climbing (+N):** This segment's Difficulty gains a +N bonus against action rolls made to climb it. *(Can also be a negative number to reduce the climbing Difficulty.)*
> - **Not Climbable:** This segment can't be climbed.
> - **Weak Point:** The colossus has a weak point on this segment. When this segment marks HP from an attack within Melee range, it marks an additional HP. *(Try other effects too — segment unusable for a few GM turns, halved damage next spotlight, etc.)*

> [!feature]+ Actions
> - **Shake Off:** Spend a Fear. All creatures on the colossus must make a Strength Reaction Roll. Fail: fall to the ground and mark a HP. Succeed: mark a Stress.
> - **Kudamat's Rage:** Spend 2 Fear. All PCs within Very Far range succeed on a Presence Reaction Roll or become Shaken. While Shaken, they don't gain Hope on a result with Hope and instead clear the Shaken condition.

> [!feature]+ Reactions
> - **Swatting Pests:** When the colossus is attacked by a flying target within Far range, make a standard attack against the attacker. Success adds a d20 to the damage roll and knocks the target to the ground within Far range.
> - **Collapsed:** When the colossus marks at least 1 HP on all Leg segments, it Collapses. While Collapsed, segments not normally accessible from the ground can be climbed. *(Often paired with Not Climbable.)*
> - **Colossal Power:** When the colossus fails an attack, you gain a Fear.

#### Segment vs. Colossus effects

When a feature names a specific segment ("the Head"), it's only that segment. When it names the colossus as a whole ("Ikeri"), it's the adversary collectively. So a reaction triggered when Ikeri marks 2+ HP from a single attack triggers when *any* segment marks 2+ HP from a single attack.

#### Adjacent segments

Note which segments are adjacent. PCs climbing on the colossus can only traverse adjacent segments.

### Building Colossi as a Puzzle

Make a colossus more than a bag of HP. Some segments only attackable after others are Destroyed. Each segment with its own thresholds. A parameter satisfied before the colossus can be hit at all. Maybe the colossus only takes Major damage. Maybe it's only harmed by a certain weapon, requiring the party to hunt it down before the fight. Lean on passive features. Start simple and ramp up.

### Running Colossus Fights

#### Scale

Use a standard form of measurement (feet, meters — whatever your table likes) to talk about a colossus's scale. Normal range bands still apply for actions, but expressing height/width with recognizable sizes helps the table understand the colossus they're facing.

#### Moving PCs around a colossus

PCs are expected to scale the colossus. Use these guidelines instead of normal movement:

- PCs can climb onto each segment unless its stat block says otherwise.
- Once on a segment, they can usually move around that segment without additional rolls.
- To move to an adjacent segment, the PC makes the appropriate action roll (usually Agility or Strength) to leap onto it.

#### Teamwork

While fighting a colossus, PCs may initiate any number of **Tag Team Rolls**.

#### Using a colossus in battle

On your GM turn, treat each segment as its own adversary when moving the spotlight. Spotlight a segment as your GM move; spend a Fear to spotlight an additional segment as you would in a regular fight. Some colossi have actions in the framework — spotlight the framework to use that action as you would a segment, but highlight the colossus as a whole in the narrative. You can still spotlight additional segments on your turn.

While a PC is on a segment of the colossus:

- They have advantage on attacks against that segment.
- The segment's standard attack cannot be used on that PC (e.g., the colossus can't grab a PC riding on its hand). The colossus would need to use a different segment to attack them. Any feature that allows a segment to attack a PC currently on it will say so.

Any feature that would pull or push a normal adversary can't move a colossus; instead it pulls or pushes the PC making the attack. Any time an effect targets the colossus, use the targeted segment's Difficulty. If targeting the colossus collectively, use the highest Difficulty among segments. If a PC's feature targets multiple adversaries, it can target multiple segments.

Typically, no additional adversaries are needed in a colossus fight — colossi are powerful enough alone. If you do add others, think about their relationship to the colossus (an aberration? a faction using the colossus for their own purposes?). Keep additional adversaries limited and purposeful.

Give the characters info about how to take down a colossus, and consider how creatively that info can be delivered. Part of the hunt phase should include the PCs tracking down knowledge before facing it; seed rumors from townspeople, then provide payoff when the rumors prove true. Examine the colossus's stat block and plan ways to give clues using color, light, and shape — describe heavy armor surrounding a delicate blue light pulsing in the chest's core, and they'll know where the weak point is.

#### Colossus Maps

If you want to use maps and minis to maintain spatial awareness of a colossus fight, build the basic shape using **notecards** — one card per segment. When a PC climbs onto a segment, place their mini directly on the notecard. This setup helps everyone remember PC positions and which segments are adjacent.

### Leveling Up

Upon defeating each colossus, the soul shard in that location shatters and the PCs immediately level up as a reward. After defeating all nine colossi, the PCs are at level 10 and ready to take on Kudamat as their last hunt.

### Emergence of Kudamat

Track the power Kudamat is siphoning. Copy these trackers into your notebook:

```
Siphoning Track: ▣ ☐ ☐ ☐ ☐ ☐ ☐ ☐ ☐ ☐
Kudamat's Power: ___
```

The Siphoning Track starts with one slot marked. Kudamat's Power starts at 0. When the PCs take a [[Short Rest]] during the hunt phase, mark the Siphoning Track once. When they take a [[Long Rest]] during the hunt phase, mark it twice. **Each time a colossus is defeated, or when the Siphoning Track is full**, roll as many d12s as marked slots to determine how much power the soul shard gives back to Kudamat. Increase Kudamat's Power by the **highest value rolled**, then reset the Siphoning Track to 1.

Once **all nine colossi have been slain or his Power reaches 100**, Kudamat emerges from Godfell Mountain. Build him as a Colossus adversary with a **Severe threshold equal to Kudamat's Power** and a **Major threshold equal to half that value (rounded up)**.

## SESSION ZERO QUESTIONS

- Is your character native to the Drylands, or did they come from elsewhere? How have they been treated by the locals and incoming prospectors?
- What is your character's favorite thing about the geography of the Drylands? Do they have a notable landmark they're drawn to?
- Since the rise of the colossi, who or what has your character lost, and how did that event change them?
- Some believe that, despite the prophecy, Kudamat will bring prosperity to mortals when he rises. Does your character believe this? How do they interact with the people who do?
- Describe an outpost your character visited in the past. What did they love about it? What worried them?
- What terrors has your character seen roaming the desert now that Kudamat is raising ever more aberrations?
- What themes are you most interested in exploring with your characters, and how might they manifest in some of the colossi?
