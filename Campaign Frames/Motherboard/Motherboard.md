## THE PITCH

Among the ruins of a fallen world, the great walled cities of the **Echo Vale** stand as bastions of hope in an otherwise hostile land. They draw on technology powered by a mysterious language that runs through metal threads tethered high above the valley. Beyond the walls, the **Wastes** are roamed by autonomous machines and great wandering cities scouring for scrap. When a malicious virus infects the machine beasts that work alongside the Vale's people, the once-docile creations turn violent. PCs play extraordinary heroes leaving the safety of the walled cities to travel the Wastes and bring peace to the Vale once more.

## OVERVIEW

The Echo Vale is named for the **echoes** — the remains of a fallen civilization whose ancient technology its people now repurpose. Hundreds of years after that civilization's mysterious annihilation, steel and concrete have given way to plants and animals. Grasses crack the ancient roads, trees grow in derelict skyscrapers, and history forgot the names of the figures depicted in crumbling statues. Those who built homes in the husks of the once-great cities muddle through the powerful programs and machines left behind.

Today, formidable settlements like **Argent** rise within decaying ruins, their walls keeping violence out and workers in. **Carrowcroft Walkaway** and other **wandering cities** travel the valley on all manner of vehicles — towns atop lumbering machines.

Above the valley arches the **Network**: a dense web of glimmering metal wire that shepherds information and communication between the disparate cities. The valley's inhabitants have used it for ages, but the complex language used to create and modify it was lost when the previous civilization fell.

### Remnants

Throughout the Echo Vale roam autonomous machines of the fallen world called **remnants**. They come in two forms: some resemble animals; others were designed for a function (defenders, builders, watchers, domestic laborers). Some live within cities and are considered **tame**; others roam **wild** in the Wastes, often territorial and hunting for energy. Some are large enough to carry entire wandering cities on their backs; others are small scrap-creatures that serve no apparent purpose beyond supplying parts.

Remnants gain energy from a variety of sources. Tame remnants connect to the Networks within cities. Wild ones must do so by photosynthesis, killing other remnants for power stores, or hunting people to siphon energy. They act like wild animals — focused on the next meal. Rumors say some can harvest information from the brains of the creatures they kill.

### The Wastes and the Network

The **Wastes** are the overgrown, hostile lands between cities — wild remnants and ancient ruins. Most citizens spend their lives within walled or wandering cities, but needs arise: city defenders stop wild-remnant attacks, messengers run between cities to repair the Network, hunting parties hunt for food.

In the Wastes, travelers and remnants alike access the Network through **free data wells** — slender towers reaching up to the Network's lines, with ports at the base. Many data wells exist within walled cities, inaccessible to outsiders. The free wells in the Wastes become hotbeds of violence as wild remnants treat them like watering holes for power and information. The **Pilgrims of the Spire** are known to value and protect free data wells as sacred places connected to the Motherboard.

### The Motherboard

The tallest spire at the center of the Echo Vale is the **Motherboard** — origin of all Network threads. No one has entered the daunting tower; each city has its own beliefs about its ancient power. Most citizens revere the presence within as a god, believing it was worshipped by the fallen civilization and carries forward its wisdom. Others believe it's a "master program" left by the technomancers of the fallen world, as fallible as any other remnant. Though the Motherboard doesn't speak directly, she shepherds all data and power through the Network. Without her operating system, the Vale's technology would eventually fail. Some technomancers and pilgrims claim to speak to the Motherboard in the machine language **kohd**.

### Technomancers and Machinists

**Technomancers** are the datacrafters who use ancient machines for new purposes. Historians work to decipher kohd; practitioners work directly with remnants and other technology to gain a deeper understanding of the language. Some skip the study of kohd in favor of just using machines for power. Most have a general grasp of how to operate technology even without understanding it — but because the tech isn't fully understood, technomancers can manage only temporary repairs on remnants.

**Machinists** focus on physical aspects of remnants. Many work in factories or scrap works, repairing gears, wires, and processors. Some can cobble new remnants together from spare parts — but only by using old remnant cores from the fallen world to give them life. Stories of wild remnants creating more of their own kind are mostly considered traveler's myth.

### The Remnant's Fury

A recent disaster, the **Remnant's Fury**, was a virus spread through the Network that caused remnants to attack in the dead of night. Machines that had worked peacefully in homes and businesses assailed hundreds in walled and wandering cities; in the Wastes, travelers found themselves facing particularly violent wild-remnant packs. Autonomous machines have always posed dangers — working remnants short out, wild remnants kill for energy — but this was different. This was a massacre.

More remnants are turning violent. Those under the Network must figure out what to do before the Echo Vale meets the same fate as the fallen world. No one knows the source of the virus, but for it to be this widespread, there's growing certainty it must somehow involve the Motherboard.

## COMMUNITIES

All [[Communities]] are available; some carry a particular shape in this campaign.

- **[[Loreborne]]** — Many devoted to the history of the fallen world and its technology. Could be experts in mechanical workings, technomancers attempting kohd, or academics studying the fallen civilization. Highly sought after wherever they go.
- **[[Orderborne]]** — Most belong to a group focused on protecting a larger community. They might defend a walled or wandering city, or work as contractors hunting and scrapping remnants in the Wastes. Skills are sold for housing, scrap parts, or profit.
- **[[Ridgeborne]]** — The mountains bordering the valley support the spider-like web of the Network, making peaks neutral territory. All cities contribute citizens to the **Tower Watch** — the independent group that tends towers and wires and protects the Network from wildlife and weather. Ridgeborne could belong to the Tower Watch, or to smaller wandering/walled cities perched on the cliffs for safety.
- **[[Wanderborne]]** — May be **Pilgrims of the Spire**, devotees who leave the cities to traverse the Wastes and connect to the "voice of the Mother" at base camps surrounding the central spire. Or citizens of a roaming city — small homes to massive metropolises that move through the Wastes. Wandering cities eschew walls in favor of mobility. Some are built on mechanicals piloted by drivers; others ride atop giant remnants. After the Remnant's Fury, the cities atop remnants are in a dangerous position: lose pacification, lose home and community.
- **[[Underborne]]** — The fallen ones dug labyrinthine buildings underground. Underborne characters are often from communities that avoid the surface in dark twisting passages below. They protect their few aboveground entry points fiercely; old farming remnants operating wild are a particular threat.

> [!session-zero]+ Sample background questions
> - *(Loreborne)* You discovered something about the fallen ones you've never shared. What secret do you keep?
> - *(Orderborne)* You have a trick for catching and scrapping wild remnants that city officials pay handsomely for. What is it, and who taught it to you?
> - *(Ridgeborne)* There is a secret that only the Tower Watch knows. What is it?
> - *(Wanderborne, Pilgrim)* You're certain you once heard the voice of the Motherboard. What did she tell you?
> - *(Wanderborne, City)* You were part of the team that cares for your city's giant remnant. What do you know about its personality that others don't?
> - *(Underborne)* Members of your community have a unique way of dispatching remnants underground. How?

## ANCESTRIES

Some [[Ancestries]] are not available in a Motherboard campaign.

- **[[Clank]]** — *Unavailable.* All autonomous robotic beings are remnants — unique forms, but none have sapience.
- **Magical ancestries** — Some ancestries (e.g., [[Drakona]]'s Elemental Breath) have inherent magical features. Adapt the flavor of each feature to a world where magic comes from technology.

## CLASSES

All [[Classes]] are available with these notes:

- **All classes with spellcasting** — Magic doesn't exist; spellcasting characters use technology to carry out their abilities. Some come to technomancy through years of study; others feel an innate call. Decide what tools, processes, or systems your character uses and how their tech mirrors traditional spellcraft.
- **[[Sorcerer]] & [[Wizard]]** — Sorcerers are biohackers incorporating tech into their physical form; wizards tinker with what they find, combining programs and machines into something new.
- **[[Druid]]** — When transforming into a Beastform, it wraps around the druid as a mechanized suit that can enhance senses, change movement, or grant unique abilities. Can resemble Echo Vale remnants or living animals.
- **Beastbound [[Ranger]]** — Companion can be a living creature (rare and valuable in cities) or a remnant.
- **[[Seraph]]** — Generally worship the Motherboard as their god, treat kohd as a holy language, and treat technology as power imparted by faith. Others believe in many gods called the **Faint Divinities** living within the Network. Decide why your character relates to technology spiritually rather than just employing it.
- **[[Warrior]]** — The "Combat Training" feature is replaced with **Ikonis Training**: *You start with one Force augment already crafted and installed in your Ikonis.* (Ikonis is detailed in Campaign Mechanics below.)

> [!optional]+ Optional Rule: Beastbound Companion Corruption Track
> If a Beastbound ranger has a remnant companion, they can use these rules to make the remnant subject to the same threat of corruption from the Remnant's Fury.
>
> Add a Corruption track to the Companion sheet. At the start of downtime, the ranger rolls their Fear Die. If it lands on a 1, mark a Corruption and describe the temporary glitch overcoming their companion.
>
> Specialized technomancers may temporarily repair the remnant (clearing 1d4 Corruption), but the only way to truly save them is to stop the malicious virus coming from the Motherboard. If the last Corruption is ever marked, the remnant becomes violent and can no longer be commanded or repaired. The ranger must take a new companion with two new Experiences, carrying over the same number of level-up options already chosen.

## PLAYER PRINCIPLES

- **Replace magic with technology.** When your character casts spells, consider how the effect manifests in this world. Each weapon use ties to repurposed and rebuilt fallen-world tech.
  - Does your character wear gauntlets with a screen for scrolling through their grimoire? Does their staff crackle with electricity? How do they store information from the Network when disconnected? Do they use discs, drives, or plugs?
- **Contrast the technological with the emotional.** The Vale is filled with exciting tech and unique adaptations, but also with people experiencing hardship, fear, and love. Each interaction with new people and technology — consider how your character feels and act on emotional response.
- **Create a shared language.** The fallen ones' lexicon is incredibly advanced and obscure. Make a shared vocabulary at the table for the technology your characters use, deciding what they understand vs. don't. Their people may use words descended from the original but with different meaning. They may have ascribed spiritual value to mundane objects, or use once-significant items for mundane tasks.

## GM PRINCIPLES

- **Fill the world with the ruins of technology.** The fallen ones' innovation was so advanced the Vale's people molded their lives around it without rebuilding the systems that gave the past world its power. Show ruins of state-of-the-art buildings and machines, weathered by centuries of nature, with the less-advanced modern world built among them. Decide what led to the past civilization's downfall and use that to flavor what they left behind. Reflavor Daggerheart adversaries as remnants — circuits and steel instead of flesh.
- **Make the details fantastical and the conflicts grounded.** Fantastical technology causing large-scale problems is great; each confrontation should center the struggles of its people (cultural, political, spiritual). Use technology as window dressing for grounded conflict. A remnant abilities or city aspects can act as allegory for the party's emotional experiences.
- **Pit tradition against progress.** Two distilled schools of thought run through the Vale: those who reject the fallen ones' tech to define themselves anew, and those who believe they must dig further into the past to harness those powerful machines. How do these viewpoints come into conflict? How do the political/social conflicts affect characters personally?

## DISTINCTIONS

### Technomancy

Imagine explaining modern technology to our distant ancestors — it would seem like magic. That's how fallen-world tech should be engaged with and described by Echo Vale people. Reading the ancient language of kohd and pressing buttons on screens to perform supernatural feats is impossible without the Network. The ways technomancers use technology are limited only by your imagination.

### The Fallen World

The fallen ones developed the systems that probably destroyed them. The world they left is rich and obscure: shimmering wire Network overhead, steel skyscrapers, concrete buildings, machines with their own consciousness. Rivers pass through cities; buildings fill with new growth; ancient monitors hang on walls like panes of shattered glass.

### Scrap Operations

The modern world is built on the ruins of an ancient civilization — it relies on the **scrap** they left behind. There's a unique economy and vocabulary around it.

**Salvagers** venture into the Wastes to unearth ancient tech and hunt remnants for parts. Survivors earn considerable commission depending on what they bring back and where they sell it. Some cities only want replacement parts for their remnants; others search for artifacts of historical significance.

Within cities, **scrappers** process and sell parts for mechanical repair and technomancy. **Scrap shacks** are the (often illegal) businesses run by individuals; some cities require all machine parts to pass through government hands first.

People who work with remnants or practice technomancy use varied terms — **mechanists** and **scrap hackers** repair or rebuild machines; **techs** are technomancers working with datacraft.

### Quantum

**Quantum** is the Echo Vale's currency. A person's quantum is generally stored on a small black disc that can be inserted into devices throughout the valley. People often decorate and personalize their discs to distinguish them.

### Network Tethers

When wandering cities and travelers want Network access in the Wastes, they throw large hooks called **tethers** over the wires above, allowing them to charge tech and collect data from anywhere. Some technomancers can intercept Network messages with tethers — but it requires specialized knowledge and equipment.

More ambitious or desperate individuals — **skimmers** — climb up to the lines and use smaller hooks or contraptions to slide along the Network cables, gathering data and traversing the Wastes from above.

### Wandering Cities

The wandering cities testify to the Vale's ingenuity. Most precariously balance atop a remnant or specially crafted vehicle piloted by a single driver or team. They come in all shapes and often have collections of smaller crafts traveling with them for hunting, defense, or expansion.

#### Carrowcroft Walkaway

One of the largest. Layers of buildings crowded atop a walking machine, guns jutting out from its turtle-like form. Recently dissatisfied with sending small **Croft Walkers** to pick on less-protected towns and travelers, leaders have tried something more ambitious.

For decades they've eyed the abundance of tech and scrap behind Argent's walls, recently launching a number of attacks. Argent's guard has held them off — at considerable loss of life on both sides.

With Argent thrown into violence by the Remnant's Fury, politicians worry the Croft Walkers will return while the city is vulnerable. There's a rumor that Carrowcroft somehow infiltrated the Motherboard and initiated the virus to harvest scrap from the other settlements one by one. Since they use only operator-driven mechanicals, they have a considerable advantage until someone reaches the Motherboard. A Wastes rumor: although Carrowcroft doesn't use remnants for labor, they're harvesting their cores for unique uses no other technomancer has matched.

#### Other example wandering cities

- **The May Trader Caravan** — a collection of smaller vehicles. Travels the Wastes hunting wild remnants for scrap and trading with anyone with quantum or rare tech. Considered especially brave: no walls, no tall walker. Their caravan sits low to the ground; they sacrifice strong defenses for speed and maneuverability.
- **The Library** — built atop a large spider-like remnant. A prime example of insular Vale culture: residents aren't interested in adapting and developing new tech as much as protecting items and information from the fallen world.
- **Nereidus** — a long centipede-like ship with hundreds of oars that snakes through the water off the Echo Vale's coast. Very communal; instead of a single leader, they elect a new **Mouth of the People** each season.

### Walled Cities

Most stationary cities were built into ancient ruins, with new walls around their borders. Some use geographical formations and ancient structures for defense; others rapidly develop innovative defense systems.

#### Argent

Long the focus of valley-wide attention. Best-preserved skyscrapers, best fortifications, the largest collection of tame remnants in the Echo Vale. Heavily stratified, also a bastion of wealth. A strictly regimented guard protects streets and borders; a prison labor system keeps government scrap operations running 24/7.

After the Remnant's Fury, **the Priv** (Argent's leader) sent messengers to all walled cities to try to unify and uncover a solution. There's talk of organizing an attempt to enter the Motherboard's tower — which would violate many of the most devout orders in the Vale.

#### Other example walled cities

- **Calcarus** — a single ancient skyscraper, now filled with its own small forest. Citizens have grafted wooden buildings onto the tower's exterior, using Calcarus's height to keep safe from remnants.
- **The Northern Oracle** — a small village hosting a massive amphitheater filled with screens. The High Oracle claims she can use the screens to speak to the Motherboard, but only her most devout followers have ever claimed to see it.
- **Switch Withawick** — entirely underground; citizens' eyes are sensitive to light because they rarely surface. Two prominent rumors: the citizens are cannibals (unlikely), and they know how to create new remnant consciousnesses (possible).

#### Designing your own city

Useful starting questions:

- Does this city wander? If so, what is it built on? If not, what do its walls or fortifications look like?
- What does this place look like? What architectural elements are from the fallen world? What portions are new?
- In what unique ways have the people used fallen-world scraps and technologies?
- How do the citizens feel about remnants? The Motherboard?
- What cultural custom is unique to this city?
- What do these people want that they don't have? What do they have to offer in trade?

## THE INCITING INCIDENT

After the outbreak of the Remnant's Fury, Argent's city guard scrambled to subdue the violent machines. Wave after wave succumbed to the virus; politicians grew desperate to avoid widespread fear.

**Priv Maelor Rhodek** (orderborne elf, she/her), Argent's charismatic leader, sent a messenger to assemble a team with the skill and experience to handle remnants, go undercover as civilians in the **machinists' Build Sector**, and figure out what's causing the attacks.

Priv Rhodek gives her word that any scrap the party acquires while protecting the city is theirs to keep. In return, she asks they collect as many **remnant cores** as possible so her technomancers can diagnose the cause of the violence. She also gives the party a surprising contact to see before they head into the scrap factory where many attacks have occurred.

The contact is **Mac the Scrap Jack** (wanderborne faun, he/him), who runs an illegal scrap operation — making him an unlikely friend for the very person whose laws outlaw his work. On the Priv's word, Mac gives the party a **Hotspot**: an illegal device carried as a backpack that, when set down and activated, stalls out nearby remnants for a few moments.

He also offers a deal: save one of the recovered remnant cores and bring it to him instead of Rhodek. He'll pay them a few quantum, and they'll get to look at the data from the core to try to see what's "causing all this ruckus with the rems."

Will the party have compassion for the once-tame remnants, or dispose of them indiscriminately? Will they talk to factory workers to learn more about the Fury, or operate like a cleanup crew with their eye on the scrap? Will they bring the Priv all the cores they collect, or save one for Mac and risk trouble with the city guard? If they perform well, will Priv Rhodek tell them more about the Remnant's Fury and the Motherboard's involvement?

> [!gm-aid]+ Hotspot
> *(Consumable)* Activate to make all remnants within Very Close range Restrained.
> **Scrap to craft:** 5 triggers, 8 wires, 9 copper, 6 fuses, 3 relays, 1 battery.
>
> As the party defeats violent remnants in the factory, give each PC one **remnant core** alongside the standard scrap rewards.

## CAMPAIGN MECHANICS

### Tech Damage

In a Motherboard campaign, **magic damage** is instead called **tech damage**. It's caused by technomancy: a piercing shrill, a concussive blast, a blinding flash, swarming nanobots, a drone emitting a high-powered plasma beam, or any other product of supremely advanced technology.

### Ikonis

Characters don't have access to Daggerheart's normal selection of primary and secondary weapons in this campaign.

Instead, each receives a personalized weapon called an **Ikonis** — a special weapon given to citizens of the Echo Vale when they come of age. It starts as a simple rod of metal designed to receive modifications as the bearer ages, shaped to their needs. It can evolve into many weapon types — a staff with webs of colorful wire wrapped around its hilt, an ornate bow humming with energy, a heavy hammer for the forge, a spear with knobs and triggers for different attachments, anything that suits the wielder.

Players design their character's Ikonis using the Motherboard module sheet (available on the [Daggerheart downloads page](https://www.daggerheart.com/downloads)). Each player takes a copy and slides it under the right side of their character sheet, makes selections about trait, range, and damage in the top section, then names and describes their weapon. Record the weapon's details in the Primary Weapon slot.

Ikonis are always two-handed and start with the **Bonded** feature:

> **Bonded:** Gain a bonus to your damage rolls equal to your level.

#### Augments

Ikonis allow PCs to install **augments**. A character's Ikonis starts with **2 Augment slots** at Tier 1 (showing it has evolved past its most basic form), gaining **1 additional slot per subsequent tier**. Augments can be crafted as a downtime move when PCs have the scrap.

An Ikonis starts with no augments installed. A character may build as many augments as they like, but can only install up to their available Augment slots. A level 1 PC with three augments built can install only two; at level 2 they can install all three. While installed, augments are treated as additional features for that weapon. PCs may freely swap installed augments during downtime.

The Motherboard character sheet module includes a base augment selection; GMs are encouraged to design more using the provided options as a template.

### Armor

Characters have access to Daggerheart's normal [[Armor|armor selection]], reflavored to taste within the campaign setting.

### Network Tether

Each PC adds a **Network tether** to their inventory at character creation. Tethers are typically large hooks attached to a data cable — toss the hook onto an overhead wire to charge tech and gain Network access while moving through the Wastes. **PCs must have access to the Network (via a tether or other means) to perform downtime moves.**

### Crafting & Trading

Gold is not used as currency in this frame. Instead, PCs gather **scrap** to trade for **quantum**. The Motherboard character sheet module tracks current scrap and quantum. **All PCs start with 5 quantum.**

| Quantum | Equivalent |

| 100 quantum | 1 bag of gold |
| 1000 quantum | 1 chest of gold |

#### Gathering Scrap

When PCs defeat remnant adversaries, they can collect scrap after combat. Three categories, each with an associated die size:

- **Shards (d6)**, **Metals (d8)**, **Components (d10)**.

Each player rolls the dice the GM determines. Each die's result is compared to the relevant scrap-category table. Each die produces one piece of scrap. If a result falls between two listed values, take the lower-numbered option.

> Tip: think of the numbers as thresholds — when you reach that threshold, you get that scrap.

#### Relics

An additional scrap category, found only on specific remnant adversaries. When PCs defeat a particularly important or powerful remnant, you might grant each a **Relic** alongside scrap. Customize Relics based on the adversary — *steelhorn of the stag*, *lens of the flickering falcon*, *jaw piston of the cryodrake*, *gold scales of the scrappersnap*. Relics are worth **20 quantum** and are often used to craft powerful augments.

Provide opportunities for PCs to seek specific Shards, Metals, Components, or Relics through NPC contracts, hunting particular adversaries, exploring areas, or visiting scrap shacks.

#### Crafting with Scrap

PCs can use collected scrap to craft augments for their Ikonis and other items. When they have enough scrap to build an augment, they use a downtime move and spend the appropriate scrap. When an augment calls for a generic Relic, any Relic the PC has can be spent.

Some augments have prerequisites called **precompiles** — when the precompile is met, the augment can be crafted. Base augments have tier-based precompiles, but a precompile can be anything: a specific trait or Evasion value, a class or domain, etc. Augments can always be broken back down into their scrap parts.

You're encouraged to give PCs additional tasks or challenges that use scrap — building a specific machine for a task, collecting scrap for an ally trying to repair a remnant, etc.

#### Selling Scrap for Quantum

Local **scrap shacks** in each city generally trade scrap for quantum and quantum for scrap. By default, each piece of scrap is worth its value on the roll table (whether buying or selling). Each shop has a daily stock of **1d10 of each Shard, 1d8 of each Metal, 1d6 of each Component**. When PCs want to buy, roll to determine stock. Values can change at GM discretion.

> Example: A PC sells 2 circuits and 1 battery (3 quantum each × 2 + 10 = 16 quantum); also 2 lenses and 6 coils (5 × 2 + 2 × 6 = 22 quantum). They now have 38 quantum. They want 1 crystal and 2 silver to craft an augment — the shack has stock; pay 6 for the crystal and 12 (6 × 2) for silver = 18 quantum. They leave with 20 quantum, 1 crystal, and 2 silver.

### Writing in Kohd

*Language designed by Chris Willett.*

**Kohd** is the language of the fallen world's technology. If you'd like players to learn to decipher and write it over a campaign, deliver them messages early on from the Motherboard that they can't read, have technomancers reveal pieces of the language as they progress, and finally give them tools to fully read and write it by the climax.

Kohd is a **node-based glyph language**. Each "word" is a circuit that flows from a **charge** node (start) to a **ground** node (end). Words are constructed on a **3×3 grid of nine nodes**, eight of which contain three letters each (ABC, DEF, GHI, JKL, MNO, PQR, STU, VWX) plus one with two (YZ).

Each word starts at a charge node, is traced through nodes containing the word's letters in order, and ends at a ground node. **Subnodes** (dots) on each trace line indicate which letter within the node is meant — one dot for the first letter, two for the second, three for the third. To avoid ambiguity when revisiting the same node, draw a **ring** around or within that node and connect to it. **Null nodes** in remote corners disambiguate words that would otherwise occupy too few grid positions.

Sentences begin with a **coupler** (a series of solder-points where multiple sentence circuits could fuse) and continue by connecting one word's ground to the next word's charge. Logical operators have shortened forms — `and`, `or`, `true/is`, `false/not`, `because/since`, `so/thus/causing`, `if`, `if/then`, `there is`, `unique/exactly one`. Pronouns (`I`, `you`, `we`, `you all`, `them`, `their`) and articles (`the`, `a`) also have shortened, non-nodal forms; articles modify the charge node of their word.

Punctuation attaches to the ground of the final word: period, exclamation point, question mark. Commas (which mean the circuit continues) cross the trace between words.

Full glyph diagrams, the lexicon of shortened forms, and the worked example of *"The Network carries communication from city to city."* are in the source PDF — the system is visual and best read there.

## SESSION ZERO QUESTIONS

- What is your character's relationship with remnants? How do they rely on them?
- How did your character use technology or technomancy in their daily life before the Remnant's Fury? What, if anything, has changed since that catastrophic event?
- Describe a structure built by the fallen civilization that your character has seen before. What do you think it was originally used for? What is it used for now?
- How do remnants physically change as they begin to deteriorate into violence?
- Describe a wandering city your character once visited. What was unique about it?
