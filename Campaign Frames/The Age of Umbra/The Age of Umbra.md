# THE AGE OF UMBRA

> In a dying world immersed in relentless shadow and nightmarish monsters, small communities hold fast to the light, hoping those who endure can find a way to save this broken realm before all is lost to darkness.

**Designed by:** Matthew Mercer
**Complexity:** ••• (3/5)

## TONE & FEEL

Ancient · Daunting · Epic · Grim · Ominous · Terrifying · Tragic

## THEMES

Apocalypse · Corruption · Darkness vs. Light · Hope · Redemption · Survival

- *Dark Souls*

## THE PITCH

A century has passed since the God-King **Othedias** betrayed the Pantheon, drawing divine punishment and abandonment that left the **Halcyon Domain** shattered, desolate, and eternally cursed. In this shadow-choked land of decaying fortresses and dreadful monstrosities, surviving communities cling to hope in the face of lightless oblivion. The PCs are heroic survivors who defend their community from the terrors beyond their walls, venture into the unknown to hunt nightmares twisting from each fallen soul, seek answers to the curses corrupting the world, and perhaps find a way to change its fate.

## OVERVIEW

The Age of Umbra is a dark and broken age within the Halcyon Domain. Grand cities and cathedrals to the **Veiled Gods** crumble into ruin, the prosperous epoch of God-King Othedias' rule reduced to wreckage. Othedias — chosen by the Veiled Gods as the bridge to divinity — conspired with his **Grand Ordinants** and **Aetherlords** to alter the celestial balance. The Pantheon answered with cataclysmic retribution, ending the Old World, then abandoned the realm in the event called the **Apostasy**. The skies have darkened with each generation since.

The cycle of the soul has been sundered. Every mortal now bears the **soul blight** — upon death, they rise as a corrupted revenant bent on destroying everything they once loved. If their risen body is destroyed, the marked soul withdraws into the **Umbra** — a dark, ethereal mass — to seek others to haunt and corrupt.

The survivors of the Halcyon Domain call themselves the **Enduring**. They huddle in reinforced battlements where **Sacred Pyres** burn as beacons of hope and safety. From a young age, all Enduring train to survive and defend; many become fierce warriors and **Aetherweavers** by adulthood. Hunters brave the cracked wilds daily for sustenance, materials, and contact with other settlements.

Many wandering beasts have mutated from Umbra corruption — a transient storm of twisted souls that merges with creatures and contorts them into nightmare monstrosities. When these monsters slay the people who confront the wilds, their victims' blighted souls fuse into the horrors that killed them. With the gods absent, dreams turn ominous and empty; oracles glimpse great alien minds reaching from beyond the cosmic veil.

### Key Settlements

- **Okros** — A fort village of about fifty years' standing, in the swampy flood plains of the **Idol Hollows**. Walls of melted shields and stone surround a central **Sacred Pyre** whose flames climb above the tallest ramparts. A recent seismic event opened the **Shalk Chasm**, exposing buried Old World structures; brave Enduring slowly explore them while scouts seek allies from places like Amber Reach.
- **Amber Reach** — Once-prosperous capital nestled against the **Griefcleft Mountains** at the edge of the Idol Hollows, dividing it from the tainted jungles of **Aveidoora**. Now fallen and haunted: gothic towers and fortresses lie damaged, household ruins shelter horrors and looters. In the **Ashcall Quarter**, the **Celsian Athenaeum** — a college and library turned stronghold — still holds. Hundreds of Enduring there work to reclaim the streets while the rising **Damask Queens** (hedonic raiders out of the **Cinnabar Quarter**) push back.

For all the despondency, hope sparks. Whispers of remnant divine power lingering in lost shrines that can rebuke the soul blight. Heroes felling greater evils and reclaiming ancient relics. Spirited leaders rallying the Enduring to expand walls and avenge their dead. Zealot factions seeking to commune with the absent gods and repent for Othedias' crimes. Aetherweavers seeing visions of distant uncorrupted fanes. Hermetic societies claiming tomes of prophecy. The lands are dark, but the points of light burn ever brighter.

## COMMUNITIES

All [[Communities]] are available; many take a particular shape in this dying world.

- **[[Highborne]] and [[Slyborne]]** — Some communities carved out a surviving section of an Old World cityscape; others built new townships near resource-rich locations. Highborne are extremely rare — often the ghost of an Old World political power, or a newly forged tyranny built on stolen resources. Slyborne thrive alongside influence and wealth: working for highborne factions, scouting between settlements, trading rumors, or running roguish societies like the Damask Queens.
- **[[Loreborne]] and [[Orderborne]]** — Many cities and centers of higher learning are now ruled by cults, monstrous entities, or complete ruin. Loreborne and orderborne characters hail from a crumbling bastion of Old World academia, upholding aspects of lost knowledge in their community. Many factions remain obsessed with discovering truths of the ancient Grand Ordinants and Aetherlords.
- **[[Ridgeborne]]** — The mountain ranges are imposing and cold, prone to extended winds that carve rock into spires. Ancient wood-and-stone bridges across mountain passes are deeply important and well-protected. Ridgeborne are scouts, hunters, or trade sentinels. Some villages keep vigil over mountainside tombs and Old World knowledge sites.
- **[[Seaborne]]** — Coastal cities along the Halcyon Domain's shores live between the dangerous waters of the **Ashen Seas** and the encroaching shadow on land. Important hubs of commerce and resources, with mighty fishmongers and sailors fighting waves of deadly sea threats. Often more carefree than other communities, taking inspiration from their mercurial waters.
- **[[Underborne]]** — Many subterranean societies exist, but only some have made surface contact since the Apostasy. Some half-buried cavernous metropolises fight underground terrors; others build new in vacant ruins. With the Shalk Chasm's appearance, some underborne communities have surfaced for the first time since the world's fall.
- **[[Wildborne]]** — Sprawling haunted woodlands hide vibrant glades of natural aether, where bioluminescent flora gives signs of solace or magical protection. Wildborne communities live symbiotically with the rare uncorrupted pockets of nature, or are made of lost soldiers and survivors the wild spirits protect.
- **[[Wanderborne]]** — Either flee destruction to start anew, uproot frequently for fear of attracting horrors, or band together with common purpose, where braving the chaos doubles as a quest for answers.

> [!session-zero]+ Sample background questions
> - *(Highborne)* How did your bloodline accumulate enough resources or reputation to carve influence within your community? Among the upper echelon, who do you trust most, and least?
> - *(Slyborne)* You've built a rapport with a highborne figure. Who are they, and do you trust them — or are they an easy mark? You were part of a botched job recently; what happened, and who was at fault?
> - *(Loreborne/Orderborne)* What secret about the Old World have you been entrusted with or discovered? What heirloom do you carry, and what's its significance? What did you leave behind that still haunts you?
> - *(Ridgeborne)* Who is a childhood friend or rival who left a year ago and was never seen again? What mountain predator's call fills you with abject fear?
> - *(Seaborne)* What is the one tale about the depths that still makes you nervous? When you were young you swam deep and encountered something unexpected — what was it?
> - *(Underborne)* When you're alone in quiet places, what sound do you believe you hear? What place beneath your community does everyone know to avoid — and what happened when you snuck in as a kid?
> - *(Wildborne)* Which nature spirit does your community rely on for protection? What early signs of corruption are you worried about?
> - *(Wanderborne)* Why does your community keep moving on? What do some fear is following them?

## ANCESTRIES

All [[Ancestries]] are available. Consider how the harsher setting and environment intermingle with the existing ancestries to inspire dark, weird, unique interpretations.

- **[[Clank]]** — Often carved from stone and iron, originally developed as vessels for holy spirits and messengers. Many stopped functioning and became statues when the gods left.
- **[[Ribbet]]** — Large, often 6–8 feet tall, preferring to skulk on all fours when not leaping or standing still.
- **[[Drakona]]** — Taller and more reptilian in physicality and proportions. They lean forward as they move, using their tails for balance.
- **[[Faerie]]** — More sensitive to the soul blight than other ancestries, causing their physical appearance to develop in jagged and monstrous ways.

## CLASSES

All [[Classes]] are available. Setting-specific notes:

- **[[Bard]], [[Druid]], [[Rogue]], [[Ranger]], [[Sorcerer]], [[Wizard]]** — The forces of magic flowing unseen through the world are called the **Aetherweave**, and the rare few who can manipulate it are **Aetherweavers**. The art is hard to teach and rarely wielded instinctively. With many secrets lost to the previous age, an Aetherweaver is met with awe, fear, or mistrust. Players should consider the responsibility their character carries with these gifts and how brazenly they use them.
- **[[Splendor]] domain** — Since the gods' abandonment and the fall of the Grand Ordinants, divine power and healing rituals are a fading art. Some see Splendor users as hope and foist sudden worship onto them; others see them as targets for hate against the absent gods. Seraphs and wizards displaying Splendor abilities should consider how openly to use them.

## PLAYER PRINCIPLES

- **Treat community as a means of survival.** You were raised in a world of despair, taught to rage against the dying of the light. Forging and protecting bonds — close and far — surmounts most other goals. If the Enduring can't work together, all is lost.
- **Remember that not all darkness is evil.** Most shadow here is born from tragedy. Many threats have a sad story that put them on this path. With the right inquisitiveness and creativity, you can often parlay with — even redeem — what hunts you. Many lost to the dark still retain something to appeal to.
- **Embrace lethality.** This is a realm of hardship, challenge, and loss; that's what allows for momentous revelations and incomparable victories. Few groups carry the same faces from start to finish. Find joy in the risks; relish epic, memorable character deaths; excitedly create new characters to meet the others at the next encampment.
- **Consider your role.** Backstory defines your previous role and where you honed your skills — hunter, scavenger, philosopher, storyteller, oracle, scoundrel, leader, demagogue, bandit. Do you continue that role, or yearn to reinvent yourself in the dying light?

## GM PRINCIPLES

- **Relish in the omnipresent threat.** Set the scene for each ominous locale beyond the walls. Solemn colors, dark beauty of crumbled antiquity, perpetual sense that nowhere is safe. Play with shadows, noises, monstrous shapes on the horizon — even when the PCs aren't in immediate danger. When a real conflict comes, exemplify the harrowing with baleful descriptions.
- **Shine light in the darkness often.** Even amid bleak atmosphere and tragic narrative, highlight chances for hope, joy, and victory. Balance terror with locales of untainted beauty, moments of inspiring humanity, chances to truly make a difference. The highest highs make room for the most memorable lows, and vice versa.
- **Emphasize compassion amidst hardship.** When a society faces annihilation, communities come together in passionate and wonderful ways. Create story moments where the downtrodden find courage and rise. Let the people who fight alongside the PCs be vulnerable; let them share their fears and aspirations.
- **Create many goals across many minds.** Survival breeds deeply conflicted personalities — altruistic to selfish, sometimes both. Some communities want sanctuary; some embrace chaos to indulge worst impulses; some place hope in ancient secrets; some are driven by sorrow and vengeance. Even the terrible beasts have a wish and a story.
- **Build combat as a test, a puzzle, and a journey.** Sometimes a grand throwdown is thrilling. But much that's monstrous became so through corruption, guilt, exploitation, or circumstance. Bring pathos and humanity to some threats — encourage alternative victory paths beyond slaughter. If the players develop a unique theory on how to cleanse, befriend, or absolve a foe, follow it.

## DISTINCTIONS

### Eternal Gloom and Volatile Weather

Endless gloom engulfs the skies. Nights are pitch; even noon is a dreary haze. Heavy rains and erratic weather break the somber grays. Cursed regions host never-ending storms of vibrant hues. Even underground caverns can host strange weather phenomena.

The gloom only breaks over hallowed grounds or on mysterious, unpredictable days, revealing golden sunbeams or gossamer moonlight that grants esoteric boons (or curses). Some Enduring believe the highest mountain peaks pierce the gloom, offering possible new homelands free of turmoil.

### Death Becomes a Curse

The places not protected by Sacred Pyres are vulnerable to the **Umbra** — the flow of malevolent unholy spirits clinging to lightless spaces. This amorphous necrotic energy moves through the Halcyon Domain like a dark wind, transient but with unknowable intelligence. It calls to the souls of the dead like a mother to its children, drawing them into its shape.

The Umbra is a swirling, wailing black mist that invokes delirium and despair at its threshold while physically corrupting and transforming the bodies of those who surrender to it. Many of the horrifying monsters in these lands have been changed by the Umbra — left with part of its mass like a foul seed inside them, mutating the host and perpetuating its expansion through wanton destruction. Those who live with the Umbra inside them are a shade of their former selves: extreme emotion, fractured memory, twisted form warped with the features of others lost to the Umbra. Whispers spread of those cleansed of the Umbra's touch — but for most, they remain only whispers.

### The Rarity of Respite

Wherever the gloom touches, danger lurks. Ancient evils thrive; warped soul-blighted spirits corrupt what they can. Persisting requires the rare scattered places of safety: where a **Sacred Pyre** burns. These hallowed fires were lit by the waning conclave of Ordinants before they vanished, and their flames are one of the few wonders that keep the Umbra at bay (see Campaign Mechanics).

The Enduring formed communities around many Pyres and kindle them daily for fear of them dying out. Smaller consecrated embers are said to persist undiscovered in distant places, in danger of going cold. Even still — not all evil is repelled by the Pyres' light.

### The Shadow of Society

The Old World was one of grand castle fortresses, sprawling cityscapes, and imposing temples. The Veiled Gods' wrath toppled most of that extravagance. Surviving remnants of the old ways struggle to reclaim a forgotten glory while most survivors lead grueling lives across splintered communities. The tattered tapestry of heritage and history is fading with the light, yet the Enduring forge new culture through hardship, determination, and ambition — picking through the debris of what was in hopes of building what will be.

### Magic Is Suspicious

Aetherweaving was once paramount to the Halcyon Domain's prosperity. Now, even though rune-marked arms and enchanted relics of the Old World are considered boons, the Aetherweave is widely believed to have caused the Veiled Gods' wrath — and thus the Age of Umbra.

Much of the world is now suffused in dark magic that saturates the wilds, chaotic and corrupting, wielded by those who worship the gloom or yield entirely to it. Superstitious disdain for spellcraft is pervasive; tales of Aetherweaver hubris are taught as fables to dissuade youth. Places harboring lingering magic are instinctively shunned. While Aetherweaving can protect and preserve, it's deeply mistrusted — careless use can lead to confrontation or exile.

## THE INCITING INCIDENT

Recently, one of Okros's hunting bands — led by **Huntmaster Evrados** (ridgeborne giant, he/him) — delved into the **Shalk Chasm** for resources. They found an eroded, forgotten temple to an obscure Veiled God. Evrados was caught by a monster and dragged into the temple's depths. The remaining three hunters tried to follow but a terrible odor forced them to withdraw.

Back at the settlement, **Elder Akadona** (wanderborne orc, she/her) and the village speakers debated the response. Their dispute was interrupted — by Evrados's voice screaming for help from beyond the camp's walls. Scouts couldn't see him in the murky swamp, but the hunting band rushed out from the Pyre's light to his aid. The screams mercifully halted for a time.

Then they broke out again — Evrados's lament joined by the wails of his hunters.

The denizens of Okros are now terrified, the unsettling din heightening their agitation. The shrieking doesn't change or wane, exemplifying its unearthly nature. Desperate, Elder Akadona has gathered the party to investigate what happened to Evrados and his crew — save them if possible, end their suffering if not.

The gates open onto the forlorn willows and mist-choked wetland valley. What monster is responsible? How does it steal the voices of those missing? What is its connection to the uncovered temple — and if it recoils into its lair, what dangers wait inside? Are Evrados and his team beyond rescue? What other macabre revelations might require reaching out to another settlement?

## CAMPAIGN MECHANICS

### The Strength of Hate

The Umbra can seep into all manner of beings, twisting them into a malformed mockery of their former selves with preternatural strength.

Adversaries and environments in this campaign can take an additional type: **Umbra-Touched**. When an adversary is Umbra-Touched, describe how the Umbra has warped their essence and form. **All Umbra-Touched adversaries critically succeed on attacks against a PC on a die roll of 19–20.**

As the PCs gain [[Scar|scars]], they themselves become slowly corrupted. **When a PC deals damage, they deal extra damage equal to their number of scars.** When a PC marks their last [[Hope]] slot with a scar, they don't retire — they succumb to the Umbra's influence and charge forever into the dark to join the terrors stalking the lightless places.

### Fiery Beacons

Every surviving settlement huddles around a **Sacred Pyre** — a vibrantly burning flame sparked by a **Blessed Branch** and continuously kindled by those given the mantle of **Pyrekeeper**. Blessed Branches are a rare and hallowed artifact carried as reliquaries by those who wish to expand the reach of the Enduring and found new encampments.

If the characters acquire a Blessed Branch, they can reignite an extinguished Sacred Pyre or create a new one as a bastion of warmth and safety.

> [!relic]+ BLESSED BRANCH
> An ashen relic from the Old World that carries a splinter of divinity. When ignited, all Enduring present gain 3 [[Hope]], and the flame it emits repels all but the most powerful monsters from its immediate vicinity as long as it burns. Only a bit of kindling must be provided daily to maintain this slow-burning blaze.

### Lurking Darkness

The **Lurking Darkness** represents the ever-present dangers of the Umbra. When the party finishes a [[Short Rest]] or [[Long Rest]] **while not in the vicinity of a Sacred Pyre**, roll a d12 for the Lurking Darkness:

| Roll | Effect |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Describe how something monstrous found them in the dark. An adversary of the GM's choice initiates conflict. |
| 3–5 | Describe how something terrifying stalks nearby. **Gain 2 [[Fear]].** |
| 6–9 | Describe how the imposing darkness intensifies. **Gain a [[Fear]].** |
| 10–11 | Describe how the characters rest undisturbed. *No effect.* |
| 12 | Describe how the characters stumble upon a hopeful omen. **Each PC gains a [[Hope]].** |

With danger always present, parties have access to an additional downtime move:

> [!downtime]+ Keep Watch
> Describe how you stay vigilant against what lurks beyond your camp. When the GM makes a Lurking Darkness roll at the end of downtime, you roll your Hope Die and choose whether to replace their roll with yours. If multiple players choose this move, take the highest die roll.

### Soul Blight

If a humanoid NPC or any PC dies, the GM can spend a [[Fear]] to immediately raise them as a Shambling Zombie — or **2 Fear** to raise them as a Brawny Zombie — who tirelessly seeks to kill everyone they once cared for. Once their zombie form is destroyed, the victim's blighted soul withdraws and joins the Umbra. Their features can appear on future Umbra-Touched adversaries.

## SESSION ZERO QUESTIONS

- If members of the party hail from different communities, why did they leave, and what called them to this particular settlement? Do their home communities still exist? What rumors and mysteries surround them?
- Are any characters burdened with burgeoning oracle sight? If so, what visions have begun creeping into their sleep?
- What do your characters see when they stare into the inky void of night too long?
- How deadly does the group wish this campaign to be? Would you enjoy a possible churn of many character deaths across a campaign, or a more balanced flow where danger is real but not stacked so hard against you?
