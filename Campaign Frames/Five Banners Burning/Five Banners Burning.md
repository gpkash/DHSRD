## THE PITCH

Five years into a fragile armistice that ended a decade of war, the continent of **Althas** teeters on the edge of renewed conflict. Five nations — bound by a brittle web of alliances and grudges — race to consolidate power amid a moment of rapid magical, political, and social change. Each nation deploys agents to strengthen alliances, sabotage foes, and tilt the balance their way. The PCs are heroes caught in the escalating intrigue, navigating competing loyalties, generational hatreds, and opportunistic villains.

## OVERVIEW

Althas was once a single nation called **Voldaen**, founded after the gods departed. Over centuries it fractured:

- **Voldaen** — proud monarchy with traditional authority and inherited wealth, recently shaken by the loss of its rebellious province. Old ally of Hilltop, ideological rival of Polaris.
- **Hilltop** — a wealthy theocracy that monopolizes access to the gods, with shrines and influence inside every other nation's settlements down to village level. Old ally of Voldaen, unfriendly with Armada and Jesthaen.
- **Polaris** — a progress-focused magocracy at the dawn of a magical industrial revolution. Ideological rival of Voldaen; ally-of-convenience with Jesthaen.
- **Armada** — a federation of city-states founded by pirates-turned-merchants. Pursues recognition through naval supremacy. Allied with Jesthaen, disliked by Voldaen and Hilltop.
- **Jesthaen** — the youngest nation, a republic that broke from Voldaen fifteen years ago in a bloody revolt. Hungry to prove itself militarily. Allied with Armada, ally-of-convenience with Polaris.

Open hostilities ended five years ago with an armistice, but the peace is tenuous. Jesthaen's emergence has redrawn the lines; the Polaris/Armada/Jesthaen alliance is fragile; both Voldaen and Jesthaen carry unfinished business from the war. Border clashes and trade disputes threaten to escalate at any time. Most believe war is coming again — only when and how is in question.

The campaign leans toward a nuanced moral landscape, with morality and ethics in tension with duty and allegiance. Heroes may do terrible things that weigh on them; villains are often motivated by love and loyalty as much as ambition or vengeance.

## COMMUNITIES

All [[Communities]] are available; some carry particular weight in this campaign.

- **[[Highborne]] and [[Orderborne]]** — Most strongly associated with the centers of power. Highborne characters are likely tied to a ruling class; orderborne (most common in Hilltop and Voldaen) face the test of having their codes broken or vindicated by the conflict.
- **[[Ridgeborne]], [[Underborne]], [[Wildborne]]** — Common across this mountainous continent. These communities often feel more kinship with each other across borders than with their lowland neighbors. Wildborne are rare and often founded by people fleeing larger conflicts.
- **[[Loreborne]]** — Most prominent in Polaris (mages, engineers, architects), but exist in every nation: priests in Hilltop, sages in Voldaen, etc.
- **[[Seaborne]]** — Common across coastal Althas (rare in landlocked Polaris). The continent's best sailors and navigators, torn between tide-to-tide life and wartime duty.
- **[[Slyborne]]** — Found everywhere but most prominent in Jesthaen and Armada, where criminal organizations have shaped national fortunes (smugglers backing the Jesthaen revolution; the pirate families that founded Armada).
- **[[Wanderborne]]** — Tied to no single region. Even those who consider themselves outside national authority will struggle to stay clear of the conflict.

> [!session-zero]+ Sample background questions
> - *(Highborne)* Are you a member of the aristocracy/nobility, or the scion of a prominent merchant or priest family?
> - *(Orderborne)* What lines are you willing to cross to protect the people you love?
> - *(Ridgeborne)* Who from your community was conscripted in past conflicts, and what tales do they tell?
> - *(Loreborne)* What secrets does your community protect or control? What impact would those secrets have on the burgeoning conflict?
> - *(Seaborne)* What do you fear will happen if conflict comes to your community by land?
> - *(Slyborne)* What is your community's relationship to its nation? How are they using political instability to their advantage?
> - *(Wanderborne)* How has the growing conflict altered your community's travels?

## PLAYER PRINCIPLES

- **Make your actions reflect or refute your allegiances.** You're implicated in the actions of those you serve; consider your character's loyalty, and let every action either reinforce an ideal or push back against it. Build a character arc with a clear distinction between where they stood and where they end up.
- **Stick to your principles or break them for good reasons.** Decide where your lines are and what would make you cross them. Share that struggle with the party.
- **Take small actions that have big implications.** Push back against the overwhelming forces straining the continent. Build the future you want one step at a time — a small gesture for you might mean the world to someone else.
- **Grapple with the impact of your actions on everyday people.** Treat every person as important. Foster hope where fear has bred wrath or despair. If your character becomes a symbol others look up to, decide whether they embody the heroism or reject the lionizing.

## GM PRINCIPLES

- **Force them to choose between their loyalties.** Put allies at cross purposes. Make it impossible to keep everyone happy; force the cost of compromise.
- **Entangle them in a web of old grudges and new schemes.** Show people motivated by inherited ideology and personal ambition. Highlight the rising tensions; dramatize the breaking points.
- **Put the party in the crucible of history.** Stage dramatic moments through drastic action — create a chance for compromise, then fill the room with NPCs willing to do anything to stop it. Position the party as the catalyst at every fulcrum.
- **Zoom in and out to show the far-reaching impact of decisions.** Trace the line from an individual's motivation to its continent-wide consequence. Make actions reverberate like falling dominos.

## DISTINCTIONS

### A World on the Edge

The setting is on the verge of open war. The conflict will be fought in ballrooms and back alleys as much as in castles and valleys. People from every walk of life will be caught in it despite their best efforts.

### Tipping the Scales of History

The party occupies a unique position to influence the conflict. Keep them connected to the major players, factions, leaders, and catalysts so they're always in the thick of it.

### Big Questions

Themes to keep in front of the table each session:

- Does power always corrupt?
- In what ways is the road to disaster paved with good intentions?
- What does just governance look like?
- How do you decide who to protect and uplift with limited resources?
- How do you manage divided loyalties without betraying someone — including yourself?

### Collateral Damage

As conflict escalates, monsters are displaced from their ecosystems, the natural and magical balance of Althas falters, and people become refugees within or beyond their nation's borders.

## THE INCITING INCIDENT

**Clover of Towers** (orderborne firbolg, they/them), child of Jesthaen revolutionary hero **Adamant Towers** (orderborne firbolg, she/her), was kidnapped en route to a political marriage with **Enchanter Kalashae** (loreborne orc, she/her), a rising figure in Polaris's High Circle.

Polaris distrusts Jesthaen's claim of kidnapping and demands the marriage proceed. Voldaen denies all involvement and dispatches a diplomatic investigation to **Lightning Valley** — a border town protected by huge magical lightning rods, famous as the site of the talks that ended the war. Days later, reports describe a "disaster" during the initial Voldaen/Jesthaen meetings there.

The party is sent to Lightning Valley to investigate. They'll find representatives from every nation doing the same:

- **Oziver of Storms** (ridgeborne simiah, he/him), sheriff of Lightning Valley — overwhelmed but trying to keep the peace. The last person seen with Clover was one of his guards, still missing; Oziver is convinced the guard isn't to blame.
- **Cerulean Prism** (loreborne clank, he/him), apprentice Mage-Inquisitor of Polaris — quiet and diligent, secretly in love with Clover after months of correspondence; will take reckless action to find them.
- **Kostren, Seeker of Hilltop** (orderborne halfling, she/her) — a formidable presence taking over the local temple as her base, conscripting clergy for a divination ritual. Loudly claims a vision proving Polaris's foul play. She's exaggerating to stoke conflict so Hilltop can claim more fertile land.
- **Zeshthon, County Judge of Voldaen** (highborne dwarf, they/them) — elderly, with a compelling silence others fill. Volunteered for the inquiry to visit family; secretly an art collector hoping to procure embargoed Goledraelle tapestries — which could expose the merchant as a spy.
- **Goledraelle, merchant of Armada** (seaborne elf, he/him) — charming, hapless-seeming textiles merchant. The image of incompetence is a cover; he's an Armadan spy dispatched to ensure the marriage happens.
- **Clover of Towers** themself — wanting to make their mother proud and find happiness far from home. Last seen drinking with city guards at the Three Poles tavern. Carries a sense of inferiority about being too young to have fought in the war.

The original investigators arrived two days after Clover vanished and rode east into the hills; they haven't been heard from in over a week. Did they meet the same fate? What townsfolk helped the kidnappers? Which of Jesthaen's, Polaris's, or Armada's enemies were positioned to sabotage the marriage? Did Jesthaen sabotage it themselves? Did some danger draw Clover from the town, or was this their own escape from their mother's expectations? Which second-wave investigator finds them first — and which version of the story spreads like wildfire?

## CAMPAIGN MECHANICS

### Faction Relationships

Use a campaign sheet to track relationships, assets, and problems for each nation. Each pair of nations has a relationship rating from **Nemeses (−3)** to **Close Allies (+3)**. The intensity sets how far a nation will go for or against another, even at risk to itself: Friendly (+1) renders help when easy; Nemeses (−3) goes out of its way to harm.

| Nation | Relationships |
|---|---|
| **Armada** | Allies (+2) Jesthaen · Friendly (+1) Polaris · Unfriendly (−1) Voldaen · Enemies (−2) Hilltop |
| **Hilltop** | Allies (+2) Voldaen · Enemies (−2) Armada · Enemies (−2) Jesthaen · Nemeses (−3) Polaris |
| **Jesthaen** | Allies (+2) Armada · Friendly (+1) Polaris · Enemies (−2) Hilltop · Nemeses (−3) Voldaen |
| **Polaris** | Friendly (+1) Armada · Friendly (+1) Jesthaen · Enemies (−2) Voldaen · Nemeses (−3) Hilltop |
| **Voldaen** | Allies (+2) Hilltop · Unfriendly (−1) Armada · Enemies (−2) Polaris · Nemeses (−3) Jesthaen |

> [!gm-prep]+ Per-nation assets, problems, and objectives
>
> **Armada** — Powerful navy; superior maps of currents; allied water monsters. *Problems:* small land area, limited overland access, lingering pirate reputation. *Major:* friendly port infrastructure in Voldaen and Polaris. *Minor:* acquire airship tech, deploy privateers.
>
> **Hilltop** — Wealth from tithes; devout army; priests and seraphs; divine power; influence through temples. *Problems:* surrounded by adversaries, negligible navy, food scarcity. *Major:* bring Armada back into the fold and redeem them. *Minor:* beseech the gods for a bountiful harvest, frame Polaris as backers of a Fallen cult.
>
> **Jesthaen** — Strong land army; natural resources; arable land. *Problems:* political instability, residual pro-Voldaen elements. *Major:* take revenge on Voldaen. *Minor:* lock in trade with Armada and Polaris, take local temples from Hilltop.
>
> **Polaris** — Skilled mages; magitech innovations; airships; magical creatures. *Problems:* labor shortage, scarce raw materials. *Major:* perfect magical servitor enchantments. *Minor:* secure new mines and quarries, foster immigration through job programs.
>
> **Voldaen** — Traditional authority; seemingly boundless wealth. *Problems:* major settlements close to large borders with Polaris and Jesthaen; political upheaval after the revolution. *Major:* reclaim key resources from Jesthaen without restarting the war. *Minor:* turn Armada against its allies, steal magical talent and tech from Polaris.

### Objective Countdowns

Use long-term [[Countdowns]] to track each nation's pursuit of its goals.

- **Each in-game week**, pick one countdown per nation and step it down by one. Countdowns can also tick up or down based on the fiction.
- A completed countdown represents a nation gaining an asset, removing a problem, developing new tech, or shifting political fortunes. When one completes, pick a new objective from the fiction and start a fresh countdown.
- Each nation runs **one major countdown** (10 steps) and **up to two minor countdowns** (4–6 steps each, depending on scale).
- **Stagger them** so only one or two nations complete in the same week — triggering all five at once leaves a tension lull afterward.
- The party may be dispatched to complete or block these objectives; completed countdowns generate major narrative shifts for them to respond to.

## SESSION ZERO QUESTIONS

- Should all the PCs come from and/or have allegiance to the same nation? Why are they allied and traveling together?
- How big of a role will war play in this campaign compared to diplomacy, intrigue, or mystery?
- How much conflict do we want between PCs? What safety tools will we use to negotiate those conflicts when they arise?
