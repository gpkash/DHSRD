# REIGN OF THE WEREDRAGON

*In a land ruled by a violent weredragon tyrant, heroes ordained by the stars to defeat her must rise to the challenge and defend their people.*

Designed by Jeremy Crawford, Rowan Hall, Christopher Perkins, and Spenser Starke

**Complexity Rating: ••**

## THE PITCH

*Read this section to your players to introduce them to the campaign.*

Once upon a time, in the peaceful land of Whelvia, a powerful sorceress named Forlorne Lykona deceived the country's great dragon warden to absorb his power and rule the kingdom herself. Transformed into a weredragon by the powerful ritual that fused the warden's power with her cold heart, Forlorne now takes to the skies, feasting on all who challenge her and cursing innocent villagers to become ravenous weredrakes. The people called on the brave defenders of the realm, the Knights of Whelvia, to protect them—but the knights instead bent the knee to the draconic tyrant, allowing her to command their forces and leaving the land without protectors.

In a Reign of the Weredragon campaign, you play destined heroes of Whelvia, rising up against Forlorne Lykona and her knights to save this once-idyllic land from the jaws of tyranny.

## OVERVIEW

*If your group decides to play this campaign, give your players the following information before character creation.*

In the land of **Whelvia**, some people still tell stories of the constellations that illuminate the sky. Among them, dragons made of stars appear each night to guide travelers, deliver counsel, and perhaps answer the prayers of those who call their names in times of need. That is, after all, how the first dragon constellation **Whelvarax** came to be.

Long ago, in the time of Whelvarax (she/her), from whom the country of Whelvia would one day derive its name, the land was in ruins from decades of invasions. Borders were drawn and redrawn as foreign armies came to conquer the verdant forests, azure coasts, and high mountain peaks. At the time, Whelvarax ignored the people's plight. The great dragon was annoyed by the ruckus and destruction but would long outlive the silly conflicts of smaller creatures, so she had no desire to wade in.

But the **Elder Druids** were desperate to stop the fighting. With great love for the land and reverence for the dragon, this order made an appeal: "Great Whelvarax, we beg your aid in driving the invaders from your beautiful home. We know a way to raise mortals to godhood through worship. If you help us, the people will serve you, and we can found a nation that protects your land and exemplifies your wisdom. We will make you a god among the stars—an **Ascendant**."

Whelvarax was intrigued by this offer and did as the druids asked. Using her powerful, fiery breath, she drove out the armies and assembled the people to rebuild the territory. She taught the Elder Druids and their people how to grow more fertile farms, create more just systems of government, and defend themselves with more fortitude. Over the decades, the land became known for its dragon defender, and people began to call themselves **Whelvians.**

After over two hundred years of prosperity, Whelvarax reached the end of her life, and the druids made good on their offer. Because her people were so loyal and she was so beloved, when the old dragon came to pass through the veil of death, the druids were able to perform a ritual that called on the gods in the Hallows Above, asking them to raise the beloved leader to divinity. Through the druids' ritual, Whelvarax ascended into the sky to become a constellation. In subsequent years, people began to look up at night, searching for guidance and trusting that their former leader would light their way.

Seven dragon wardens followed. Each ruled the land of Whelvia with unique powers, personality, and wisdom. Some were benevolent, others ruled with fear, but only the most powerful ascended to the stars to become Faint Divinities.

During these long years, some dragons sought to expand Whelvia's borders, others to grow the country's wealth, and still others to protect its natural resources. The Elder Druids who once saved the land died off, fading into the mists of history. In their stead, the **Knights of Whelvia** rose as sworn defenders of the realm. Valorous and true, this band protected Whelvians and embodied the hope for a prosperous and just nation. Like the druids before them, they were bound to the dragon warden through their common goal: the safety and prosperity of the people of Whelvia.

![[reign-of-the-weredragon-p130-1882.png|500]]

For the last hundred years, the warden of Whelvia has been a purple-scaled dragon named **Sazanthe** (he/him), who made his home in **Vexstone Abbey** overlooking the sea. He was known for his vast knowledge of magic, and visitors came from faraway places to confer with him. Sazanthe hoped to fortify his people with knowledge and curiosity, ushering in an era of peace and prosperity where everyone, even those who came from beyond Whelvia, could enjoy the beautiful and abundant land they shared.

Like many wardens before him, Sazanthe took a non-dragon companion. Warden companions were meant to support Whelvians, aid their dragon, and live out the harmonious ideals of the druids who once allied with the great dragon Whelvarax.

**Forlorne Lykona** (she/her) did not embody these characteristics. An orphaned faun sorceress and cult leader, she was more likely to inspire Sazanthe's suspicion than his intellect. But she could read the stars, perhaps better than anyone else in Whelvia. In them, she saw Sazanthe's future: that he would become the next Ascendant. Desiring this power for herself, she came to Sazanthe promising to share an abundance of magic with Whelvia should Sazanthe partner with her.

Sazanthe, for all his wisdom, was taken in by the sorceress. He was compelled by her incredible talent, and she capitalized on this. When the pair undertook the ritual to bond, she instead twisted the magic and absorbed the great dragon into herself. As Forlorne and Sazanthe became one, the dragon's shock and horror triggered a release of magical energy that tore apart the abbey and killed most of the spectators, transforming them into ghosts who are trapped in the ruins.

In the months since that night, Forlorne has ruled Whelvia as a tyrannical **weredragon** queen, able to transform at will between two forms: her own faun body and Sazanthe's stolen dragon body. Through her absorption of Sazanthe, she intends to use the worship of the people of Whelvia to become an Ascendant and achieve the power of divinity as so many of the dragon wardens before her did. Where Sazanthe spent his time as warden bettering the lives of his people, Forlorne intends to subjugate Whelvians and use their fear to enhance her power.

She withdrew to **Nwarlyth**, her fortress of stone on the region's tallest mountain, where she uses the Knights of Whelvia to carry out her tyranny. Under her orders, they've turned their forces from protecting against external threats to controlling the Whelvian people. Those valorous few who once swore to protect the realm now bend the knee to Forlorne, claiming that following her ways will "keep the peace."

Meanwhile, Forlorne sends soldiers to imprison those who disagree with her rulings, feeds upon anyone she feels threatened by, and uses her dragon's breath to transform dissenters into **weredrakes**—vicious, bestial predators. Sazanthe refused to use this unique power against all but his gravest of foes, but Forlorne has embraced it to create a loyal army.

Forlorne is desperate to ensure she does all that is necessary to ascend to the stars and become a god upon her death, but ever since she absorbed Sazanthe and seized his power, the constellations she used to look to for guidance—especially the stars of the great dragon Whelvarax—have given her nothing but silence.

Now, she sends riders in all directions to collect **sky seers**, those who can read the constellations, to provide her counsel in place of her own absent ability. But the tyrannical queen has a tendency to kill seers who give her answers she doesn't like, leading those with true talent to disappear into hiding and false prophets to flock to her fortress. There is a rumor among sky seers, whispered in only the most secret places, that unexpected and powerful heroes have arisen in Whelvia, and they will defeat the weredragon queen.

This land needs protectors—but it won't be the Elder Druids, a dragon, or the Knights of Whelvia riding to the rescue. Instead, it will be a band of adventurers destined by the stars to play a part in the fall of Forlorne Lykona. You are those adventurers.

## COMMUNITIES

*All communities are available, but some have unique aspects in a Reign of the Weredragon campaign. As needed, provide the following information to your players and choose one or more of the questions to ask them during your session zero.*

### Highborne

Prior to Whelvia's descent into tyranny, highborne Whelvians coexisted relatively peacefully with the peasantry while enjoying the prestige and privilege afforded by their status and wealth. Now, many highborne households cater to Forlorne's will and whims to gain power. For most, this isn't motivated by a belief that the queen's decisions are just, or even beneficial to Whelvia, but rather the desire to use her reign to grow their coffers and improve their station.

- What ties do you have to the Knights of Whelvia, and how did their betrayal of the people affect you?
- You are the rightful owner of a valuable parcel of land. How has Forlorne's tyranny placed your holdings in peril?
- You are closely allied with another noble house. Which family is it, and how has this alliance been affected by recent political turmoil?

### Loreborne

Although Whelvia has no large academic institutions, the presence of dragons and other knowledgeable individuals has fostered smaller enclaves of sages, scholars, sky seers, and other truth-seekers. During Sazanthe's rule, there was a great push to cultivate and maintain these collectives, fostering many loreborne communities to grow in numbers and influence. But ever since Forlorne claimed the throne of Whelvia, loreborne communities have become targets. The queen sends her knights and their militias to collect or destroy grimoires, capture sky seers, and imprison prominent scholars who then face corrupt trials that result in them being turned into weredrakes.

- You were punished for warning others of the danger Forelorne poses to Whelvia. What was done to you, and how did you escape?
- What do you know about Whelvia that no one else knows?
- You protect a small piece of knowledge. What is it, and how do you believe the queen and her militia would react to learning it?

### Orderborne

New orderborne communities are forming, both in support and in defiance of Forlorne's rule. These groups range from sects of a larger whole—such as political supporters or opponents—to entire settlements.

As Forlorne has gained power, her forces have swelled. The Knights of Whelvia control militias that carry out the queen's orders. These groups were once trained to fight foreign enemies and keep the peace, but now they're ordered to hunt and harm their fellow citizens for simply disagreeing with the beliefs of the queen.

- You were once given an order that you defied. What was the order, and what were the consequences?
- When Forlorne's forces came, your community submitted to her rule. Why did they do this, and how did you react?
- You protect someone in your community. Who is it, and why are you concerned for their safety?

### Reborne

As the queen has gained more power and forced more of her subjects to fear for their lives, increasing numbers of communities are yielding to her rule. Those who refuse to bow alongside their community might leave it instead, either to preserve their ideals or save their own lives. People may find that their community disavows them if their political and moral beliefs don't align with Forlorne's proclamations. Reborne may come from families, villages, or even entire regions of Whelvia that claim to support the queen's regime— either as true believers or people too afraid to oppose the weredragon.

- Someone in your family sold out a neighbor to Forlorne's forces. What was the neighbor's crime, and what consequences did they face?
- The last words you heard from your family when you left will haunt you forever. What did they say?
- Someone you love is a dedicated follower of Forlorne. Who are they, and what did they do that let you know it was time to leave them behind?

![[reign-of-the-weredragon-p132-1894.png|500]]

### Slyborne

The Knights of Whelvia have always worked to stop ne'er-do-wells across the country. But now, with the knights turning their backs on the people in favor of Forlorne's rule, some career criminals are reorienting themselves as well. Once-apolitical slyborne communities have become champions of the downtrodden, vigilantes with axes to grind against the weredragon, or even rebel organizations making moves to unseat the queen.

- What did you witness that prompted you to start using your criminal skills to go against the queen's regime?
- You have a contact inside Forlorne's court. Who is it, and why have they told you they want to fight Forlorne?
- Since the rise of the queen, your community has begun smuggling something through parts of Whelvia. What is it, and why must this effort remain secretive?

Some slyborne could be current or former members of Forlorne's network of spies. The queen commonly sends individuals to infiltrate groups she believes oppose her. These spies might sabotage freedom fighters, tip off the knights, or even turn in rebels to face Forlorne's corrupt court system.

- You've spent most of your life indoctrinated by Forlorne's lies. What broke you out, and who are you now running from because of it?
- You infiltrated a rebel group and learned critical information about their plans. What did you do with this knowledge?
- You agreed to become a spy for the queen because her forces are holding someone you love captive. Who is it, and what deal did they make with you?

### Underborne

Beneath the verdant forests, rolling hills, and rugged peaks of Whelvia lie rich subterranean dens and enclaves, many unknown to the queen and her spies. Enemies of the weredragon can find sanctuary here and avail themselves of tunnel networks that are often safer to travel than the roads above.

The magic portals that permit access to these underground sanctuaries are invisible to outsiders and require special keys to step through. Members of underborne communities can always sense the nature of a portal, which tells them what key is needed to pass through it. Portal keys can take many forms, such as seeds, berries, leaves from a particular kind of tree, passwords, musical notes, or objects of a certain scent or hue.

- Your people have contemplated opening the caverns to surface dwellers to help protect them from the queen. What stance have you taken and why?
- One of the Knights of Whelvia and their militia have taken control of the underground warren you call home. What information does your community keep secret from them, and what consequences could there be?
- What is the nature of one of the portals that leads to your community? What do you keep with you that allows you to enter?

### Warborne

Since Forlorne's absorption of Sazanthe and her takeover of the country months ago, many peaceful, prosperous parts of Whelvia have fallen into destruction and tragedy. Warborne individuals are often from small villages that reside on lands regularly fought over by the Knights of Whelvia and rebel forces. Others come from the warfront itself, their lives transformed by the fight for the soul of Whelvia, no matter which side of the conflict they've fallen on.

- What role have you played in the current conflict so far? What circumstances led to your involvement?
- Someone you fought alongside lost their life. Who were they, and what did they say to you before they died?
- You once protected someone on the other side of the conflict. Who were they, and why did you save their life?

### Wildborne

The woodlands of Whelvia are resource rich, which has made them highly sought-after by both Forlorne's forces and local rebels, and they've become dangerous territory as a result. Forests held by the Knights of Whelvia are filled with militia encampments that decimate the land through hunting, foraging, and deforestation. In response, many wildborne are joining rebel groups in an effort to drive out Forlorne's soldiers, using their knowledge of the landscape to build traps and orchestrate attacks from the canopies above and the underbrush below.

- What sacred place was destroyed by the conflict moving into your forest? How has your community reacted?
- In your forest there lives a plant or creature that is particularly dangerous to those unfamiliar with it. What is it, and what makes it deadly?
- Your home was taken over by one of the queen's militias. What have you often overheard troops saying to one another?

## TRANSFORMATIONS

*All transformations are available, but some have unique aspects in a Reign of the Weredragon campaign. As needed, provide the following information to your players.*

### Werewolves & Shapeshifters

Because the queen uses her power as a weredragon to control the people of Whelvia, even forcibly transforming those that oppose her into weredrakes, anyone who can shift their form may be treated with suspicion. People might assume those who can transform have that power due to association with Forlorne and her terrifying magic. Others with loved ones who were transformed into weredrakes might turn to people who can shift their forms, hoping to learn ways to help family and friends who fell victim to Forlorne's magic.

## CLASSES

*All classes are available, but some have unique aspects in a Reign of the Weredragon campaign. As needed, provide the following information to your players.*

### Druids

While the order of the Elder Druids that founded Whelvia no longer exists, many in this land still treat druids with reverence. In particular, villages and small communities that still believe in the Ascendants are more likely to treat druids like minor celebrities, with locals asking to see displays of magic or receive healing. This has more to do with their title as "druids" than any specific powers, and other classes that utilize the Sage and Arcana domains are less likely to receive similar attentions.

The ancient druids left behind large stones carved with runes that defy attempts to decipher them. Druids in modern Whelvia might be interested in finding these monoliths, studying them, and harnessing their power. There's hope among those who study the ancients and believe in their ways that the stones can be utilized to conjure the ancient druids' protective wards, which could be especially potent in the fight against Forlorne and her weredrakes.

Like werewolves and shapeshifters, druids might be treated with suspicion if they shift into their Beastform around Whelvians who fear Forlorne, her weredragon form, and the weredrakes she creates. Those who still hold reverence for druids might instead seek out these practitioners for this exact reason: hoping they might have the power to transform their weredrake loved ones back into their original form.

### Guardians

The Knights of Whelvia have long been the protectors of the realm. Some guardians held childhood dreams of becoming knights, going so far as to learn all the proper rituals of initiation, only to see their beloved heroes forsake their oaths and bow to a tyrant.

A Stalwart guardian might take it upon themself to restore the honor of the Knights of Whelvia, perhaps with the help of knights who have fallen but aren't beyond redemption. A Vengeance guardian might strive to destroy the fallen Knights of Whelvia, judging them as unworthy of the trust placed in them.

### Seraphs

While once a seraph who worshipped the Ascendants might have been viewed as traditional, or even rustic, this has changed since Forlorne's rise to power. By the weredragon queen's decree, the worship of higher powers other than the Ascendants is now illegal in Whelvia. Now a seraph must consider the divine being they serve and what that being has come to symbolize in this new regime.

## PLAYER PRINCIPLES

*If your group decides to play this campaign, give your players the following information before character creation.*

### Embrace Your Destiny

All the PCs in this campaign have a fate that is intertwined with Forlorne Lykona. Consider how your character came to know this—perhaps they received some kind of astral omen, they were born under strange stars, or they have a constellation-shaped birthmark. Who is the sky seer who read your character's fate? What unique detail did the seer tell your character about their destiny?

### Heal and Unite the Realm

The people of this land remember the idyllic realm Whelvia was before Forlorne Lykona became queen. When building your character, consider what drives them to hope that the realm can be restored and how that affects the way they interact with others. Though they might differ in tactics, attitude, and levels of dedication, all PCs in this campaign should have—or aim to find—a reason to believe in hope for Whelvia despite its current state.

### Make It Personal

Strive to make the destruction of Whelvia have a personal impact on your character's life. What have Forlorne and her subordinates done to set your character on their current path? How has her reign of terror changed what is most important to them? What specific event or moment made your character decide to take the fight to the weredragon tyrant and join forces with other like-minded heroes?

### Remember Who the Villain Is

Forlorne Lykona is an evil that corrupts all that's good in Whelvia. She turns innocents into weredrakes. She compels the Knights of Whelvia to enforce her vile decrees. She frightens her subjects into submission while rewarding those who spy and snitch on their neighbors. But as your characters encounter her minions, be aware that not all of them are irredeemably evil or in complete control of their actions. Some might even help the PCs under the right circumstances.

The seeds of Forlorne's destruction are scattered throughout Whelvia, waiting to be found. Discover how the tyrant came to be. Learn her weaknesses from those who know her best. Search for cracks in her armor. Forge alliances. Obtain magic that can counter her sorcerous power or break her hold over the lesser evils of the realm.

## GM PRINCIPLES

*Keep the following guidance in mind while you GM this campaign.*

### Make Tyranny Omnipresent

No matter what area of Whelvia the PCs are in, the effects of Forlorne's tyranny should always feel present. Consider the ways the culture has changed since Forlorne seized power. A former festival of joyous celebration might have been transformed into one of regimented reverence—or dangerous rebellion. There might be settlements that have fallen under complete control of or been leveled by the Knights of Whelvia. Travel across the kingdom is regulated, certain kinds of speech outlawed, accusations of dissent treated with hostility, and certain types of innocent people targeted for imprisonment or death simply for existing. Ensure that the source of all major conflict in Whelvia directly or indirectly traces back to Forlorne.

### Showcase Forlorne's Power

Make it clear to the PCs that Forlorne's power is extraordinary and unmatched. The effects of her mere presence should leave NPCs with terrifying stories and horrific nightmares, experiences they should share with the PCs. Any direct sightings or encounters the PCs have with the weredragon should highlight her brutality and unwavering evil—for example, Forlorne flying over a town and dropping the head of a Whelvian rebel into the center square as a reminder of what happens to those who disagree with her, or devouring a member of her own forces for failing an order. Above all, she should be ruthless, violent, and unyielding.

Before players reach the final battle with Forlorne at the end of the campaign, any violent confrontations the PCs have with her don't use her stat block. Instead, she laughs in the face of attacks and disappears from the scene at the first opportunity, leaving behind utter destruction.

![[reign-of-the-weredragon-p135-1914.png|500]]

### Reveal the Cracks in Her Regime

Though Forlorne herself is very powerful, that doesn't mean everyone in her regime is loyal, competent, or evil. As you're building out your campaign, plant opportunities for the PCs to exploit these cracks to get a leg up on their foes. For example, the Knights of Whelvia might have a member who's willing to share information for a price or can be persuaded to turn on the weredragon under the right circumstances.

### The Weredragon Queen

The following sections provide an overview of Forlorne Lykona. For her stat blocks, see pages 142 and 143.

#### THE FIRST ACT OF EVIL

There are many tales of the origin of Forlorne Lykona. Most speak of terrible treatment at the hand of her mother, **Dolorez** (she/her). Some include the deep affection she had for her twin sister, **Harrowyrr** (she/her). But all speak of *Trigleer's Incantations*—a grimoire of dark magic that was used in Forlorne's first act of evil.

What exactly happened in her childhood cottage the night of that act is still debated, but the story goes that Forlorne used the grimoire to summon a monstrous shadow beast that would steal her mother away to the Valley of Death forever. Dolorez, a great sorcerer in her own right, set her own house ablaze during the attack in an attempt to keep the shadow creature at bay with the firelight. Forlorne's power was greater than her mother's, even at her young age, but while she murmured dark spells that ensured Dolorez succumbed to the darkness, she did not hear her sister's cries for help in the flame. Harrowyrr was trapped by the fire, burned alive. The charred remains of this cottage have sat abandoned since that fateful night, a haunted place now known as Witch's End.

#### BUILDING FORLORNE COLLABORATIVELY

During your session zero of a Reign of the Weredragon campaign, you will build Forlorne Lykona together with your players. Print out the Reign of the Weredragon Villain Builder sheet (available in the appendix or at www.daggerheart.com/ downloads).

Before character creation, cut out the prompts, fold them up, randomly select about fifteen prompts (it's okay to use more or fewer prompts), and place them on the table within reach of everyone participating. Then go around the table, starting with the player to your left, and have each player grab a random prompt, read it aloud, and answer the question. Other players may follow up with additional questions based on their response, but it's up to that player if they want to answer them. When there are no more follow-up questions, the next player reads a prompt and answers it.

If the player chooses a question they would rather not answer, or would prefer somebody else answer instead, they can pass it to that player or discard it from play. When there are no more prompts on the table, continue to character creation.

The questions from the Villain Builder sheet are included on the next page for reference and have numbers next to them in case your table would rather roll dice than physically cut out each option.

### The Knights of Whelvia

There are typically around sixteen Knights of Whelvia and, under the queen's rule, each leads a militia that presides over a sector of land to enforce Forlorne's will. In the past they protected Whelvia from outside threats, stopped violent crime, and even built infrastructure and collaborated with citizens to bolster the well-being of all. Turning their swords against unarmed Whelvians and peaceful foreigners violates the very ideals of the order itself. Now they find themselves torn between duty to the realm and duty to their sovereign.

Knights of Whelvia often justify their allegiance to Forlorne by making false claims about her intentions and blaming others for inciting her acts of terror, but at times they quietly lament that the order is a pale shadow of its former self, unequipped to deal with a threat as great as the weredragon.

**Lady Lavender** (she/her), a retired knight, is a noteworthy exception who cleaves to her oath even though she's too old to challenge Forlorne openly. Whether the Knights of Whelvia as a whole can be redeemed is a question the PCs should ask throughout the campaign, with opportunities to form new opinions as they meet more members of this troubled order.

### Schon

**Schon** (pronounced "shawn"; they/them) is a firbolg and highly skilled sky seer who has served under Forlorne since they were both members of the same stargazing cult. Schon was loyal to Forlorne and even aided her in her plot to absorb Sazanthe. But Schon's loyalty turned to hatred the moment Forlorne's ritual destroyed the abbey and killed many of the guests. Only then did Schon realize the depths of her selfishness and cruelty.

Nevertheless, Schon remained in Forlorne's service as her most trusted advisor.

Schon knows Forlorne Lykona better than anyone and is the only person she trusts. But she doesn't know the truth—that Schon spends every waking moment plotting her downfall. Armed with secrets, Schon can guide the heroes to Heaven's Eye, the hidden observatory where a celestial being called the Astromancer awaits them (see "The Astromancer" in the next section).

If the PCs are stuck or unsure about how to proceed, have Schon relay an anonymous message to them via **Astrid**, their seagull familiar. Schon can suggest a course of action to guide the characters and story in a new direction.

### The Ascendants

The Ascendants are a collection of Faint Divinities who were once mortals but ascended upon their death, becoming celestial constellations above Whelvia. The writings of the Elder Druids declared that the most powerful, revered, and worshipped creatures are worthy of ascension. While mythical beings such as dragons, and in particular the dragon wardens, often fit these qualifications, anyone deemed sufficiently worthy of apotheosis can become an Ascendant.

#### BELIEF IN THE STARS

While belief in the constellations' ability to impart guidance from the Ascendants has diminished over the many centuries since Whelvarax's reign, citizens across the nation still incorporate the night sky into their lives. Most Whelvians engage with the stars on a purely cultural level: celebrating holidays dedicated to past Ascendants or incorporating celestial motifs into their clothing and home, but not connecting on a spiritual level. Others follow the various religious practices that Whelvians have established around Ascendants or visit local sky seers for inspiration or solace during important life events. Only the most devout—many of them professional or amateur sky seers themselves—believe in the stars' ability to impart destiny or foresee the future.

Work with your players to craft the various rituals and customs that Whelvians practice in relation to the Ascendants and the stars. Which aspects are only for the devoutly religious? Which customs are open to all?

#### THE ASTROMANCER

There exists a reality in which Forlorne Lykona becomes an Ascendant. The **Astromancer** (she/her) is an immortal, celestial creature from that reality—more precisely, the Astromancer is Forlorne in an enlightened state of being.

In a possible future—after she accomplishes her goal of ascension to become a Faint Divinity—Forlorne's enlightenment opens her eyes to the extent of the horrors she committed in the Mortal Realm to gain power. In an effort to counteract the tragedies she caused, the Astromancer found a way to return to Whelvia in the past and orchestrate her own doom. If Forlorne dies, the Astromancer ceases to exist.

Because they are one and the same, Forlorne is unable to perceive the Astromancer in any way, shape, or form.

Moreover, the Astromancer is dedicated to remaining nonviolent in her immortal state and will not directly harm anyone.

The Astromancer appears as a luminous, masked being who dwells in a magical observatory called Heaven's Eye, where she waits for the PCs. When the heroes arrive, the Astromancer performs a reading of the night sky, drawing the party's attention to prominent constellations in an effort to arm them with information they can use to orchestrate Forlorne's downfall (see more about this in the upcoming "Campaign Mechanics" section).

#### KEY LOCATIONS

Some of the key locations for a Reign of the Weredragon campaign are detailed below.

**Froghaven Bog** is the swamp that holds what's left of Forlorne's stargazing cult. It's rumored that the inside of their headquarters, the Dragonmyrth mansion, has become its own pocket dimension, a warped place of endless hallways, upside-down rooms, and violent cult members. *What saying or tenet is the cult best known for? Who from the cult has spoken out against Forlorne*?

**Heaven's Eye** is a magically hidden observatory. Inside dwells a mysterious celestial being called the Astromancer (see "The Astromancer" on page 137). *What unique architectural feature does Heaven's Eye have? In what way does the Astromancer's presence affect this place*?

**Knightstead** is the ancestral home of a retired human Knight of Whelvia named Lady Lavender, who lives there with her two pet hounds. Although she's too old to swing a sword, Lady Lavender aims to redeem the Knights of Whelvia by helping all who oppose Forlorne. *How is the legacy of Lady Lavender memorialized prominently in Whelvia? What are the knights who follow Forlorne doing to stamp out that legacy*?

**Nwarlyth** is the towering fortress of Forlorne Lykona. It houses the magical tapestry that sustains the wards protecting Whelvia's borders, as well as the statues of enemies Forlorne has magically turned to stone. *What prominent figure has recently disappeared while visiting Nwarlyth? Who or what stands in defense of the fortress*?

**Vexstone Abbey** is a clifftop ruin haunted by the spirits of those killed by the ritual in which Forlorne absorbed Sazanthe. These spirits are trapped inside mundane objects and can animate them. Rough-hewn tunnels connect the abbey's vaults and cellars to sea caves where local pirates conduct business with Forlorne's underlings. *How do the ghosts here react to people passing through? What wards or charms do the pirates carry to protect them from the supernatural forces they often encounter*?

**Witch's End** is a gloomy grove that contains the charred remnants of a cottage that was once Forlorne Lykona's childhood home. Forlorne's eleven-year-old twin sister, Harrowyrr, haunts the grove as an animate skeleton wrapped in an ashen shroud. *What does Harrowyrr want from those who pass through the grove? How has the legacy of Forlorne affected the people who still live here*?

### Border Wards

Forlorne Lykona uses magical wards to discourage foreign invaders and prevent terrified subjects from fleeing the realm. Burning the magical tapestry housed in Forlorne's fortress, Nwarlyth, dispels these wards.

#### LAND WARD

This ward activates whenever a creature or vehicle tries to cross Whelvia's border by an overland route without Forlorne's permission. When the ward is activated, stony vines erupt from the ground, grab the creature or vehicle, drag it down into the earth, and entomb it.

#### OCEAN WARD

Whelvia's ocean ward destroys any boat that isn't flying either the new Whelvian flag or the flag of the Crimson Trident, a consortium that facilitates trade between coastal nations, by using watery tentacles to grab the boat and drag it down into the depths of the sea.

## THE INCITING INCIDENT

*Use the prompt below to start your campaign, or create your own.*

While staying at the **Red Leaf Inn**, a small establishment within the Whelvewood, the party's breakfast is interrupted by an insistent seagull that squawks in their faces before disappearing and reappearing in a cloud of glittering blue smoke only to squawk again.

The seagull eventually drops a scroll of parchment. Unrolling it reveals a map with a route drawn from the inn through the Whelvewood to a mysterious location labeled "Heaven's Eye." Troublingly, the Whelvewood has long been the site of skirmishes between Forlorne's forces and rebels who oppose the new queen. Many of the Knights of Whelvia have sent their militias to the Whelvewood in an attempt to take the forest and establish a large encampment. Meanwhile, rebel forces and locals are trying to drive out the tyrant's soldiers and protect the people and creatures living among the trees. Any stranger passing through is likely to be targeted by both the militias and freedom fighters, as no one knows whom to trust and neither side can afford to put faith in the wrong people.

On the backside of the map is the drawing of a rabbit's silhouette and a note that reads:

*Find the place where the trees cross*

*Show this to the Foes of the Fox for safe passage through the warren*

*There will be a price*

After delivering the scroll, the seagull steals part of someone's breakfast, squawks loudly, and disappears in its usual smoky fashion.

What dangers will the party encounter on the way to Heaven's Eye? Do they trust the seagull and whoever sent the map, or are they wary of the mysterious delivery? Will they attempt to collaborate with the militias or the rebels to traverse the Whelvewood without running afoul of the other group? What will they discover in "the warren," and what price are they prepared to pay for safe passage?

##### FOR THE GM

> The seagull was sent by Schon, Forlorne's right hand who's secretly orchestrating her downfall. Schon, a highly competent sky seer, received a message from the stars to send the party to Heaven's Eye. Though they don't know the significance of the location, they do know how to get there and that the party will play a prominent role in the fall of Forlorne Lykona. Schon's seagull can reappear to help the party if they are lost during their journey.

## CAMPAIGN MECHANICS

*The following mechanics are unique to this campaign.*

### Curses

A creature afflicted with a magical curse gains a special condition (see "Conditions" on page 102 of the core rulebook). The following two features inflict curses that are common in this campaign, but you can also create your own:

- Forlorne's "Breath Weapon," which turns people into weredrakes, known as the *Weredrake Curse*.
- Forlorne's "Stone-Cold Gaze," which turns creatures and their equipment to stone, known as the *Petrified Curse*.

Any *Cursed* condition can be cleared only by magic from a spell, ritual, magic item, location, supernatural occurrence, higher power, or some combination of these options. Two examples of curse-ending items—Lady Lavender's Longsword and the Zoetrope of the Bright Bacchanal—are described in the upcoming "Keys to Victory" section.

Discovering how to end a curse often requires research, which might prompt the PCs to undergo a quest for additional information or help from an NPC.

### The Astromancer's Revelations

From the top of the Heaven's Eye observatory, the Astromancer gazes into the night sky. The reading she performs for the PCs unveils five revelations. These are determined by the players collectively rolling 5d6. Match the results on each die to the corresponding d6 row in the Night Sky Reading table. Then, assign one die to each revelation column. You can have only one die in each column, but if the players roll multiples of a number, there might be more than one in a row. For example, if the players roll two 3s, you might choose to put one in the Key to Victory 2 column and one in the Showdown Location column. The result would be "Lady Lavender's Longsword" and "Nwarlyth" respectively. Be sure to spread out where the PCs have to go when making your selections. For example, avoid having more than one revelation occur in Elevarne or Nightshade.

***Tip:*** *You can always replace any table entry with different information after your session zero, especially if you want to use an interesting location, NPC, treasure, or piece of lore created by the PCs*.

**First Revelation (Secret's Location):** Use the Secret's Location column of the Night Sky Reading table to determine where the PCs must go to learn a secret that could help them defeat Forlorne. Give each player a piece of paper, then ask them to concoct one secret Forlorne is hiding, either about her history or current rule, and write it down without telling the other players what it is. Gather up these papers and, between sessions, choose one of the ideas from the PCs to inspire the secret they'll uncover, and use that secret to prepare the adventure they'll go through to find it.

**Second Revelation (Powerful Ally):** Use the Powerful Ally column of the Night Sky Reading table to identify one ally who can help the PCs defeat Forlorne. This ally has a pool of 3 Hope at the start of any scene that includes Forlorne as an adversary. A PC within Far range of the ally can spend a Hope from this pool as if it were their own. If the ally falls unconscious or dies, this feature can't be used.

**Third Revelation (Key to Victory 1):** Use the Key to Victory 1 column of the Night Sky Reading table to identify an item that provides a defensive benefit that can help one or more PCs in a battle against Forlorne. The item's location is also disclosed. When Forlorne appears, a PC holding at least one of these items gains 1d4 Hope. An item marked with an asterisk is described in the "Keys to Victory" section.

**Fourth Revelation (Key to Victory 2):** Use the Key to Victory 2 column of the Night Sky Reading table to identify a weapon that can cause harm to Forlorne. A PC wielding this weapon gains a +1 bonus to their Proficiency for attacks against Forlorne. An item marked with an asterisk is described in the "Keys to Victory" section.

**Fifth Revelation (Showdown Location):** Use the Showdown Location column of the Night Sky Reading table to identify where the PCs are most likely to face Forlorne in their final battle. If the PCs arrive at this location with knowledge of the secret from the first revelation, the ally from the second revelation, and the items from the third and fourth revelations, Forlorne appears alone and tries to destroy them. Two of Forlorne's passive features—"Feed on [[Fear]]" and "Terrifying"—aren't available while she's in this location.

#### Weredrake

A weredrake is what a PC or an NPC becomes after being transformed by Forlorne's "Breath Weapon."

Elements of the weredrake's former ancestry might be discernible in the creature's form; for example, a clank transformed into a weredrake might retain a mechanical appearance, while a faerie weredrake might have a carapace instead of scales. These creatures may have other unique aspects of their appearance that you can create with your players or craft yourself.

A weredrake can assume their original ancestry's form. When they do, they retain the terrifying claws and teeth of their new draconic form. They typically use this form as a ruse to lure prey close before attacking.
