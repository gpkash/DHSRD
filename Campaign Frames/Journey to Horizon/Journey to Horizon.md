# JOURNEY TO HORIZON

*After a thousand-year cataclysm, a fellowship of heroes embarks from their magically protected sanctuary to journey across a broken continent and resurrect the fallen goddess who can heal their world.*

Designed by James Haeck, Rob Hebert, James Introcaso, and Spenser Starke

**Complexity Rating: ••••**

## THE PITCH

*Read this section to your players to introduce them to the campaign.*

Long ago, the vast continent of Valloren was home to wondrous landscapes shaped by the goddess Aurelia, wielder of the elemental essence called terrana. But when her disciples' betrayal threatened to extinguish magic forever, Aurelia sacrificed her own life to call down the stars and purge the land of corruption. To protect her people from annihilation, Aurelia raised magical barriers that would last a thousand years.

In a Journey to Horizon campaign, you play heroes chosen by fate to leave their recently unsealed sanctuary and travel across a turbulent and hostile world on a quest to resurrect the goddess.

## OVERVIEW

*If your group decides to play this campaign, give your players the following information before character creation.*

The land of **Valloren** is a vast continent first raised out of an endless ocean by the goddess **Aurelia** (she/her). Wielding the magical essence **terrana**, Aurelia created incredible magical landscapes: supernaturally lush forests teeming with oversized game, crystal mountains floating high above fields of singing flowers, and enchanted plains of perpetually falling snow—all in impossible proximity to one another.

Amid this tremendous bounty, the people of Valloren flourished and multiplied, building shrines across the continent to honor their goddess. To guide and protect her creation, Aurelia selected thirteen followers to become her magi and granted each of them a fragment of her immortal power. Under their watchful eyes and steady hands, Valloren enjoyed an **Age of Wonder**. But it wouldn't last.

Among Aurelia's magi were **the Lovers,** Yrska and Drago. Alone, each of them surpassed their fellow magi in skill and power—but *together*, they rivaled Aurelia herself. In pursuit of the people's praise and their own prideful ambitions, the Lovers drew upon ever-increasing amounts of terrana to fuel the swift expansion of incredible, sprawling settlements.

But while Valloren's reserves of terrana were endlessly renewable, they were not inexhaustible. Whereas Aurelia had never drawn more from the land than it could safely replenish, the Lovers' reckless consumption threatened to drain the world of magic forever. When Aurelia counseled—then commanded—restraint, the Lovers rebelled. In response, Aurelia raised an army of devotees and appointed her eleven remaining magi its generals. At first, a quick victory seemed assured. But one by one, the Lovers lured the other magi into their rebellion with promises of unlimited power.

As the war dragged on, the land's magic was nearly depleted. If the last mote of terrana were consumed, magic would vanish from Valloren, never to return. Exhausted by decades of conflict, Aurelia's forces were driven back to the eastern edge of the continent, where—with no hope of victory—they made their last stand. There, in a final desperate gambit, Aurelia performed a fatal ritual that raised magical barriers around her shrines and pulled the stars from the sky, destroying all thirteen traitorous magi and their followers. With her last breath, Aurelia promised the faithful that, in time, the barriers would fall and she would return.

The **Starfall** scoured the face of Valloren in a maelstrom of cosmic energy, with only a fraction of Valloren's population surviving the cataclysm behind the barriers raised by Aurelia. For a thousand years, these survivors persevered within their sanctuaries, each enclave completely sealed off from the outside world—and one another—as the rest of the continent was razed. Vallorens named this period of isolation and calamity the **Long Sorrow**.

In time, the storm relented and the land began to heal. Slowly but surely, the quantum of terrana that had been saved by Aurelia's dire sacrifice replenished itself—before surging wholly unrestrained. When the sanctuaries finally opened, their inhabitants emerged into the wilds of a New Valloren. Whole regions had been transformed—first devastated by the

Starfall, then cleansed in the Long Sorrow, and finally mutated by the resurgence of terrana. From the cratered graves of fallen stars, a toxic radiance called **shadowblight** had seeped into the environment, turning nearby habitats into malicious wastelands stalked by ravening abominations.

Now, the maps and legends of Old Valloren no longer match reality—and in the short time since the sanctuaries opened, the people of Valloren have set about reforging old alliances and cutting new trails. But the outside world is hostile and chaotic, with the shadowblight spreading and raw terrana amplifying the untamed wilderness. For Valloren to thrive once more, Aurelia must return as promised. Only she has the power to lift the stars skyward, drive back the smoldering creep of shadowblight, and give birth to a second Age of Wonder.

But where is she?

At the shrine in the center of the **Marrogate** sanctuary, at the southwestern tip of Valloren, **High Priestess Saraya** (she/her) has made a stunning discovery: a **Soul Echo**. Through her holy order's sacred rituals, Saraya has learned that, to resurrect Aurelia, the lingering fragments of Aurelia's divine essence must be collected from throughout Valloren and transported to the site of her death, where body and spirit can be reunited. In secret, Saraya crafts a **Vessel of Light** to protect the Echoes and makes preparations for her long journey across Valloren.

Unfortunately, there are those who wish to prevent Aurelia's resurrection. Faith in Aurelia faltered during the Long Sorrow's thousand-year span—and the **Cult of the Two-Headed Coin**, a religious faction devoted to the Lovers, grew to fill the void. Though most members of Saraya's **Aurelian Order** remain devoted to the goddess, their ranks have been secretly infiltrated by a feared company of cultists known as the **Fellsworn Templars**. Meanwhile, wraithlike **Seekers-in-Shadow**—wretched shades that wield terrana and shadowblight alike in powerful displays of dark magic—have been spotted throughout Valloren.

On the day Saraya plans to embark from Marrogate, the Fellsworn Templars attack. In desperation, she entrusts the Vessel to your care and, knowing it means her own death, draws the templars' attention to enable your escape.

Now you must collect the Soul Echoes and resurrect Aurelia before darkness and despair overtake New Valloren. Whether you've prepared for this quest or found yourself entangled in it unexpectedly, you know the journey will be long and treacherous. The Cult of the Two-Headed Coin is on the rise, the Fellsworn are on your trail, and the Seekers-in-Shadow stalk the path ahead of you. You'll find no safety in the wilderness and must rely on sanctuaries transformed by one thousand years of isolation to rest and recover.

But you must complete your quest, the Vessel must be protected, and the goddess must be revived—for only her magic can heal this land and save it from destruction.

## COMMUNITIES

*In a Journey to Horizon campaign, all communities aside from wanderborne are available, but some have unique aspects. As needed, provide the following information to your players and choose one or more of the questions to ask them during your session zero.*

### All Communities

In this campaign, all PCs are from the sanctuary of Marrogate, but that doesn't mean they all come from the same community.

After the players choose communities for their characters, discuss as a group how one thousand years of isolation inside a sanctuary shaped their characters' communities and establish the necessary truths to support the choices made at character creation. For instance, while long sea voyages would be impossible during the Long Sorrow, the Marrogate sanctuary could border a bay or float atop the ocean waves, making a seaborne PC playable.

Bear in mind that no sanctuary is a monoculture. Each society still has various social classes, vocations, religious enclaves, and other groups and cultures within its walls. Some sanctuaries are large enough to encompass more than a single biome. If this is the case for your campaign's version of Marrogate, think about how people living in or between each of these environments might interact.

### Habitat-Based Communities

When a player chooses a habitat-based community for their character, such as ridgeborne, duneborne, or seaborne, that habitat should inform what kind of sanctuary Marrogate is. The magic of terrana allows for biomes to be as realistic or fantastical as you want. For example, if different members of the party choose all three of the communities previously listed, Marrogate could encompass a region with a mountain range separating a small sea and a sprawling desert. Alternatively, it could be a desert that encircles a large body of water with a single mountain jutting from its center.

### Loreborne

From the outset of the Long Sorrow, many saw the need to preserve not just life but knowledge, and they formed communities dedicated to assembling and maintaining vast libraries of information from Old Valloren that would otherwise be lost.

- What knowledge from Old Valloren did your people think most worthy of preservation? How did they keep it safe?
- What do you most hope to learn about New Valloren on your journey across the continent?
- What mystery about Old Valloren do you hope to solve by exploring New Valloren?
- What was the story or piece of information from the old world that you found hidden away in an ancient book— and that you believe nobody else knows about?

### Reborne

As the sanctuaries opened, so did a new chapter for Valloren and its inhabitants. Anyone who felt trapped within their original community gained the opportunity to leave it behind and seek a sense of belonging on the path to parts unknown.

- How or why did you split from your original community?
- How has the opportunity to leave the sanctuary affected your perspective on your community? Is this the first time you've rejected it?
- What person or practice are you sad to have lost when you left your community? If you don't remember your life before, what face do you keep seeing as you fall asleep?

### Wanderborne

Because of the isolated nature of the sanctuaries, the wanderborne community is not available in a Journey to Horizon campaign.

### Warborne

The isolation and deprivation that characterized life inside many sanctuaries sometimes pushed the people there into all-out conflict with one another. In some sanctuaries, internal clashes lasted generations and eclipsed any hope of peaceful coexistence. If a player chooses to play a warborne character, it means Marrogate is one such sanctuary. Think about how the other PCs, regardless of their community, would have been affected by war.

- What political crisis within Marrogate triggered the first strike?
- What resources do the people of Marrogate still fight over?
- What factions arose against one another within Marrogate's walls?

## ANCESTRIES

*All ancestries are available, but some have unique aspects in a Journey to Horizon campaign. As needed, provide the following information to your players.*

### Long- and Short-Lived Ancestries

Many ancestries, such as drakona and dwarf, have natural lifespans of 250–350 years, meaning that a full-grown adult might be only a handful of generations removed from the Starfall. For these people, the Age of Wonder is less a legendary lost era and more a near-historical period about which they have family lore. On the other hand, some ancestries (such as faerie) have a relatively short lifespan. Sanctuary life is essentially all they, or any ancestor they feel a strong connection to, have ever known. Think about how the natural lifespan of your character's ancestry might shape their relationship to Old Valloren, and how they would react to the perils and promise of New Valloren.

### Clank

Since many raw materials would be scarce in certain sanctuaries, a clank's body composition is often a representation of what resources were plentiful where they were built. Because clanks often live as long as they can source new parts, a clank might have experienced much of the Long Sorrow—or even some of the Age of Wonder. Because their memories fade over time, however, it's unlikely that many clanks remain who fully remember life outside their sanctuary.

### Elves

An elf's mystic form can shift as they travel between regions where terrana flows freely. If you decide to play an elf who possesses a mystic form, consider how its appearance changes as you encounter the strange biomes and natural wonders of New Valloren.

### Fungril

During the Long Sorrow, the mycelial arrays of fungril networks couldn't penetrate the sanctuary barriers. Now that the walls have come down, this network is slowly rebuilding, and fungril across the land are connecting with each other for the first time. This new connectivity is very exciting to some fungril, while others are wary of the risks it might bear. If you choose to play a fungril, consider how the slow regrowth of this psychic network might affect your journey, and what your role might be in its ongoing development.

## CLASSES

*All classes and subclasses aside from the Syndicate rogue are available, but some have unique aspects in a Journey to Horizon campaign. As needed, provide the following information to your players.*

### Magic Users in Valloren

In Valloren, all magic relies upon the mystical essence called terrana. No matter the source of their power—be it faith, education, or talent—a magic user requires terrana to fuel their spells. It's up to your table how terrana manifests: it could be a glowing mist that rises out of the land, iridescent electricity, or simply an invisible sense of calm that permeates the atmosphere.

For the first few centuries of the Long Sorrow, the near-total depletion of terrana rendered magic nigh-impossible to cast, but the recent exponential resurgence of terrana has given birth to a magical renaissance. In New Valloren, terrana is raw and wild. Whenever magic is used, the environment responds. Plants might bend toward the caster or burst from the soil at their feet, visible lines of crackling energy might crawl up from the ground and into their body, or rain clouds might churn and swirl overhead. The more powerful the spell, the more dramatic the effect—and the more potential for surprising or chaotic results.

This is especially noticeable for druids, rangers, witches, and elemental origin sorcerers, who all have a more intimate connection to nature magic through their class features or access to the Sage domain.

### Druids

A druid's intimate connection to the natural world can help them quickly intuit deeper truths about the strange flora and fauna they encounter on their journeys through Valloren, and many druids are excited to explore a world made miraculous by wild magic. Players who create a druid should think about how their abilities might be shaped by the strange and chaotic environments they travel through. For instance, their Beastform may manifest differently depending on the habitat they're in, or their proximity to the shadowblight might affect them in peculiar and dangerous ways.

### Guardians & Warriors

Aurelia's barriers made full-scale war between sanctuaries impossible, but many still suffered from prolonged civil conflicts. Some sanctuaries kept standing peacekeeping forces or militias for the sake of order and stability, with variable success. And now that the walls have fallen, many sanctuaries have spun up military forces to ensure their people's safety—or the conquest of their neighbors. If a player chooses to play a guardian or warrior, it's likely their character has a connection to one of these forces.

## CLASSES (CONTINUED)

### Rangers

Throughout the Long Sorrow, rangers hunted, tracked, and patrolled across relatively limited areas. Most have little experience beyond one or two biomes, and many creatures they encounter beyond the walls of their sanctuary will be strange to them. They do, however, have better-than-average survival skills honed in their home territories, as well as a knack for adapting to whatever they find on their journey. Within their sanctuaries, rangers often took on more of a conservationist role, rather than a hunter or trailblazer role, because land—and the game it could produce—was so limited. Once the sanctuaries were unsealed, however, many of the first inhabitants to explore the surrounding areas and map their new terrain were rangers.

### Seraphs

Before the Long Sorrow, nearly all seraphs put their faith in Aurelia—but a dead goddess doesn't answer prayers. If a player chooses a seraph at character creation, discuss with your table what this could mean: Have they discovered other deities? If so, how does that alter their beliefs about Aurelia's place in the pantheon? If they still worship Aurelia, what does it mean that their prayers are now being answered? How does the knowledge that they are in the presence of a fragment of Aurelia's essence affect their faith?

### Syndicate Rogue

Because of the isolated nature of the sanctuaries, the Syndicate rogue is not available in a Journey to Horizon campaign.

### Warlocks

Warlocks wield power bestowed upon them by entities from other realms, but in Valloren these magic abilities must still be fueled by terrana. Thus, like other spellcasters, warlocks have seen their power restored as terrana has returned over the last 1,000 years. If you choose to play a warlock, think about how your patron came to interact with this realm and what their feelings toward Aurelia or terrana would be.

## PLAYER PRINCIPLES

*If your group decides to play this campaign, give your players the following information before character creation.*

### Explore the Land and Discover Its People

While a trek through the lands of New Valloren is filled with danger and hardship, it's also a wellspring of discovery and wonder. No path is properly mapped, and impossible vistas lie just beyond the next horizon: crystal ruins untouched for a thousand years, a river that glows with a cerulean light, green lightning striking a shrine atop a floating mountain. These curiosities exist to be investigated and explored.

Characters who take risks and seek to uncover the unknown are often rewarded with priceless information or relics from an ancient age. Journey smart, but don't be afraid to go off the beaten path when something draws your interest. When your character reaches a new sanctuary, learn about its denizens and their way of life. When you find a mystery, solve it. If you see an injustice, rectify it.

### Grow Together

While traveling with your fellowship, ask honest questions and give honest answers. Steal moments from the main journey for quiet bits of conversation and comfort. Aid your allies and help your friends. Long journeys through new places teach us about ourselves and one another. Exploring a new land can make you more courageous, compassionate, and open-minded than before. The experiences your character has and the people they meet should change them for the better, one step at a time.

### Keep Moving Forward

While it's important to take time away from the main storyline for more episodic or emotional beats, don't forget the ultimate goal: to resurrect the goddess Aurelia. The forces of darkness are aligned against your party and grow in power with every step of the journey. Remember the weight of the mission and press on accordingly.

## GM PRINCIPLES

*Keep the following guidance in mind when you GM this campaign.*

### Reward Curiosity

Don't penalize the PCs for exploring the wider world of New Valloren. They must face challenges on their journey, but not punishment. When a PC delves into a dangerous ruin or pursues a tantalizing sidequest, reward them with information, treasure, or something else they desire. The PCs should never end an adventure empty-handed.

### Make the World Wondrous

Terrana not only fills Valloren with lush, diverse biomes—it also makes those environments fantastical. Your New Valloren can include a range of extraordinary landscapes, such as clouds you can walk on, a jungle of neon flora, or a lake of living glass. This land is also dotted with the remnants of the old world, like submersibles of silver now beached on the shore and inhabited by giant, human-faced crabs. Create environments that the PCs will be eager to explore and let your imagination run wild.

### Put Other People Out There

New Valloren is vast and unexplored, and the PCs aren't the only people with purpose. Many others are leaving their sanctuaries to establish small villages in fantastic places, build roadhouses for weary travelers, and go on pilgrimages to holy sites. These folks allow you to give the PCs information about nearby landmarks and environments, news from the sanctuaries, and a way to rest and recuperate away from the road. They also allow the PCs to chat with other people aside from each other and share stories of their adventures.

Many agents and factions want the Vessel for themselves. Hound the party with dark forces that threaten to discover their goal. Waylay them with difficult dilemmas. Tempt them with easy outs, then confront them with the consequences.

### Show the Scars of the Long Sorrow

Though terrana has returned to Valloren with a vengeance, the continent still carries the trauma of the Starfall and its aftermath. The party should come across regions that terrana's resurgence has not reached or that have been corrupted by shadowblight. Confront them with places where fallen stars exude toxic magic: ruined quarries where time and space are warped, sprawling marshes teeming with undead, and abandoned fortifications where twisted beasts that know only hunger dwell.

Additionally, not everything went according to Aurelia's plan. Some things—somehow—survived the Starfall. There are sanctuaries whose walls came down early, some that are still trapped within their barrier, and others still that were never fully protected to begin with. Some sanctuaries, left to stew in their own juices, fell to internal crises or conflicts. And a rare few are festering sources of shadowblight, filled with people transformed into something unnatural and wrong.

### Think in Themes

This campaign features disconnected locations, huge swathes of wilderness, a large number of random tables, and numerous mechanical abstractions; you can unite these disparate elements by linking them to special themes that you select while preparing a session, improvise at the table, or notice as they naturally recur throughout the campaign. Think about how a great episode or arc of a television series often juggles multiple storylines that each touch on related ideas or questions: "found family," "loss of innocence," "what is the nature of evil?" If you're having trouble finding a throughline, you can't go wrong with "the power of love." Try to weave these themes into the people, places, and events you present to the party, and the story you tell together will cohere into something beautiful and surprising.

## DISTINCTIONS

*Use this information to prepare your campaign. You can also share it with your players as needed.*

### An Uncharted World of Wonder

There are no accurate maps of New Valloren as it exists today. When terrana returned, it didn't restore the world exactly as it was; instead, citrus groves grew where deserts once sprawled, icy snowfields covered former grasslands, and inland seas became canyon labyrinths.

The map that comes with this campaign frame is of Old Valloren. The only data the party can rely on is the locations of sanctuaries—though some sanctuaries might have fallen by the time the party arrives at them. Outside the sanctuaries, New Valloren is wild and dangerous. When the party travels from one sanctuary to another, they'll invariably encounter:

- Overgrown environments bursting with terrana
- Magical wonders that defy explanation
- The ruins of Old Valloren, many of them filled with ancient relics or haunted by baleful specters
- Pockets of shadowblight where fallen stars bleed gray
- Lairs and encampments inhabited by shadowblighted survivors
- New settlements built by former sanctuary-dwellers— temporary encampments at first, but eventually permanent fortifications and villages as the party's journey wears on
- Travelers, peddlers, and explorers
- Ancient routes and fresh-cut pathways
- Hazards both magical and mundane
- Agents of the enemy (Cultists of the Two-Headed Coin, Fellsworn Templars, and Seekers-in-Shadow)
- Bandits, brigands, and outlaws

#### IMPOSSIBLE ENVIRONMENTS

The terrana-charged habitats and biomes of Valloren defy explanation and border each other in impossible ways: rivers flow uphill, tropical jungles give way to frozen tundras, and patches of earth only a few leagues wide somehow possess their own cycle of day and night. When designing a new area for the PCs to explore or traverse, consider the following questions:

- What is the natural environment this area is based on, and how can I make it magical?
- What unique dangers can the PCs face here?
- What benefits or boons might the party find?
- What unique landmarks or curiosities could the PCs encounter?

This campaign frame includes special mechanics for creating, exploring, and mapping New Valloren (see the "Journeys in Valloren" section on page 169).

### Shrines & Sanctuaries

Throughout Valloren are shrines dedicated to the goddess Aurelia. In Old Valloren, these were fonts of terrana maintained by her priests and devotees. In New Valloren, they're the only points of true safety in a dangerous world. Resting and recuperating in the wilderness is difficult, so reaching a sanctuary—and therefore a shrine—will usually be a moment of relief for the party.

When Aurelia performed her final rite, she caused a ring of colossal trees to erupt from the ground surrounding each of her shrines. These trees grew until they touched the clouds, then twisted and curved in on themselves, forming a tightly woven, impenetrable canopy. On the interior face of each barrier, the trees bore magical fruit and flower blossoms that shone with sunlight and cried tears of rainwater, staving off drought and famine throughout the Long Sorrow. When creating a sanctuary, think about how it might have developed over a millennium of isolation—what changed or transformed? What unique customs or cultures emerged? What stayed the same or was preserved?

Here are a few sample sanctuaries to inspire your own creations:

- **Cyntix** is a city built atop a seemingly inexhaustible vein of iron ore. Its people have become some of the greatest blacksmiths and tinkerers of their time. Among other incredible steam-powered technologies is a working rail system that transports people via mine cart. The Machinist Guild operates as the de facto political body, though technically there are still noble titles inherited from Old Valloren. The current Guildmaster is Esio Bonte (he/ him), a galapa with a level head and an analytical mind.
- **Polwood**, known as the Sleeping Sanctuary, is named for its practice of using a specially brewed tea to place the entire population, save a single caretaker, into a trance-like slumber that could stretch resources for decades. The current caretaker, Maia Brody (they/them), is a timid orc who has, for some reason, refrained from awakening their people even though the sanctuary has been unsealed.
- **Harthaven** is encased in the colossal ribcage of a long-dead beast, and its people draw warmth from the life force that lingers within the bones. They are healers, philosophers, and bonewrights who carve relics of protection. The priestly caste that rules over Harthaven forbids discussion of the creature's name or origin. Its youngest member, a gnome named Kael Orin (he/him), has begun hearing a heartbeat echoing through the marrow halls.
- **Emberhold** is situated inside the caldera of a dormant volcano, where its forges never truly cooled. Its people channel geothermal heat into radiant energy that powers entire neighborhoods. The current Speaker of the Cindermarch, a katari named Liora Dane (she/her), fears that the resurgence of terrana has reawakened the mountain's lust for fire.

For special mechanics and additional guidance related to creating sanctuaries, see the "Dice Tables for Creating Sanctuaries" section on page 174.

Not all shrines are identical. Generally, the larger a settlement was, the larger their shrine—and the larger the perimeter of the sanctuary that rose up around it. A small village might have a simple stone shrine about the size of a well, whereas a city may be home to a multi-level temple.

The party can take a long rest only at a sanctuary (see the upcoming "Campaign Mechanics" section for more information). The priests who maintain the shrine within a sanctuary are oathbound to care for respectful travelers and give them access to facilities for hygiene and laundry, clean food and water, and a safe place to recover from wounds or illness.

#### SOUL ECHOES

The goddess is dead, but her spirit reverberates throughout Valloren. Now, these vibrations have coalesced into Soul Echoes that can be found within ten shrines. To complete their quest, the party must locate all the Soul Echoes, gather them into a specially crafted Vessel of Light, and carry them to the site of Aurelia's holy remains. At the start of the Inciting Incident, the party will have acquired their first Soul Echo, the one placed into the Vessel of Light by High Priestess Saraya. To locate the remaining nine, they must use the Vessel itself: the Soul Echoes inside call to their sisters, and the song they sing together lights the party's path.

For more about placing these Echoes on the map, see the "Placing Soul Echoes" section on page 169.

#### THE VESSEL OF LIGHT

The Vessel of Light was created by High Priestess Saraya to securely gather and transport Aurelia's Soul Echoes. The nature of the Vessel is intentionally ambiguous so you and your table can decide what it is. Below are three options:

- The Vessel is a seemingly mundane object, such as a carved urn or a large acorn that must be planted in her grave.
- The Vessel is an obviously magical item of power, such as a glowing orb that must be hidden under thick velvet to avoid suspicion.
- The Vessel is an infant or young child named Echo who must be cared for by the party.

***Tip:*** *Some players might be uncomfortable with the idea of a child being put in danger of any sort but still want the Echo to have a personality. If so, you can make Echo a non-humanoid creature, such as a dragon hatchling or a spirit that speaks in the voice of the goddess*.

Regardless of the Vessel's form, the Soul Echoes within it are sustained by terrana from the earth and as such must remain close to the ground lest they fade and perish. As a result, the party can't significantly shorten their journey by flying across Valloren.

It's up to you and your table how much the average person knows about Aurelia's prophecy. Discuss who might have heard her last words. They might have been a psychic projection heard by all, or only her most faithful followers. They might have been written in stone on her shrines, or merely an article of faith preached by the hierophants of her holy order. Whatever your table decides, think about how the form and reach of the prophecy might affect the party's quest.

#### CLIFFS OF HORIZON

The site of Aurelia's final ritual is the northeasternmost tip of Valloren, the Cliffs of Horizon. This is the final destination of the party's journey, but they should go there only when they've collected all ten Soul Echoes to reunite them with the goddess's remains. Doing so will resurrect Aurelia and allow her to restore Valloren to its former glory.

As with the Vessel, the nature and form of Aurelia's remains have been left up to your table. She might be a 100-foot-tall statue, a twisting tree, or a perfectly preserved corpse protected by her own sanctuary walls. When the party reaches the Cliffs of Horizon with all ten Echoes, use the following questions to guide the scene: What's the current state of the site and Aurelia's remains? What must the party do to complete the reunion? In what form is Aurelia resurrected from her long death? When the goddess's soul is reunited with her body, how does the world around her change?

### The Factions of New Valloren

There are two main religious orders in New Valloren: the Aurelian Order and the Cult of the Two-Headed Coin. The Aurelian Order is still dedicated to the goddess for whom it's named. In Old Valloren, it was the dominant religion, respected and celebrated in virtually every settlement. During the Long Sorrow, faith in the goddess—and thus the order's ranks—diminished as prayers to Aurelia went unanswered and numerous generations passed without her return. As the influence of the Aurelian Order waned, the power of the Cult of the Two-Headed Coin rose in equal measure.

Before the Long Sorrow, devotees of the Lovers secretly carried coins with Yrska's head on one side and Drago's on the other. Showing one's two-headed coin to another devotee proved your membership in this secret cabal. During the Long Sorrow, some came to believe that the world would have been better served by the Lovers' victory. The ranks of the cult swelled until, in many places, there was no longer need for secrecy. Many cultists now practice in the open, and sanctuaries fly banners bearing the image of the Two-Headed Coin. There are even cultists who have infiltrated the Aurelian Order: the Fellsworn Templars. Once the cult's preeminent warriors and spies, many Fellsworn Templars went into hiding during the Long Sorrow. Down the centuries, they secretly bequeathed their mantles from mentor to apprentice and embedded themselves into the Aurelian Order hierarchy, where they could prevent Aurelia's resurrection—or use the vehicle of her rebirth to bring back the Lovers.

![[journey-to-horizon-p167-2076.png|500]]

### Shadowblight

Shadowblight is the toxic radiance that oozes from the craters caused by the Starfall. This destructive energy leaches into the earth and drains both life and color from the land. While most travelers avoid shadowblighted regions, an adventurer might have to cross them to shorten a journey, evade pursuit, or locate something important.

At these corrupted sites, acid storms, life-draining fog banks, and clouds of impenetrable darkness can manifest at any time. Spells cast here can drain the caster's life force or trigger dangerous and unpredictable side effects. The air is hot and thick with a malice that makes it difficult to breathe. Even just being near a patch of shadowblight is dangerous, as twisted beasts and relentless undead often emerge from these chaotic wastelands in search of lives to consume.

Areas infected with shadowblight are drained of color and eventually dissolve into gray sludge. Any creature, living or dead, that stays in close proximity to a shadowblighted environment for long enough becomes corrupted in body, mind, and soul, turning grayscale and exuding a gloomy aura that desaturates their surroundings. Shadowblighted creatures are ravenous, undead things that greedily consume life and terrana alike.

### The Seekers-in-Shadow

For one thousand years, the Seekers stalked the wastelands of Valloren. Though the party is unaware, these shades are the baleful spirits of the eleven fallen magi, starved of terrana and furious at the failure of their rebellion. Now, they finally have a chance to bring back their overlords—or take power for themselves.

In their natural form, the Seekers are tattered, vaguely humanoid shrouds of shadow-stuff with stretched and contorted proportions. Much as in life, they can draw terrana from the land to power their magic spells. Unlike in life, their doing so leaves a wound in the environment that fills with shadowblight.

After absorbing terrana, a Seeker's appearance takes on aspects of the environment they devastated. For instance, after consuming terrana in a forest, their body might grow gray leaves and become covered in bark. A Seeker who devastates a snowy field might gain crystalline skin or ice-white patches of fur. This absorbed terrana doesn't just change their appearance, it also grants them access to new abilities that reflect these altered states. When a Seeker is defeated, their body regenerates some time later—though a party could endeavor to find a special weapon or ritual that can destroy the Seeker for good.

### Impending Doom

The party's journey has a ticking clock. The shadowblight spreads, the Seekers sniff them out, the Cult of the Two-Headed Coin expands its reach, the Fellsworn Templars close the distance, and sanctuaries fall to the dangers and discord of New Valloren. As the party presses on, the threats they face rise to meet them.

This campaign has special mechanics to handle the escalation of disasters and cataclysms that befall New Valloren (see the "Doom Track" section on page 177).

## CAMPAIGN MECHANICS

*The following mechanics are unique to this campaign.*

### The Hex Crawl

For travel across the continent of New Valloren, Journey to Horizon uses a map divided into hexagons (commonly known as a **hex map**). Your table can use the map to track the party's movement across the continent, moving one hex at a time in a style of travel called a **hex crawl**. Each hex on the map represents roughly 24 miles of land from one side to another—just remember that in Daggerheart, narrative always supersedes representation with maps and minis, so the area represented by some hexes may be a bit larger or smaller in your story.

The following sections provide more specific directions on how to run this hex crawl, but generally the players will travel between sanctuaries one hex at a time, resolving encounters along the way. A party can take up to three short rests while traveling through the wilderness but is restricted from taking long rests outside a sanctuary. Therefore, the PCs must plan ahead to ensure they don't exhaust their resources.

#### FLOW OF THE NARRATIVE

The journey procedure mechanizes certain aspects of overland travel. In some ways, the hex crawl rules are more regimented than other aspects of Daggerheart—but they're designed to support the narrative, not abstract it away. We intend this system to help your table tell the kind of travel adventure stories the fantasy genre excels at, where the heroes cover long distances and pass extended periods of time in only a handful of scenes, and most mundane or routine events take place off-camera.

When using the hex crawl rules, consider the pace and flow of this kind of story. Often, there are travel montages broken up by dramatic scenes where we zoom in and watch the story unfold in detail. As you become comfortable with this style of play, you'll develop your own intuition for how much to prepare in advance and how to cooperatively build the rest of the travel sequence, empowering your players to become fellow authors of the world their characters inhabit. For instance, as the PCs plan their route, you can guide the conversation by asking one or two of the following questions to your players, or create your own:

- What rumors have your characters heard from other travelers about the land ahead?
- What sound did your character hear in the night that concerns them about the upcoming journey?
- What surprising detail did your character notice that makes them excited to venture forth?
- Who does your character know that might be found nearby?
- What tales or legends of Old Valloren has your character heard about this region?

It's important that the following procedures feel connected to the fiction and driven by character action, so don't treat them like a mere bookkeeping exercise. As with everything else in Daggerheart, use them to present difficult dilemmas and fuel interesting scenes where the players' decisions define their characters' internal journeys just as much as the external— and always amend or ignore what you feel you must in service of the story.

#### JOURNEYS IN VALLOREN

New Valloren is a vast continent distinguished by a patchwork of wild environs punctuated by scattered sanctuaries. The party's mission is to locate the nine shrines containing Aurelia's remaining Soul Echoes, transfer each of them to the Vessel of Light, and then deliver the Vessel to the Cliffs of Horizon where it can be used to resurrect the goddess.

A Journey to Horizon hex crawl uses two copies of the same campaign map: a GM's key map and a player-facing map. The GM fills out the key map before the campaign with important information about each hex—such as habitat, terrain, and potential points of interest or encounters. Meanwhile, the player-facing version of the map only indicates the locations of sanctuaries and the geography of Valloren as it existed before the Long Sorrow. As the party journeys across the continent, the players track their movement on the player-facing map, and the GM describes the contents of each hex they enter, using their key map as a reference. The players should fill out the player-facing map as they go until it eventually matches the GM's prepared key map. The campaign map is available as a two-page map in the appendix or as a larger map at www.daggerheart.com/downloads.

#### Preparing the GM's Key Map

There are many ways to fill out the key. You can choose the contents of each hex, procedurally generate them, or use a combination of both. The following procedure is one method of doing so; feel free to use whatever method speaks to you.

***Tip:*** *You don't have to fill out the entire key map before your first session—in fact, it's often better to limit your map generation to the hexes the party is likely to reach in the next session or two. This has a couple of benefits. First, it greatly reduces the amount of time you'll have to spend prepping your first session. Second, you can adjust the contents of the hexes or your generation method as the campaign continues to better serve the story or dial in the difficulty*.

#### PLACING SOUL ECHOES

Before the first session, choose nine sanctuaries in which a Soul Echo can be located. The sanctuaries should be far enough from one another that the PCs will have to travel all over Valloren to reach each one, but not so far apart that their locations become predictable—for example, all along the coastlines or in every third sanctuary in a line. You can randomly assign them, but if you do, use the randomness as inspiration rather than edict. In particular, ensure the Echoes are distributed fairly evenly from west to east so that the PCs get to collect Echoes early in the campaign but end up relatively close to Horizon when they acquire the final one.

#### FILLING WILDERNESS HEXES

Start in any hex. You fill out the key map one region at a time by doing the following.

- Roll a **d20** to determine the region's **habitat** on the Habitat table.
- Roll a **d12** to determine the region's **size** (how many contiguous hexes the region covers). The shape of the region can be whatever you want, as long as you don't encircle empty hexes.
- Roll and add the results of a **d8** and **d6** to determine one type of **encounter** the party might face while traveling through the region, as shown on the Encounter table.
- Roll a **d4** to determine the **terrain** of the region on the Terrain table. This determines how many days it takes the party to travel into the hex (see the "Travel Days" section on page 176).
- Roll a **d100** to determine a **rumor** on the Rumors table that the party might hear about this region. You decide how true the rumor is and whether the PCs learn it.

### Tracking the Party's Location

The party tracks their movement with the player-facing map. This procedure assumes the PCs are capable wayfinders and trailblazers, so they know the contents of the hex they are in (their current hex) and any hex adjacent to it. If an adjacent hex contains a non-hidden point of interest, such as a tower, the PCs are usually aware of it as well. Altogether, this gives the players enough information to decide which way to proceed. As always, the GM decides how much information they convey to the players.

### Travel Days

This system assumes that, on a full day traveling, the PCs…

1. Break camp, eat breakfast, and pack up shortly before sunrise.

2. Travel overland for about 6–8 hours.

3. Take a short break at midday to eat and get their bearings.

4. Travel overland for another 6–8 hours until about sundown.

5. Make camp, prepare and eat dinner, set a watch, and bed down for the night.

As needed, various party members scout ahead, forage or hunt, refill waterskins, repair gear, and keep watch. If you like, you can narrate or even roleplay these activities if your table finds it interesting, but you don't need to spend valuable table time describing or tracking these events.

### Resting

This campaign frame doesn't require you to meticulously track rations or other supplies. Instead, travel moves at the pace of the story, and the party's resources are tracked via rests. Outside a sanctuary, the party can take only short rests, each of which represents an entire day spent in one location. Inside a sanctuary, the party can take a long rest, which represents multiple days recovering from hard travel and rough sleeping.

Keep in mind that the term "rest" has a specific mechanical meaning in Daggerheart. Within the fiction, the party still rests (in the colloquial sense) most nights they spend in the wilderness, but they don't gain the *mechanical* benefits of a short rest every time they go to sleep. There are very few creature comforts in the wilderness, and this means that travel wears the PCs down until they take a day off. This extended period allows for downtime moves.

The PCs can gain the benefits of a long rest only in a sanctuary because that is the only place they can feel safe, fully recuperate, and replenish their stock of any resources not plentiful in the wilderness. This essentially expands the standard Daggerheart adventuring rhythm—where a short rest represents an hour or so and a long rest represents a full night's sleep—to this campaign frame's scale of days, weeks, and months.

When the party takes three short rests in a row, their next rest must be a long rest (see page 105 of the core rulebook).

Narratively, this limitation on short rests represents the party's inability to fully replenish their resources in the wilderness; the heroes can take a day off of traveling to repair their gear and rest their weary bodies, but there's only so many times one can stitch armor and field dress wounds before they need a few days inside civilization.

#### SPECIAL DOWNTIME MOVE: LOCATE SOUL ECHOES

In a Journey to Horizon campaign, the players gain access to an additional downtime move: Locate Soul Echoes. When a PC uses this downtime move, they commune with the Vessel and have the Echoes within "call out" to their sisters. The PC learns the direction (north, south, east, west, or a combination) of the nearest sanctuary with a Soul Echo at its shrine. At the GM's discretion, using this downtime move alerts nearby Seekers-in-Shadow of the Vessel's general direction as well.

### Encounter Rolls

When the party enters a hex, roll a number of d6s equal to the hex's terrain rating. If any of the dice show a result of 1, activate an encounter you designed during region creation. Otherwise, you can spend a [[Fear]] to activate an encounter.

***Tip:*** *You can change the size of the dice you roll to reflect more dangerous (d4) or safer (d8) areas*.

The party can trigger more than one encounter in a single region. If this happens, either roll on the Encounter table to generate a new encounter in the moment or shift an encounter from a region that the party seems unlikely to pass through, then modify or reflavor it to suit the current situation.

> ***Example:*** *The party enters an 8-hex alpine region with fair terrain. The GM, Peter, rolls 2d6 and gets a 4 and a 1, triggering an encounter. Peter runs the Mountain Chasm Traversal environment (9 on the Encounter table) he prepared before the session. After the encounter, the party moves into another hex with fair terrain, and Peter rolls 2d6; he rolls a 3 and a 5, so no encounter is triggered. When the party enters the next hex, Peter rolls two 1s, triggering an encounter. Peter recognizes that the party has already moved past the badlands region with a saloon encounter (13 on the Encounter table), so he reflavors the saloon to a ski chalet to match it with the alpine habitat of the party's current region*.

#### OPTIONAL RULE: ENDURANCE COUNTDOWNS

You can heighten the tension of traveling through the wilderness with this optional rule. At the end of a rest, each PC gains an Endurance Countdown (6). When the party enters a wilderness hex, each player rolls their Hope Die. If the result is equal to or lower than the value of their PC's Endurance Countdown, they mark a [[Stress]]. Otherwise, they tick down their Endurance Countdown. When a PC's Endurance Countdown triggers, they become *Vulnerable* until their next rest. At the start of a rest, all PCs' Endurance Countdowns end.

#### WHEN DISASTER LOOMS

It's possible that a party can be stuck in the wilderness with no short rests and all HP and [[Stress]] marked. As with a combat encounter that results in all PCs being defeated, it's up to the GM to decide how to proceed. Here are a few possibilities:

- The party's quest ends in failure. Discuss as a table what events unfold in the world of Valloren as a result.
- The party is lost forever, but the Vessel finds its way to a new band of heroes. The players create new characters who continue from where the previous party left off. If you decide that a significant amount of time has elapsed since the original party fell, discuss as a table what might have befallen the continent in the interim.
- The party is rescued by a friendly NPC or taken hostage by the enemy, and they most likely no longer have the Vessel. Decide what happened to it, then let the PCs figure out how to reacquire it.

### Traveling over Water

Valloren is crisscrossed by a network of rivers and other waterways that can be traveled by boat. It's also surrounded by an ocean that can be traversed by ship. For simplicity's sake, traveling by water uses the same basic mechanics as overland travel, with the following changes and clarifications:

- **Rivers:** The party can travel across overland hexes if they acquire passage on a boat and the GM decides that there are navigable waterways in the hexes they wish to travel through. Moving **downstream** reduces the terrain rating of the hex the party enters by 1—so taking a boat downstream into a hex with rough terrain takes only 2 days instead of 3. Entering a hex by boat **upstream** takes an extra day.
- **Ocean:** The party can travel through ocean hexes if they acquire passage on a ship. When the party enters a new ocean hex, roll a **d4** to determine the weather they encounter:
- On a 1, the party gets a **tailwind**, and entering the hex takes a day.
- On a 2, they enjoy **fair weather**, and entering the hex takes 2 days.
- On a 3, the party encounters **rough waters**, and entering the hex takes 3 days.
- On a 4, the party encounters **extreme weather**, and entering the hex takes 4 days.

***Tip:*** *When the party travels by boat or ship, remember to describe how working the rigging, fording shallows, and performing other necessary jobs exhausts them*.

#### CHARACTER ADVANCEMENT

The party levels up each time they add a new Soul Echo to the Vessel of Light. Because there are nine Soul Echoes (in addition to the one they begin the campaign with) for the party to collect before delivering the Vessel to Horizon, the party will advance from level 1 to 10 over the course of the campaign.

#### DOOM TRACK

The Doom Track (available in the appendix or at www.daggerheart.com/downloads) represents the growing disasters that befall New Valloren if the party doesn't resurrect Aurelia. At the end of each session, mark a box in the party's tier or below. You can't mark a nested box until you mark the box it's nested under.

When you mark a box on the Doom Track, try to incorporate the connected event into the next session, but don't force it if it doesn't make sense. For instance, if you mark "A new settlement is overtaken by surging wilderness," and the party doesn't leave their current location during the next session, it probably makes more sense to wait until they embark on a journey into the wilderness. Each event should occur at least once after it's been marked, but that doesn't preclude it from happening multiple times as long as it doesn't get boring or predictable.

Where possible, you should choose an option that makes sense—for example, don't choose "Travelers trade stories of nearby Seeker sightings" if the party is deep inside a network of abandoned mines and you don't expect the PCs to interact with other travelers for a bit.

As your table's story develops, feel free to adjust how many boxes you mark at the end of a session. If the heroes discover a way to drive back the shadowblight or reignite faith in Aurelia along their journey, you can refrain from marking a box for one session to reflect the extra time the heroes have bought themselves. On the other hand, if the heroes travel too slowly or let evil expand without resistance (such as by allowing a sanctuary to fall to the Cult of the Two-Headed Coin), you can mark one or more additional boxes to reflect the growing despair taking hold in New Valloren.

***Tip:*** *The Doom Track is built for a campaign that lasts 30–40 sessions. If you want your campaign to be shorter or longer, you'll want to adjust how often it's marked*.

#### CAMPAIGN ADVERSARIES

This campaign includes two new features, "Shadowblighted" and "Terranamancer," which you can add to any adversary. The "Shadowblighted" feature empowers adversaries inside shadowblighted regions, while the "Terranamancer" feature showcases an adversary's command of terrana.

***Shadowblighted - Passive:*** This adversary is corrupted by shadowblight; describe how this manifests in their form or demeanor. When encountered within a shadowblighted region or near a powerful source of shadowblight, this adversary critically succeeds on attacks on a die roll of 18–20.

***Terranamancer - Passive:*** This adversary can draw on terrana and use it to fuel magical effects. **Mark a [[Stress]]** to activate an effect based on the habitat of their current location (if an effect's damage roll uses the adversary's tier, roll the damage die a number of times equal to their tier and total the rolls):

- **Heat Spell (Desert, Salt Flats, Volcano):** The adversary exudes oppressive supernatural heat. Each target within Very Close range must succeed on an Instinct Reaction Roll. Targets who fail must mark a [[Stress]] and take **d6** direct magic damage using this adversary's tier.
- **Overgrowth Spell (Forest, Jungle):** This adversary unleashes a burst of branches, brambles, or vines in all directions. Each target within Close range must make an Agility Reaction Roll. Targets who fail take **d8** physical damage using this adversary's tier and are *Restrained* until they break free with a successful Strength Roll.
- **Wind Spell (Cliffs, Plains, Steppe):** A tornado surrounds this adversary. Each target within Close range must make a Strength Reaction Roll. Targets who fail take **d8** magic damage using this adversary's tier and are pushed up to Far range. Until this adversary takes damage, attack rolls against them have disadvantage.
- **Ice Spell (Alpine, Glacier, Tundra):** This adversary unleashes daggers of ice. Make an attack against each PC in front of this adversary within Close range. Targets they succeed against take **d10** magic damage using this adversary's tier and are *Frostbitten* until they spend a Hope to clear the condition. While *Frostbitten*, a PC gains a -1 penalty to their Proficiency.
- **Poison Spell (Moor, Swamp, Wetlands):** A cloud of toxic spores explodes from this adversary. Each PC within Close range must succeed on a Strength Reaction Roll or take **d6** direct magic damage using this adversary's tier and become *Nauseated* until they clear a HP. While *Nauseated*, a PC can't gain Hope.
- **Stone Spell (Canyon, Mountain, Badlands):** This adversary gains a bonus to their damage thresholds equal to their tier until they take Severe damage or use this feature again.
- **Water Spell (Riverlands, Sea):** This adversary unleashes a geyser or tidal wave. Make an attack against all targets within Very Close range. Targets they succeed against are pushed to Close range of where they were and mark a number of [[Stress]] equal to this adversary's tier.
