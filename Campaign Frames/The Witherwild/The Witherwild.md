# THE WITHERWILD

> When an invading nation attacks an ancient forest deity, a virulent overgrowth spreads throughout the land.

**Designed by:** Carlos Cisco, Rowan Hall, & Spenser Starke
**Complexity:** • (1/5)

## TONE & FEEL

Adventurous · Dynamic · Epic · Heroic · Thrilling · Uncanny · Whimsical

## THEMES

Cultural Clash · Ends Justify Means · Grief · People vs. Nature · Transformation and Change · Survival

## THE PITCH

Fanewick — once a place of abundance, dangerous to outsiders but generous to those who respected it — has been invaded by the nation of Haven. In trying to force the land into eternal spring, Haven's interference triggered the Witherwild: a corrupting bloom that now threatens everyone living in Fanewick. The PCs are unlikely heroes, called from humble beginnings to confront their newfound duty to save the land's people from the corruption.

## OVERVIEW

**Fanewick** is a wild, dark, divinity-haunted region long avoided by outside forces. Faint Divinities perform small miracles for the locals — and just as readily lure travelers off the paths to ruin. Bogs swallow armies whole. The hardy people who make their homes here, called **Wicklings**, follow strict customs that keep them alive.

**Haven** was once the dominant power of the region. Its founders wanted to escape the influence of the Faint Divinities — they killed the great deity **Shun'Aush the Granite Ophid** and built their walled city among his remains. Centuries of prosperity followed. But Shun'Aush had a slow revenge: as Haven grew and its farmers and miners disturbed the buried stone of his body, ancient dust rose, and a plague — the **Serpent's Sickness** — incubated in the very walls that had brought safety.

The Serpent's Sickness consumes its victims in stages: first a dust-laden cough, then a scaled rash, then ossification of the body until organs solidify and the sufferer petrifies where they stand. Over two years, more than half of Haven's population fell to it.

**Archmage Phylax**, Haven's High Magus, identified the **crimson lady's veil** — a rare red flower that blooms once for every ten thousand white blossoms — as the cure. Crimson lady's veil grows in Fanewick during spring. To harvest enough to save Haven, Phylax led an invasion of Fanewick and **plucked out the Reaping Eye of the Great Owl Nikta, the Shepherd of the Seasons** — the Faint Divinity who had maintained Fanewick's seasonal balance. With the Reaping Eye gone, Nikta can only watch the land with her Sowing Eye, and Fanewick is locked in unending spring.

The result: the **Witherwild**. Unchecked growth tears through the region. Animals swell to enormous size; trees twist and hunt; vines strangle anything they touch. Fanewick's flora and fauna flourish into an unstoppable scourge. Haven, meanwhile, has occupied the bogs as flower-farms.

The PCs lived peaceful lives in Fanewick or lives of conquest in Haven. The crisis demands a response, even from the unprepared.

## COMMUNITIES

All [[Communities]] are available; some take on a particular shape in this campaign.

- **[[Loreborne]] and [[Highborne]]** — Knowledge is Fanewick's most valuable currency. Loreborne in Fanewick may be small specialist groups (hunters, historians, artisans) inside larger villages. In Haven, the wealthiest are highborne who inherited generations of city-built riches, though none were spared the Serpent's Sickness.
- **[[Ridgeborne]], [[Underborne]], [[Wildborne]]** — Likely Wicklings raised in Fanewick's deadly environs. Some have been forced into work as farmers in Haven's flower fields, trading labor for protection from the Witherwild.
- **[[Orderborne]]** — Current or former members of the Haven Army. Soldiers carry the grief of a homeland succumbing to disease alongside the violence they bring.
- **[[Slyborne]]** — Likely from Fanewick rebel groups working to expel Haven's forces.
- **[[Seaborne]]** — Both nations have coasts; Fanewick's seaborne are typically small local groups, Haven's are large commercial ports.
- **[[Wanderborne]]** — Found in both nations, though safe travel has become severely constrained by the war and the Witherwild.

> - *(Highborne)* Who have you lost to the Serpent's Sickness?
> - *(Wildborne)* What Faint Divinities do you give tribute to, and how have you been rewarded in kind?
> - *(Orderborne)* What regrets do you carry from your conquest of foreign soil?
> - *(Slyborne)* Who among the Haven Army do you wish to exact revenge upon, and why?
> - *(Wanderborne)* What do you do to keep your small community safe, independent, and hidden from Haven's forces?

## ANCESTRIES

All [[Ancestries]] are available. Setting-specific notes:

- **[[Clank]]** — Haven clanks are usually iron and steel; Fanewick clanks are usually wood and stone.
- **[[Fungril]]** — Forest fungril have grown noticeably larger since the Witherwild spread.
- **[[Drakona]], [[Faun]], [[Firbolg]], [[Infernís]]** — Their horns have grown faster and longer in the corrupted regions.
- **[[Galapa]] and [[Ribbet]]** — Many bog-dwelling families were displaced by the Haven invasion.
- **Havenites of any ancestry** may carry the Serpent's Sickness — stiffened movement, limited time without a cure.

## CLASSES

All [[Classes]] are available. Setting-specific notes:

- **[[Druid]], [[Ranger]], [[Sorcerer]]** — Common across Fanewick. Players should consider how the character's bond with the natural world is affected by the Witherwild.
- **[[Warrior]] and [[Wizard]]** — Prevalent in Haven. A large Haven wizarding school trains both, and the Haven Army is largely composed of warriors and School of War wizards. Consider the PC's personal relationship to the army.
- **Vengeance Guardian** — Wicklings seeking revenge on Haven (or to drive them out) often become Vengeance guardians.
- **Syndicate Rogue** — Haven sends spies into Fanewick communities. A Syndicate rogue may be connected to or hunted by these covert operatives.

## PLAYER PRINCIPLES

- **Make the invasion personal.** Haven's invasion has cost everyone something. Let the loss inform what your character wants and what they'll do to get it.
- **Treat death with importance.** Lives taken should weigh on the character — both lost loved ones and slain foes — and shape later choices.
- **Embrace vulnerability.** Show your character's humanity in quiet moments, not just their ferocity in violent ones.

## GM PRINCIPLES

- **Paint the world in contrast.** Let the nightmarish charm and the beautiful terrify. Show Fanewick's overgrown beauty alongside its devastation; show Haven's grim order alongside its art and culture; contrast the capricious Faint Divinities with the devoted mortals fighting to save their home.
- **Show them true danger.** Bodily harm isn't the worst tragedy. Use the PCs' origins to threaten their homes, families, communities, and core beliefs — give them something specific to fight for.
- **Offer alternatives to violence.** Compassion (rather than the sword) can free a corrupted beast from the Witherwild. The Haven/Fanewick conflict shouldn't only be solved with blood. Give PCs chances to mend wounds and heal the rift from within.
- **Create multidimensional allies and adversaries.** Haven citizens aren't all villains; Fanewick citizens aren't all virtuous. Hide darkness in friends; let enemies do right things, even for wrong reasons.

## DISTINCTIONS

### The Weeks of Day and Night

The day/night cycle is **weekly** — the sun rises for an entire week, then sets for one. This shapes agriculture, trade (many merchants refuse to travel by night), and Fanewick's ecology itself. Nocturnal and diurnal creatures share the same forest in alternating shifts. Plants live whole lifecycles in a week: **sun rose vines** climb the canopy across a week of day and wilt in moonlight; **night bloom** is a bioluminescent flower that smells of burnt sugar and decay, blooming only at evening to light dark patches of forest for travelers.

### The Serpent's Sickness

The plague spares no one in Haven. Quarantine has barely slowed it. Citizens carry flowers and snake sheddings as folk-remedies. Stage progression:

1. Hacking cough laden with bone-like dust.
2. Scaled rash that cracks the skin in serpentine patterns.
3. Body hardens until organs solidify and stop functioning. Final transformation can be so quick that victims become statues where they stand, leaving Haven a city of cenotaphs.

### Lady's Veil

Small flowers in patches of sun across Fanewick. White blossoms are common; one in ten thousand is the **crimson lady's veil**, the only known cure for the Serpent's Sickness. Long used in herbal teas; the crimson variant has ceremonial significance. Haven now cultivates the flowers on massive farms across the conquered bogs, but even with the endless growing season the red blooms remain rare. A sickly-sweet smell hangs over Fanewick from the harvest.

### The Witherwild Itself

The corruption empowers anything it touches. Plants gain hunger; animals grow grotesquely large and aggressive; even harmless prey-creatures become formidable. **Withered** is the term for a corrupted being.

When a person is corrupted (deliberately attacked, or accidentally exposed), their body fuses with plant or beast. The Witherwild gradually replaces personality with the same drive to consume. The Withered may keep a semblance of who they were for a time, but eventually lose personhood entirely. Some Wicklings believe Nikta gifts this transformation as wrath against Haven, to drive the invaders out and recover her Reaping Eye.

### The Gods of Fanewick

The Faint Divinities are not abstract — they walk the land as incarnate beings, sharing space with mortals. Many communities and even some families have a small tutelary god. Worship in Fanewick is reciprocal: gods curry mortal worship through small miracles and tests; mortals rely on divine blessings to survive the wilds. The most acknowledged Faint Divinity is **Nikta, the Shepherd of the Seasons**, whose lost eye created the current crisis.

The lesser Faint Divinities each have their own opinion of the Witherwild — some embrace the unchecked growth, many are wary of how unbalanced the land has become. Examples:

- **Fulg, Stacker of Stones** — favored with smooth stacked river rocks; bivalves and limpets bloom under his gaze. Largely uninformed about the crisis and prefers it that way.
- **Hyacynis, They Who Spread Small Blossoms** — friend of bees and small flowers, embracing the Witherwild as their domain swells (literally) to enormous proportions.
- **Ikla, the Sky Painter** — paints Fanewick's weekly sunrises and sunsets in scintillating colors, then is exceedingly lazy and unpleasant the rest of the week.
- **Oove, the Watcher of the Night Bloom** — never seen, but heard at night whispering directions to lost travelers (and reputedly leading those who displease him into danger). Said to live on the moon.
- **Qui'Gar, Whispered Sweetness for a Thorny End** — quiet god of those who die in thorns and briar patches; the Witherwild's growth has upended her once-quiet existence.
- **Rohkin, the Wandering Horn** — watches over horned herd creatures. Once preserved the balance against overhunting; now watches helpless as his herds twist into unrecognizable beasts.

These are a tiny slice of the hundreds (or thousands) of Faint Divinities in Fanewick.

> [!gm-prep]+ Faint Divinity prompts
> - Which Faint Divinities do the PCs acknowledge or pay tribute to?
> - How has the Witherwild changed each god's outlook?
> - Which gods ally with Haven (to preserve the endless spring), and which align with Fanewick?

## THE INCITING INCIDENT

The **Reaping Eye** sits in a vault beneath the central tower of Haven's wizarding school — apparently impossible to recover. A secretive rebel group, led by a mysterious figure called the **Fanewraith** (she/her), has a plan: find Nikta and pluck out her remaining **Sowing Eye**, ending the endless spring (and, as a side effect, all life that depends on the cycle of seasons). They're not thinking through the long-term consequences — much as Haven didn't.

**Kreil Dirn** (he/him), Haven's beleaguered spymaster, has uncovered the plot. He never openly agreed with Archmage Phylax's (they/them) decision to take the Reaping Eye, and the consequences have been grimly vindicating to him. Unable to send a Haven army deep into Fanewick, he uses his network of informants to find adventurers who can stop the Fanewraith.

The party is invited to meet Kreil. But is his information correct? Does he have his own agenda? Whether they come from Fanewick or Haven, the PCs must extend some trust to a man as dangerous and mercurial as the "enemy" he wants them to hunt. And once they find the Fanewraith — bring her to justice? Side with her? Try to recover the Reaping Eye themselves?

Kreil suggests the party start their hunt in the treetop village of **Alula**, where he suspects the Fanewraith runs her operation.

## CAMPAIGN MECHANICS

### Corruption from the Witherwild

Adversaries and environments in this campaign can take an additional type: **Withered**. When an adversary is Withered, describe how the Witherwild has changed their normal appearance or behavior. You'll need ~20 **Wither tokens** (the same physical tokens you use for [[Fear]] are fine).

**Trigger.** Any time a PC takes Severe damage from a Withered adversary or environment, gain a Wither token and place it on that PC's character sheet. The PC then rolls their Fear Die. **If the roll is equal to or below the number of Wither tokens on their sheet**, they immediately gain a [[Scar]] and clear all tokens — describe how the Witherwild has changed them permanently.

**End of session.** Clear all Wither tokens from PC sheets and **gain an equal amount of [[Fear]]**.

**Death with tokens.** If a character dies while carrying Wither tokens, their body is permanently taken over by the Witherwild.

## SESSION ZERO QUESTIONS

Pick any of these or write your own:

- What dangerous animal comes out during the week of night that isn't out during the week of day?
- What unique trait (appearance, smell, taste) does anything tainted by the Witherwild have?
- What superstitions does your character or their community hold about traversing Fanewick during the long nights?
- Your character has witnessed something beautiful that came from the Witherwild. What is it, and how has it transformed their view of the corruption?
- If your character is from Fanewick, what commodity do they secretly enjoy that comes from Haven? If they're from Haven, what delicacy from Fanewick can they not get enough of?
